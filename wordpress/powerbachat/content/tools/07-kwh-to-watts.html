Kilowatt hours measure energy and watts measure power, so to convert kWh to watts you need to know over how many hours the energy was used. The formula is watts = kWh × 1,000 ÷ hours. One kilowatt hour used over one hour is an average of 1,000 watts; the same kilowatt hour spread over a whole day is an average of about 42 watts.

kWh and watts, explained

Think of watts as speed and kilowatt hours as distance. A 100 watt lamp is the speed at which it uses electricity. Leave it on for 10 hours and it uses 100 × 10 = 1,000 watt hours, which is 1 kWh, the unit on your electricity bill.

  • kWh = watts × hours ÷ 1,000
  • watts = kWh × 1,000 ÷ hours

Worked examples

  • A fridge using 1.2 kWh a day: 1.2 × 1,000 ÷ 24 = 50 watts on average. The compressor draws more while it runs, then switches off.
  • A home using 900 kWh a month: 900 × 1,000 ÷ 730 = about 1,233 watts on average, around the clock.
  • An electric car charge of 60 kWh over 8 hours: 60 × 1,000 ÷ 8 = 7,500 watts, about what a home wall charger supplies.
  • A 3 kWh battery emptied in 6 hours: 3 × 1,000 ÷ 6 = 500 watts of load.

Average watts versus peak watts

The calculator gives the average power. Real appliances often run in bursts: an air conditioner or fridge compressor cycles on and off, and a washing machine heats water for a few minutes and then mostly turns the drum. So a fridge that averages 50 watts may draw 150 watts when the compressor runs. For sizing an inverter, generator or circuit, use the peak or rated watts on the label, and for estimating bills use kWh.

Frequently asked questions

How many watts is 1 kWh?

1 kWh is 1,000 watts for one hour, 500 watts for two hours, or about 42 watts for 24 hours. A kWh is an amount of energy, so the watts depend on the time.

How many kWh does it take to run a house?

It varies widely with climate, size and appliances. Divide your monthly bill's kWh by 730 to see your home's average power. A home using 600 kWh a month averages about 820 watts.

How do I convert kW to kWh?

Multiply kilowatts by hours. A 2 kW heater running for 3 hours uses 6 kWh.

Is kWh per hour the same as kW?

Yes. Using energy at a rate of 1 kWh every hour is the same as a steady 1 kW of power.
