This DPDC bill calculator is for households supplied by the Dhaka Power Distribution Company, which covers southern Dhaka, including Dhanmondi, Mohammadpur, Lalmatia, Motijheel, Paltan, Old Dhaka, Lalbagh, Jatrabari, Shyampur and parts of Narayanganj. Enter your monthly units above to see each slab, the demand charge, VAT and an estimated total, whether you are on a prepaid meter or a postpaid bill.

South Dhaka includes some of the oldest and most crowded neighbourhoods in the country, alongside planned residential areas. Old buildings, shared lines and dense housing make electricity costs a real concern for many families, and a clear estimate helps.

How to use the DPDC bill calculator

  1. Get your units. On a prepaid meter, check the monthly usage screen or account history. On a postpaid bill, use the units consumed line.
  2. For a mid-month estimate, divide by the days so far and multiply by 30.
  3. Enter the figure. The slip shows the estimated total and your average price per unit.
  4. Open the breakdown to see each slab line.

A tip before you start: if you are on a prepaid meter, note the meter's total kWh reading on the first of each month. The difference between two readings is the cleanest figure to use here, because it ignores how many times you recharged.

Old buildings and wiring

Many homes in Old Dhaka and older parts of the DPDC area have wiring that is decades old. Poor connections, overloaded circuits and worn insulation waste energy as heat and can be a fire risk. If your bill or balance seems high for what you use, and especially if switches or sockets feel warm, have an electrician check the wiring. It is a safety issue first and a bill issue second.

Shared meters and sub-letting

In dense neighbourhoods, it is common for several families or a family and a shop to share one meter. Their combined units quickly climb into expensive slabs, even if each household uses little. If you share a meter, splitting the bill by units is fairer than splitting it equally, and a separate meter for each household, where possible, can lower everyone's average price per unit. Ask DPDC about the process for a new connection.

A worked example from Dhanmondi

A family in Dhanmondi uses about 260 units in winter and around 480 in July. The DPDC bill calculator shows that at 480 units, the last 80 units are charged at the high rate above 400. They try 400 units and see the saving. Running the bedroom AC from midnight rather than from 9 pm, and using dry mode in the evening, brings July down to around 410. The month after, they do it from the start and stay under 400.

Prepaid tips for DPDC customers

  • Know your monthly spend. Use the calculator to estimate a month's deductions, then recharge once rather than many small top-ups.
  • Watch summer weeks. Deductions rise quickly when the AC runs; check the balance weekly.
  • Keep transaction numbers until the recharge shows on the meter.

Water pumps in multi-storey buildings

Many DPDC buildings pump water from an underground reservoir to a roof tank every day. When the pump is on a flat's own meter, a faulty float switch or a leaking tank can keep it running for hours, adding dozens of units a month without anyone noticing. Check the tank's float valve, fix leaks quickly and, if possible, put the pump on a timer. In buildings where the pump is on the common meter, the cost shows up in the service charge instead.

Staying comfortable in Dhaka's summer

South Dhaka's dense streets hold heat late into the evening. A few low-cost habits keep homes cooler without running the AC all night: open windows on opposite sides in the early morning to let cooler air through, close curtains on sun-facing windows by late morning, and use ceiling fans at a moderate speed rather than full. When you do use the AC, cool one room well and keep the door closed.

Lowering a DPDC bill

  • Stay under 400 units in summer where possible.
  • Replace old fans and tube lights with DC fans and LEDs.
  • Service the AC before summer. Dhaka's dust clogs filters quickly.
  • Check your IPS battery. Old batteries waste units on every recharge. See the battery price page before replacing.
  • Consider rooftop solar if you have roof access. See the solar system price page.

When something looks wrong

If your balance drops faster than expected, check the meter's deduction history and compare it with your usage. Photograph the meter, note the dates and contact DPDC's office or helpline with your meter number. For postpaid bills, compare the reading with your meter before the due date, and keep payment receipts in case a payment is not credited.

Small shops on a home meter

In many DPDC neighbourhoods, a ground-floor shop runs from the same meter as the family upstairs. A refrigerator, lights and a fan in the shop can add a hundred units or more a month, pushing the home into higher slabs. Strictly, commercial use should be on a commercial connection, which has its own tariff. Separating the two keeps the household's units in the cheaper slabs and makes each side's costs clear. Ask DPDC about the requirements for your load.

Frequently asked questions

Is the DPDC bill calculator valid for Narayanganj?

Yes, for DPDC household connections. All areas use the same residential tariff.

Are DPDC and DESCO rates the same?

Yes. Both charge the BERC residential tariff. See the DESCO bill calculator for north Dhaka.

Does the calculator include VAT?

Yes. It adds the demand charge and 5 percent VAT to the energy charge.

When was the tariff last checked?

We last checked the BERC residential tariff in [powerbachat_checked].

Why is my DPDC prepaid balance falling so fast?

Summer AC use is the usual reason. Check the meter's deduction history. If usage looks too high for your appliances, have the wiring and meter checked.

Can two families share a DPDC meter fairly?

Sharing pushes the combined units into higher slabs. Splitting by sub-meter readings is fairer, and separate connections are better still where possible.
