The first offer usually arrives when you are tired, behind on bills and ready for this to be over. That is exactly why it deserves a careful look. Sometimes it is fair. Often it is an opening move. Once you sign the release, though, the claim is closed for good.

We cannot tell you whether to accept, and nobody who has not seen your file should. But these questions will help you decide.

## 9 questions to ask before you accept

### 1. Have I finished treatment?

If you are still seeing doctors, you do not know the full cost yet. A settlement is final, so future care usually comes out of your pocket.

### 2. Does it cover every bill?

Add up medical bills, lost wages, prescriptions and out-of-pocket costs. If the offer barely covers them, it leaves little or nothing for pain and suffering.

### 3. What does it allow for pain and suffering?

Ask the adjuster to break the offer down. Compare it with the range from the [pain and suffering calculator](/pain-and-suffering-calculator/).

### 4. Did they assign me any fault?

A quiet 20% fault assignment cuts the offer by 20%. Ask directly, and ask why.

### 5. How does it compare with a realistic range?

Run the [car accident settlement calculator](/car-accident-settlement-calculator/). If the offer is well below your low number, that is worth questioning.

### 6. What will I actually take home?

If you have a lawyer, fees, costs and liens come out first. The [take-home calculator](/settlement-calculator-take-home/) shows the real number.

### 7. Are there liens I have to repay?

Health insurers, Medicare, Medicaid and hospitals may need to be repaid from the settlement. Know the amounts before you agree.

### 8. What exactly am I releasing?

Read the release. It may cover all claims, known and unknown, against the driver and the insurer. Make sure property damage and any claims against your own insurer are handled correctly.

### 9. How close is my filing deadline?

If your statute of limitations is near, a low offer puts you under pressure. Talking to an attorney before the deadline protects your options.

## Signs of a lowball offer

- It arrives very quickly, before you have finished treatment
- It comes with pressure: "this offer expires Friday"
- The adjuster will not explain how they got the number
- It covers only part of your bills
- It ignores lost wages or future treatment

## How to counter

1. **Say thank you and that you need time** to review it. You do not have to answer on the phone.
2. **Ask for the breakdown** in writing.
3. **Send your counteroffer in writing** with a specific number and your reasons, along with any missing records. Our [demand letter generator](/demand-letter-generator/) helps with the format.
4. **Expect a few rounds.** Move your number only when you have a reason to.

## When accepting makes sense

A fair first offer does happen, especially for small claims with clear fault and short treatment. If the offer covers your costs, falls inside a realistic range, and you are fully healed, taking it and moving on can be a reasonable choice. The goal is to decide with your eyes open.
