Quick answer: you need a website redesign when your site no longer matches your business, works poorly on mobile, is slow, is hard to update or simply stops bringing in enquiries. A small business redesign usually costs $500 to $2,500 with a freelancer. Done carefully with redirects in place, it should keep or improve your Google rankings, not lose them.

Not every tired website needs a full redesign. Sometimes a faster host, new photos and rewritten headlines are enough. Here is how I help clients decide, and how I run a redesign when one is needed.

Nine signs it is time for a redesign

  1. It looks dated next to your competitors. Visitors compare, whether you like it or not.
  2. It is awkward on a phone. Tiny text, zooming, buttons too close together.
  3. It is slow. Especially on mobile data.
  4. You cannot update it yourself without breaking something or calling a developer.
  5. Your business has changed: new services, new audience, new prices.
  6. Enquiries have dropped even though traffic is steady.
  7. Your branding changed and the website no longer matches.
  8. It runs on outdated software or a theme that is no longer supported.
  9. You feel embarrassed sending people to it. That feeling is data.

Redesign or refresh?

If you ticked one or two, a refresh might do: better photos, clearer headlines, a speed tune-up and a stronger call to action. If you ticked four or more, or the structure itself is the problem, a redesign is usually better value than patching.

What a website redesign costs

A redesign is priced much like a new site, because the design and build are new. What makes it cheaper is existing content you can reuse. What makes it more expensive is migrating lots of pages, products or blog posts. I explain typical ranges in how much does a website cost, and my own fixed prices apply to redesigns too.

My redesign process

1. Measure what you have

Before touching anything I record which pages get traffic, which searches bring people in, which pages lead to enquiries, and current speed scores. This protects what already works.

2. Talk to the business

What do customers ask? What do they misunderstand? Which services do you want more of? The redesign should be built around those answers.

3. Plan the structure

Usually this means one page per main service, a clearer homepage and a shorter path to contact. Pages that rank stay at the same address wherever possible.

4. Design and prototype

You click through the new design before it is built, on desktop and mobile.

5. Build on a staging site

Your live site stays online while the new one is built privately.

6. Redirect every changed address

This is the step most redesigns get wrong. Every old address that changes gets a 301 redirect to its new home, so visitors and Google never hit a dead end.

7. Launch and watch

After launch I check forms, speed, Google Search Console and traffic for the following weeks and fix anything that looks off.

How to protect your SEO during a redesign

  • Keep page titles and headings that already rank, and improve the rest
  • Do not delete content that brings traffic; move and improve it
  • Set up 301 redirects for every changed URL
  • Submit the new sitemap in Search Console
  • Make sure the new site is faster, not slower

Changing platform during a redesign

A redesign is the natural time to switch platform, for example from Wix to WordPress or from WooCommerce to Shopify. If you are weighing that, read Wix vs WordPress or Shopify vs WooCommerce first.
