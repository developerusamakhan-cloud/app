If your electricity bill shows two readings instead of one, you are on a time-of-use meter, and peak hours matter for your bill. Units used during peak hours in the evening cost more than units used at other times. For a household that does its heavy chores at the wrong time of day, that difference adds up every month. This guide explains how peak hours in Pakistan work, who they apply to and how to shift your load without making life harder.

Who is on a time-of-use meter

Households with a sanctioned load of 5 kW or more are usually billed on a time-of-use (TOU) meter. Many larger homes, especially those with several ACs, fall into this group. Smaller homes on a normal single-rate meter are not affected by the peak window, although the evening peak still matters for the grid.

Your bill shows whether you are on a TOU meter. Look for separate peak and off-peak readings in the meter box, and separate charges for each in the bill.

What peak hours are

Peak hours in Pakistan are the evening window when demand on the national grid is highest, because offices, shops and homes all draw power at once and solar output is falling. To discourage heavy use at that time, peak units are priced higher than off-peak units.

The peak window is a few hours long and moves with the season, generally starting earlier in winter and later in summer, when sunset is later. The exact timings in force for your connection are printed on your bill or available from your distribution company, so check them rather than relying on memory.

How peak units are billed

A TOU bill shows two sets of units: peak and off-peak. Each is charged at its own rate. Taxes and adjustments are then added as usual. So two homes using the same total units can pay different amounts, depending on how much of their use falls in the evening window.

Our electricity price per unit page explains the normal household slab rates. For a TOU bill, the key number is your peak share: peak units divided by total units. The lower it is, the lower your bill.

Simple ways to shift load out of the evening peak

  • Laundry: run the washing machine in the morning or early afternoon.
  • Ironing: do it in one session in the morning rather than nightly.
  • Water pump: fill the roof tank before the evening, ideally on a timer.
  • Geyser: heat water in the morning and switch it off before peak hours.
  • Cooling: pre-cool bedrooms in the late afternoon, then let the AC coast at 26 degrees through the evening.
  • Charging: charge an electric bike, car or UPS outside the peak window where possible.

None of these use less electricity; they just move it to cheaper hours. Combined with efficiency steps like LED lights and inverter ACs, the effect on the bill can be significant.

A worked example

A family in DHA Lahore with a 7 kW sanctioned load used 900 units last July, 260 of them in peak hours. They moved laundry, ironing and the water pump to the morning, set the geyser on a timer and pre-cooled the bedrooms before the evening. The next month they used about the same total, but their peak units fell to around 170. The bill dropped by the difference between the peak and off-peak rate on 90 units, plus the tax on it, without anyone feeling less comfortable.

Solar and peak hours

Rooftop solar produces most during the middle of the day and falls away by the evening, so on its own it does not cover the evening. It still helps: running heavy loads during sunny hours means they come from your panels rather than the grid. Checking a TOU bill

  1. Note the peak and off-peak readings on the meter display, which you can usually cycle through with a button.
  2. Compare them with the readings printed on the bill.
  3. Calculate your peak share for the month.
  4. Track it over a few months to see whether your changes are working.

If the readings on the bill do not match the meter, report it to your distribution company with a dated photo before the due date. Our guide on how to read your electricity bill shows where each figure appears.

Should you ask for a TOU meter?

If your sanctioned load is below the threshold, you are usually billed on the normal slab tariff, and a TOU meter is not something you choose. If you are above it, the TOU meter is generally required. The best response is not to fight it but to use it: with a little planning, peak hours become an opportunity to cut the bill rather than a penalty.

Why the grid needs this

Peak pricing exists for a reason. The evening peak forces the system to run its most expensive power plants. When households shift chores out of the evening window, they reduce that pressure, which helps keep load-shedding down and costs in check for everyone. It is one of the few ways a single family's habits connect directly to the health of the whole grid.

Peak hours in Pakistan and the AC

For most large homes, the AC is the biggest evening load. A little planning makes a big difference. Run the AC harder in the late afternoon, when it is still off-peak, to bring bedrooms down to a comfortable temperature, then raise the setting to 26 degrees for the evening. Keep doors and curtains closed so the cool air stays in. If you are choosing a new AC, an inverter model holds a steady temperature with a gentle draw, which keeps evening consumption lower. Frequently asked questions

What are peak hours in Pakistan?

An evening window, a few hours long, that shifts with the season. Check your bill or your distribution company for the current timings.

Who pays peak hour rates?

Mainly connections with a sanctioned load of 5 kW or more, which are billed on time-of-use meters.

Does peak pricing apply to protected consumers?

Protected consumers are small households on normal meters, so peak pricing generally does not apply to them.

Can I see my peak units on the meter?

Yes. Time-of-use meters show separate peak and off-peak readings; press the display button to cycle through the screens.

Does solar help with the evening peak?

Directly only with batteries, but running heavy loads in sunny hours lowers your grid use overall.
