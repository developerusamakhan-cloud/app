How panels are priced in Pakistan

Solar panels are sold almost entirely by the watt. A 585 watt panel at a given per watt rate costs 585 times that rate. That keeps comparisons simple, but it hides a few details that matter more than a rupee or two per watt:

  • Grade. A-grade panels come straight from the manufacturer's quality line. B-grade panels have cosmetic or minor electrical defects and sell for less. They are not always bad, but they should be clearly labelled and priced as such.
  • Technology. N-type panels (often sold as TOPCon) generally lose less output in heat and over time than older P-type mono PERC panels. In Pakistan's summers, that difference is real.
  • Bifacial or mono-facial. Bifacial panels collect some light from the back. On raised structures over a light-coloured roof they can produce a little more.
  • Warranty. Reputable brands offer a product warranty plus a performance warranty that promises a minimum output after many years. Check that the warranty is honoured locally through an authorised distributor.

Popular brands and what people buy

Most rooftop systems in Pakistan use panels from a handful of large manufacturers: Jinko Solar, Longi, Canadian Solar, JA Solar and Trina Solar, with newer names like Astronergy also common. All of them make good panels. Differences between them are usually smaller than differences between a good and a poor installation. In practice, pick a brand that your installer can get reliably, with paperwork from an authorised importer, and focus your attention on the inverter and the fitting.

Panel sizes have grown too. A few years ago 330 to 450 watt panels were normal. Today most homes use panels between 550 and 600 watts, which means fewer panels, less wiring and lower structure cost for the same system size.

How to tell if a quote is fair

Quotes vary a lot, and not always for good reasons. A few checks help:

  1. Ask for an itemised quote. Panels, inverter, structure, cables, breakers and labour should each be listed with brand and model.
  2. Compare the panel line with the table above. If it is far higher per watt, ask why. If it is far lower, ask whether the panels are B-grade.
  3. Check the inverter. It is the part most likely to fail. A known brand with local service matters more than saving a few thousand rupees.
  4. Look at the structure. A raised, galvanised steel structure with proper anchoring lasts much longer and keeps the roof usable.
  5. Ask about earthing and protection. DC breakers, surge protection and proper earthing are essential for safety.

Why panel prices move

Almost all panels are imported, so the rupee exchange rate is the biggest single driver. Global panel prices, shipping costs and any changes in import duties or taxes also matter. Local demand plays a part too: prices often firm up in spring, when everyone wants solar before summer, and soften in late autumn. If you are not in a hurry, winter is often a better time to buy, and installers are less busy.

Reading a dealer's price list

Market price lists in Lahore's Hall Road, Karachi's Saddar and similar trading hubs are usually quoted per watt, ex-warehouse, for full pallets or a minimum quantity. A homeowner buying ten panels through an installer should expect a little more per watt than the pallet price, to cover transport, handling and the installer's margin. That is normal. What is not normal is a per watt price far above the solar panel price in Pakistan today for the same brand and model, with no clear reason given.

Is now a good time to buy?

With household unit rates as high as they are, the bigger question is usually how quickly a system pays back, not whether prices might drop a little further. A modest fall in panel prices saves a small share of the total system cost, while each month without solar costs a full month of savings. For most homes using 400 units or more in summer, waiting rarely pays. The policy on selling power back to the grid has also changed, which affects payback. Frequently asked questions

What is the solar panel price in Pakistan today?

See the price table at the top of this page. It lists per watt and per panel prices for the main A-grade brands, with the date of the last update.

What is the price of a 585 watt panel?

Multiply the per watt price of your chosen brand by 585. The per panel column in the table does this for the models listed.

Are cheap B-grade panels worth it?

Sometimes, if they are honestly labelled, priced well below A-grade and come with some warranty. For most homes the small saving is not worth the uncertainty.

Which brand is best?

All the major brands make reliable panels. Choose one your installer can supply with proper paperwork, then put your care into the inverter and installation quality.
