If you are thinking about rooftop solar, the question of net billing vs net metering in Pakistan matters almost as much as the price of panels. Both describe how your distribution company treats the electricity your panels send back to the grid. The difference between them changes how quickly a system pays back, and it should change how big a system you buy. This guide explains both in plain language.

Policy in this area has been changing. We describe how each approach works and what it means for you. Before you sign anything, check the current rules and buyback rate with your distribution company or the regulator's latest notification. We last reviewed this page in [powerbachat_checked].

How net metering works

Under net metering, a bi-directional meter records both the units you import from the grid and the units your panels export. At the end of the billing period, exported units are set off against imported units. If you imported 600 units and exported 400, you were billed mainly for the difference. Any surplus above your imports was paid for at a buyback rate.

The result was simple and generous: in effect, every exported unit cancelled out an imported unit, which you would otherwise have paid for at your full slab rate plus taxes and fuel adjustment. That is why so many Pakistani households installed large systems in recent years. Exporting at midday was almost as good as using the power yourself.

How net billing works

Net billing treats the two directions separately. Units you import are billed at the normal retail tariff, with all the slabs, taxes and adjustments that apply. Units you export are credited at a separate buyback rate. The credit is then deducted from your bill.

The key point is that the buyback rate for exports is set well below the retail price you pay for imports. Under the changes announced for new solar connections, the buyback rate was cut sharply from earlier levels. So an exported unit is now worth much less than an imported unit costs. In plain terms, you save a lot by using your own solar power, and earn much less by selling it.

The difference in one example

Imagine a home that imports 500 units in a month, mostly in the evening, and exports 400 units at midday.

  • Under net metering, the 400 exported units were largely set off against the 500 imported, so the home was billed for something close to 100 units, at low slab rates.
  • Under net billing, the home is billed for all 500 imported units at the normal slab rates, then receives a credit for 400 units at the lower buyback rate.

The bill under net billing is clearly higher. The solar system still saves money, but much less of that saving comes from exports. To see the bill for 500 units on today's tariff, use our electricity bill calculator.

Net billing vs net metering at a glance

  • Imports: partly cancelled by exports under net metering; always billed at the full tariff under net billing.
  • Exports: worth about as much as an imported unit under net metering; credited at a lower buyback rate under net billing.
  • Best system size: larger was usually better under net metering; sized to daytime use under net billing.
  • Batteries: rarely worth it for savings under net metering; more attractive under net billing for evening use.

Who is affected

As a general principle, changes to net metering in Pakistan have been aimed at new applicants, with existing agreements running until they expire. If you already have a net metering agreement, check its start and end dates and what happens on renewal. If you are applying now, assume the newer rules apply, and confirm with your company before installing.

What this means for system size

The biggest lesson from the shift is to size solar for your own use, not for export. Under net metering, a bigger system was almost always better, because spare units still cancelled your imports. Under net billing, every extra kilowatt that mostly exports earns less and takes longer to pay back.

  • Look at your daytime load. ACs, water pumps, washing machines and home offices during sunny hours are where solar delivers the most value.
  • Use the calculator honestly. Our solar calculator deliberately ignores export income, so its payback figure suits net billing.
  • Consider a battery for evening use. Storing midday power for the evening avoids buying it back at full price. Size it with the battery backup calculator.
  • Leave room to grow. An inverter with spare capacity lets you add panels later if your use grows.

Is solar still worth it under net billing?

For most homes that use a good share of their solar power during the day, yes. Every unit you use from your own panels avoids a unit bought at your full slab rate, with GST and the fuel adjustment on top. At today's tariff, that is still a strong saving, especially for homes in the higher slabs. See the per unit electricity price page for how steep those slabs are.

Where net billing hurts most is large systems on homes that are empty during the day, because most of their output is exported at the low buyback rate. For those homes, a smaller system with a battery may be better value than a large on-grid system.

Practical steps before you install

  1. Pull the twelve-month history from your bill and note your summer and winter units.
  2. Estimate how much of your use happens between about 9 am and 4 pm.
  3. Size the system mainly for that daytime use.
  4. Ask installers to show payback both with and without export credit.
  5. Confirm the current buyback rate and application process with your distribution company.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference in net billing vs net metering?

Net metering sets exported units against imported units. Net billing bills all imports at the retail rate and credits exports at a lower buyback rate.

Will existing net metering users be moved to net billing?

Changes have generally targeted new applicants, with existing agreements honoured until expiry. Check the dates and renewal terms in your own agreement.

Should I still apply for a bi-directional meter?

If your system will export a meaningful amount, a credit at the buyback rate is better than nothing. If almost all your solar is used at home, the meter matters less.

Does net billing affect off-grid or hybrid systems?

Off-grid systems do not export, so no. Hybrid systems that store power in batteries rely less on exports, which makes them relatively more attractive under net billing.
