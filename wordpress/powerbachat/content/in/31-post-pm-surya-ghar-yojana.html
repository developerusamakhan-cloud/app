PM Surya Ghar Yojana, officially the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, is the central government's rooftop solar scheme for homes. It was launched in 2024 with the aim of putting solar panels on one crore rooftops. If you have heard that it gives "300 units free", or that it pays most of the cost of a solar system, this guide explains what is true, who qualifies and how the process works, in plain language.

What the scheme offers

The heart of PM Surya Ghar Yojana is a subsidy, called central financial assistance, for rooftop solar on residential homes:

  • ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW.
  • ₹18,000 for the third kW.
  • A maximum of ₹78,000 for systems of 3 kW or more.

The scheme has also been linked to concessional bank loans for small residential systems, so households can spread their share of the cost. For many families, the monthly loan payment ends up close to the monthly bill saving.

What "300 free units" really means

The scheme is often described as giving up to 300 units of free electricity a month. That is not a free allowance from your discom. It is the idea that a rooftop system of about 3 kW produces roughly 300 to 350 units a month, so for many homes the electricity comes from their own panels instead of the grid. The subsidy makes those panels affordable. If your system is smaller, or your use is higher, you will still buy some units from the grid.

Who can apply

  • A household with a residential electricity connection.
  • A roof suitable for panels, owned by you or with the owner's consent.
  • No previous central subsidy for rooftop solar on the same connection.

Housing societies and resident welfare associations can get support for systems that power common areas, under separate terms.

The DCR panel rule

To qualify for the subsidy, your system must use solar panels with domestic content, known as DCR panels. These use cells and modules made in India and cost more per watt than imported or non-DCR panels. Vendors sometimes quote cheaper non-DCR systems; those do not qualify. Always ask for the panel model and its DCR certificate. Our solar panel price in India page shows the price difference.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Register on the national PM Surya Ghar portal with your state, discom, consumer number and mobile number.
  2. Log in and apply for rooftop solar.
  3. Wait for your discom's feasibility approval.
  4. Choose a vendor registered on the portal for your discom and get the system installed.
  5. Submit the installation details and apply for a net meter.
  6. The discom installs the net meter and inspects the system. A commissioning certificate is generated on the portal.
  7. Submit your bank details. The subsidy is then credited to your account.

You pay the vendor first. The subsidy arrives afterwards, which is why bank loans linked to the scheme can help.

Which size should you choose?

For most homes, 3 kW is the natural choice. It captures the full ₹78,000 and produces enough for a home using around 300 to 350 units a month. Smaller homes may be well served by 1 or 2 kW. Larger homes can go bigger, but the subsidy stays at ₹78,000, so the extra capacity is paid in full. Use our PM Surya Ghar calculator to size a system from your own bill.

How much will you save?

Savings depend on your state's tariff and how much of your solar power you use yourself. In states with steep upper slabs, every unit of solar replaces an expensive grid unit, and payback can be only a few years. In states where small users already pay very little, the payback is slower. Net metering lets you export surplus units during the day and use grid power at night, with your bill based on the difference under your state's rules.

Common questions and misunderstandings

  • "The government installs it for free." No. You choose a vendor and pay for the system; the subsidy is credited later.
  • "Any panel will do." No. Only DCR panels qualify.
  • "Bigger systems get more subsidy." Only up to 3 kW.
  • "It works during power cuts." A standard grid-connected system shuts down during outages for safety. You need a hybrid system with batteries for backup.

After installation: keeping the benefit

Once the system is commissioned and the subsidy is paid, the panels need very little. Clean them regularly in dusty months, check the inverter display or app for errors, and keep an eye on monthly generation. A sudden drop usually means dirt, new shade or a fault. Your discom bill will show both imported and exported units; compare them each month to make sure net metering is being applied correctly. If anything looks wrong, contact your vendor first and then the discom.

PM Surya Ghar and your state's bill rules

The subsidy is central, but your bill is still set by your state. In Tamil Nadu, solar can keep a home under the 500 unit line in a cycle, which multiplies the saving; see the TNEB bill calculator. In Maharashtra, solar removes units from expensive upper slabs, so payback is fast; see the Mahadiscom bill calculator. In Delhi, homes already under 200 units benefit less, because the state subsidy covers their bill. Look at your own state's tariff before deciding how big to go.

Is it worth it?

For most households with a suitable roof and a bill above a few hundred rupees a month, yes. The subsidy covers a large share of a small system, the panels last for decades, and the saving grows if grid tariffs rise. Frequently asked questions

How much subsidy does PM Surya Ghar Yojana give?

₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, up to ₹78,000.

Is PM Surya Ghar Yojana available in every state?

The central scheme is national. Applications are made through the national portal by choosing your state and discom. Some states add their own support.

How long does the subsidy take to arrive?

It depends mainly on how quickly your discom installs the net meter and inspects the system. After commissioning and bank details are submitted, it is usually paid within weeks.

Can I apply if I rent my home?

You need the owner's consent and a usable roof. Many tenants discuss it with the owner, since solar raises the property's value.
