Who supplies your area

Knowing your distributor helps when you check a bill or need to complain. In broad terms:

  • DESCO serves the northern part of Dhaka, including Mirpur, Uttara, Gulshan, Banani, Badda and nearby areas.
  • DPDC serves southern Dhaka, including Dhanmondi, Motijheel, Old Dhaka and parts of Narayanganj.
  • BREB, through the Palli Bidyut Samities, serves most rural areas of the country.
  • NESCO serves urban areas in the Rajshahi and Rangpur divisions.
  • WZPDCO serves urban areas in the Khulna and Barishal divisions.
  • BPDB distributes in several other urban areas, including parts of Chattogram, Sylhet and Mymensingh.

The name and logo on your bill or meter card tell you which one is yours. Whichever it is, household tariff rates are the same.

The residential tariff at a glance

Households that use 50 units or less in a month pay the lowest lifeline rate on every unit. Above that, units are charged in slabs that rise with use, with a sharp jump above 400 units. A demand charge based on your sanctioned load and 5 percent VAT are added.

Most homes in Dhaka now use prepaid meters. The tariff is the same, but you recharge in advance through mobile financial services, banks or vending points, and the meter deducts as you use. How to read your bill

Whether postpaid or prepaid, every account shows the units used, the energy charge, demand charge and VAT. Our guide on how to read an electricity bill in Bangladesh walks through each line, so you can check yours in a couple of minutes.

Small habits that make a difference

  • Keep an eye on the 400 unit line. Units above it cost far more per unit.
  • Run the AC at 25 or 26 degrees with a fan. In Dhaka's humidity, dry mode on mild evenings saves a lot.
  • Replace old fans with energy-efficient DC fans. Fans run most of the year, so the saving adds up quickly.
  • Check the water pump. In multi-storey homes, pumps that run too long are a hidden cost.
  • Use LED lights everywhere, including on the IPS circuit, to stretch backup time.

Why the same units cost more in summer

An electricity bill in Bangladesh grows faster than usage in the hot months, for a simple reason: as units rise, more of them land in the expensive slabs. A home that uses 250 units in winter and 450 in summer does not just pay twice as much. Part of its summer usage falls above 400 units, where the rate jumps sharply. Keeping summer use under that line, even by a few dozen units, often saves more than any gadget.

Gas, cooking and electricity

In many Dhaka homes, cooking still runs on piped gas or cylinders, so electricity bills mostly reflect cooling, lighting, fans and water pumping. When gas pressure drops, some families switch to electric cookers, rice cookers and induction stoves, which can add a surprising number of units. An induction cooktop at around 2,000 watts used for an hour a day adds about 60 units a month. If gas supply is uncertain where you live, factor that into your monthly estimate.

Checking a bill or prepaid deduction

Postpaid customers should compare the units on each bill with their own meter reading. Prepaid customers should look at the deduction history in their meter or account, which shows what was taken for energy, demand charge and VAT. If anything looks wrong, contact your distributor's office or helpline with a photo of the meter and your account number. Problems are much easier to fix in the same month than months later.

A weekly look at the meter is enough to see a heavy month coming while there is still time to act.
