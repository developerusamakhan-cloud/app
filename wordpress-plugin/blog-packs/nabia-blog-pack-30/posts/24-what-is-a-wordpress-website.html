Quick answer: a WordPress website is a site built with WordPress, free software that lets you create and edit pages, posts and products from a simple dashboard. You add a domain (your address), hosting (where it lives), a theme (how it looks) and plugins (extra features). It is flexible and affordable, but needs regular updates.

Clients often tell me "I think my site is WordPress?" and are not sure what that means for them. Here is a jargon-free explanation, so you can make better decisions about your website.

The five parts of a WordPress website

1. Domain

Your web address, such as yourbusiness.com. Registered yearly with a domain company, ideally in your own name.

2. Hosting

A computer, or part of one, that stores your site and shows it to visitors. Good hosting makes your site fast and secure.

3. WordPress itself

The system you log into to edit text, add images, write blog posts and manage pages. No coding needed for day-to-day edits.

4. A theme

Controls the design: layout, colours, fonts. Can be a ready-made theme or one designed just for you.

5. Plugins

Add features such as contact forms, online stores (WooCommerce), bookings, SEO tools and backups.

WordPress.org vs WordPress.com

WordPress.org is the free software you install on your own hosting, with complete control. WordPress.com is a hosted service with paid plans; simpler, but some features depend on the plan. Most business sites, including the ones I build, use WordPress.org on quality hosting.

Why businesses choose WordPress

  • You own your site and can move it anytime
  • Easy to edit once set up properly
  • Excellent for blogging and SEO
  • Thousands of plugins for almost any feature
  • Many developers available, so you are never stuck

The trade-offs

  • Needs regular updates and backups
  • Low-quality themes and plugins can slow it down
  • Security is your responsibility (or your maintenance provider's)

What a WordPress website costs
