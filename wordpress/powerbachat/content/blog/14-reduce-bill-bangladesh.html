Every summer, families across Bangladesh watch their prepaid balance drain or their postpaid bill jump. Heat and humidity keep fans and ACs running day and night, and the tariff charges more for each unit as usage climbs. The good news is that a handful of changes can reduce your electricity bill noticeably without making the house uncomfortable. This guide focuses on the ones that matter most.

1. Know the 400 unit line

The residential tariff rises steadily through the lower slabs, then jumps sharply above 400 units a month. For a family that uses 450 units in July, the last 50 are the most expensive units of the year.

Read the meter every week. If you are heading past 400, the last ten days of the month are where small changes save the most. Our electricity bill calculator shows the exact difference for your numbers.

2. Run the AC smarter

  • Set it to 25 or 26 degrees and use a ceiling fan. Each degree lower adds noticeably to the AC's use.
  • Use dry mode on humid evenings when the temperature is not extreme. Dhaka's discomfort is often humidity, and dry mode removes it with less power.
  • Cool one room and keep its door closed, rather than cooling several rooms poorly.
  • Clean the filter every few weeks in summer. Dust makes the AC work harder.
  • Shade the outdoor unit without blocking its airflow.

3. Fans: the quiet big spender

Ceiling fans run for most of the day in Bangladesh, so their combined use is large. An old fan uses around 75 to 80 watts; an efficient DC fan uses roughly half. Replacing the three or four fans you use most can save a meaningful number of units every month, and it also stretches your IPS backup time.

4. Check the water pump

In buildings with an underground reservoir and a roof tank, the pump can quietly waste dozens of units a month if the float switch is faulty or the tank leaks. Listen for the pump running longer than usual, check the overflow pipe, and fix leaks quickly. A simple timer can stop the pump from running more than it needs to.

5. Look after your IPS

An IPS recharges its battery from the grid after every cut. Old batteries and inefficient units waste a share of that energy as heat, adding 20 to 50 units a month in many homes. If backup time has fallen and the battery is several years old, a replacement may lower your bill as well as restore backup. Our IPS calculator helps you size it.

6. Fridge and freezer habits

  • Keep the fridge away from the stove and out of direct sun.
  • Leave a little space behind it for air to flow.
  • Check the door seal; a worn seal makes it run almost constantly.
  • Do not put hot food straight in.

7. Lighting and standby

Replace remaining tube lights and older bulbs with LEDs. Switch off TVs, set-top boxes and chargers at the wall when not in use. Each one is small, but together they add up over a month.

8. Keep the heat out

The less heat enters, the less your fans and AC need to work. Close curtains on sun-facing windows by late morning. Open windows on opposite sides in the early morning for a cross breeze. If you live on the top floor, a light-coloured roof coating can lower indoor temperatures noticeably.

9. Plan prepaid recharges

If you have a prepaid meter, estimate the month's usage at the start and recharge once with a small buffer. Watching the balance weekly helps you notice when usage is climbing. Our prepaid meter guide explains what each deduction is.

10. Consider solar for the long term

If you own a house with a sunny roof and regularly use more than 400 units in summer, rooftop solar can take your usage below the expensive slabs and provide backup during load-shedding. See the solar system price in Bangladesh page for costs by size.

11. Cook and wash at the right time

Rice cookers, electric kettles, irons and washing machines draw a lot of power for short periods. Using them in the morning or early afternoon, rather than in the evening when the AC and lights are also on, does not change the tariff, but it makes it easier to spot your real usage pattern and avoids tripping breakers on older wiring. If you have solar, daytime use also means more of your own power gets used directly.

12. Get the family involved

The best way to reduce your electricity bill over a whole summer is to make it a shared project. Show everyone the weekly meter reading and the calculator's estimate, and agree simple rules: AC at 26 degrees, doors closed, fans off in empty rooms. Children often become the strictest enforcers. A target such as staying under 400 units gives everyone something concrete to aim for, and the saving at the end of the month makes the point better than any lecture.

What not to do

  • Do not buy cheap, unbranded "power saver" devices that claim to cut your bill. They do not work.
  • Do not run an old AC without servicing it to save on the service fee; it will cost more in units.
  • Do not tamper with the meter or wiring. It is illegal and dangerous.

A realistic example

A family in Mirpur used 480 units last July. This year they raise the AC to 26 degrees with a fan, use dry mode in the evenings, replace two old fans, fix a leaking roof tank valve and keep the curtains closed in the afternoon. Their July usage drops to around 390 units. Every unit they saved came from the expensive top of the bill, so the drop in cost is far larger than the drop in units suggests.

None of these steps needs special equipment or a big budget. Start with the AC setting and the weekly meter reading this month, add the rest as you can, and you will reduce your electricity bill every summer from now on.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to reduce electricity bill in summer?

Raise the AC temperature to 25 or 26 degrees with a fan, and keep total usage under 400 units if you can.

Does an IPS increase the bill?

Yes, slightly, because it recharges from the grid. An old battery wastes more.

Are DC fans worth it?

For fans that run many hours a day, yes. They use roughly half the power of old fans.

How do I know my usage before the bill?

Read your meter weekly, or check your prepaid meter's usage screen, and use the bill calculator.
