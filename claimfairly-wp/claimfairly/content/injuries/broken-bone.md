A broken bone is one of the clearest injuries to prove. It shows up on an X-ray, it has a known treatment and it comes with obvious limits on daily life. That is why fracture claims are often valued higher than soft tissue claims with similar medical bills.

## Estimate your broken bone claim

The calculator is preset to **serious**, the usual category for fractures. Pick moderate for a simple fracture that healed quickly, or severe if you have lasting limits.

## What affects the value of a fracture claim

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Type of fracture | A simple break heals more predictably than a compound or comminuted (shattered) fracture |
| Which bone | Leg, hip and spine fractures affect walking and work more than a finger or toe |
| Surgery | Plates, screws or rods usually mean higher bills, longer recovery and a higher multiplier |
| Healing time | Many fractures take six weeks or more to heal, with therapy after |
| Lasting effects | Arthritis, reduced motion or hardware that needs removal later add value |

## The recovery timeline matters

Recovery from a fracture often includes a cast or splint, follow up X-rays, and physical therapy to rebuild strength and range of motion. Keep a record of each stage: the weeks you could not drive, work or care for your family are central to pain and suffering.

## Future medical costs

Ask your doctor whether you may need hardware removal, more therapy, or treatment for arthritis later. Future costs belong in your claim, and they are easy to forget if you settle too early. Add them in the "future medical costs" field above.

## Policy limits often cap fracture claims

A fracture with surgery can easily exceed a minimum policy like $25,000 per person. If the at-fault driver's limit is low, look at your own underinsured motorist coverage. Enter the limit in the calculator to see the practical ceiling.
