Our TESCO bill calculator is for metered households supplied by the Tribal Areas Electricity Supply Company. TESCO serves the merged districts of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, including Khyber, Mohmand, Bajaur, Kurram, Orakzai, North Waziristan and South Waziristan, along with some adjoining areas. If your home has a meter and a TESCO bill, enter your units above and you will see the slab charges, the taxes and an estimated total.

Electricity supply and billing in the merged districts have their own history. For a long time many connections were not billed by meter at all, and in some places that is still changing. As metering spreads, more families are receiving regular bills for the first time, and the slab system can be confusing at first. This page explains it in plain terms.

How to use the TESCO bill calculator

  1. Look at your bill for the "previous" and "present" meter readings.
  2. Read the meter yourself and subtract the present reading on the bill. That is what you have used since.
  3. For a full-month estimate, divide by the number of days and multiply by 30.
  4. Enter the figure above. Use "Protected consumer" only if each of your last six bills showed 200 units or less.

If your home is not metered, or you receive a fixed monthly charge, this calculator will not match your bill. In that case, the tables below are still useful to understand what a metered bill would look like.

Understanding a slab bill for the first time

If you are used to a fixed charge, a slab bill can feel unfair at first, because the price per unit rises as you use more. Think of it like this. The first 100 units of the month are the cheapest. The next 100 cost a bit more. Each further block of units costs more again. That design is meant to keep basic use affordable and make heavy use pay more. In practice it means two things for a family:

  • Basic needs like lights, fans, a fridge and phone charging are relatively cheap, especially if you stay protected.
  • Heavy loads such as heaters, ACs, large water pumps and welding or workshop tools quickly push the bill into the expensive slabs.

Our guide on how to read an electricity bill shows where each of these numbers appears on the printed page.

A worked example

A family in Landi Kotal uses lights, three fans, a fridge and a TV, plus a small water pump. In a normal month the meter shows about 170 units. With six such months in a row, they are protected and pay the low rates. In one hot month the pump runs more and a borrowed cooler adds load, and the meter reaches 230 units. The TESCO bill calculator shows how much that one month costs on the normal tariff, and that the higher rates will continue for six months. Seeing this in advance, the family moves the pump to off-peak use, fixes a leaking tank float, and stays under the line.

Load-shedding, backup and solar

Supply in parts of the merged districts can be limited to certain hours. Many families have already turned to solar panels and batteries, sometimes without any grid connection at all. If you are thinking about it, size the system from your real needs rather than an installer's package. Our solar calculator estimates the panels you need, and the battery backup calculator shows how long a battery bank will run your fans and lights. The comparison of lithium and tubular batteries is worth reading before you buy, because batteries are the most expensive part to replace.

Practical ways to keep the bill low

  • Fix leaks in water tanks. A float valve that does not close keeps the pump running for hours.
  • Choose LED bulbs and DC inverter fans. They cost a little more and use far less.
  • Keep heavy tools off the household meter where you can. Workshops quickly push a home into high slabs.
  • Read the meter every week. It is the easiest way to stay under 200 units and keep protected rates.

Getting a proper metered connection

If your home does not have its own meter, or several houses share one, it is worth asking the local TESCO office about a separate metered connection. A shared meter combines everyone's units, so the bill quickly climbs into the expensive slabs even when each family uses little. With its own meter, a modest home can often stay under 200 units and pay protected rates. Take your CNIC and proof of residence when you ask, and request a written list of the documents and fees for your area, since procedures in the merged districts are still settling down.

Checking your reading

When a bill arrives, compare the present reading with your meter. If the bill shows more units than the meter, photograph the display with the date visible and take it to the TESCO office with the bill before the due date. Keeping a simple notebook of weekly readings makes any dispute much easier, because you can show exactly how your usage moved through the month.

Charges the estimate does not include

  • The monthly fuel price adjustment.
  • Arrears, late payment surcharge and income tax where they apply.
  • Any special or fixed arrangements in areas where metering is still being introduced.

Frequently asked questions

Does the TESCO bill calculator work if my home is not metered?

No. It estimates metered bills from units used. If you pay a fixed amount, your bill follows that arrangement instead.

Are TESCO rates the same as PESCO?

Yes. Metered households in both companies use the same national tariff. See our PESCO bill calculator for the rest of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.

How do I become a protected consumer?

Use 200 units or less in each of six consecutive months. The status is checked from the history on your bill.

Is solar a good idea in the merged districts?

Often yes, especially where grid hours are limited. A small system with a good battery can cover lights, fans and a fridge for most of the day.
