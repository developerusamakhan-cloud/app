The LESCO unit price is not one number. It depends on how many units your home uses in a month, whether you are a protected consumer, and which slab each unit falls into. This page lays out every rate clearly, explains what gets added on top, and walks through a real example for a Lahore home, so you can work out the true cost of a unit in your own house.

If you just want the total for your units, the LESCO bill calculator does all of this for you in a few seconds. If you want to understand where the number comes from, read on.

These slabs are telescopic. Your first 100 units are charged at the first rate, the next 100 at the second rate, and so on. Only the units inside a higher slab cost the higher price. Using 310 units does not make all 310 units expensive. It makes the last 10 units expensive.

What gets added to the base unit price

The slab rates above are only part of the story. Several charges are added per unit or on top of the energy charge:

  • Fuel price adjustment. A per-unit amount, positive or negative, that changes monthly. It can move the effective price of a unit noticeably. See our FPA guide.
  • Quarterly adjustment. When it applies, another per-unit amount for a few months.
  • GST at 18 percent on the energy charge and most adjustments.
  • Electricity duty, a small provincial percentage. We use 1.5 percent in our estimates.

Add those together and the real LESCO unit price you pay is meaningfully higher than the slab rate printed in the tariff. The table below shows the effective average, with GST and duty included, at different levels of use.

A worked example for a Lahore home

Take a family in Johar Town that used 350 units last month and is not protected. Here is how LESCO works out the energy charge:

  1. The first 100 units are charged at the first slab rate.
  2. Units 101 to 200 are charged at the second slab rate.
  3. Units 201 to 300 are charged at the third slab rate.
  4. The last 50 units, 301 to 350, are charged at the fourth slab rate.

Notice that the family never pays the fourth slab rate on all 350 units, only on the 50 that sit inside that slab. This is the part most people get wrong when they complain that crossing 300 units made everything expensive. Those four amounts are added together to make the energy charge. Electricity duty and GST are then added, followed by the month's fuel adjustment. If you want to see the exact rupee figure for each step, enter 350 in the LESCO bill calculator and open the slab-by-slab breakdown.

The useful insight from this example: the family's most expensive units are the last 50. If they can trim those, they save at the highest rate they pay. Cutting 50 units from a quiet week in winter saves much less.

Working out your own real price per unit

The simplest honest number is your own. Take your last bill, find the total amount payable before arrears and late payment surcharge, and divide it by the units consumed. That gives you the real price you paid for each unit last month, with every slab, tax and adjustment included. Do it for a winter bill and a summer bill and you will see the difference the slabs make.

This figure is also the right one to use when you compare options. If your real price is high, a more efficient AC, an LED upgrade or a solar system pays back faster, because every unit it saves is worth more. If it is low, because you use little or are protected, focus on keeping it that way rather than on expensive upgrades.

Peak and off-peak unit prices

Homes with a sanctioned load of 5 kW or more are usually on a time-of-use meter. Their bills show separate peak and off-peak units, and the LESCO unit price differs between the two, with peak units costing more. Peak hours fall in the evening and shift with the season, so check the hours printed on your own bill. If you are on this kind of meter, shifting heavy loads like the washing machine, iron and water pump to off-peak hours lowers your average unit price without using any less electricity.

How Lahore compares with other companies

The base slab rates are identical across Pakistan, so a unit in Lahore costs the same as a unit in Islamabad, Multan or Karachi before adjustments. What differs from month to month is the fuel adjustment and, for Karachi, the separately calculated fuel cost. Our national per unit electricity price page shows the full picture, and the K-Electric unit price page covers Karachi in detail.

Is it time to think about solar?

When your top slab is the one charging the most per unit, every unit of solar you produce displaces your most expensive electricity first. That is why solar pays back faster for heavy users. Frequently asked questions

What is the LESCO unit price today?

See the tables above. They show the current rate for every slab, protected and non-protected, last checked in [powerbachat_checked].

Why is my effective LESCO unit price higher than the slab rate?

Because GST, electricity duty and the monthly fuel adjustment are added to the slab rate. Together they raise the real cost of each unit.

Does the LESCO unit price change every month?

The base slab rates change only when a new tariff is notified, usually once or twice a year. The fuel adjustment changes every month, which is why bills move even when your units stay the same.

Do flats and houses pay the same rate?

Yes, for household connections. Commercial and industrial connections use different tariffs.
