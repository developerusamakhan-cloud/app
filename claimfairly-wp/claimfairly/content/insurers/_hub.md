Most car accident claims in the United States go through a handful of very large insurance companies. Whether you are their customer or the other driver, it helps to know how their process usually works: how to file, what happens after, how repairs and injuries are handled, and how to protect your side of the claim.

## What is the same at every insurer

The broad steps are almost identical everywhere:

1. **You report the claim** online, in an app, by phone or through an agent.
2. **An adjuster is assigned** and decides who was at fault.
3. **Vehicle damage is assessed** through photos, an inspection or a shop estimate.
4. **Property damage is paid**, for repairs or a total loss.
5. **Injury claims are reviewed** once medical records and bills are available.
6. **An offer is made** and negotiated.
7. **You sign a release** and the claim is paid.

## First-party vs third-party claims

| | Claim with your own insurer | Claim with the other driver's insurer |
|---|---|---|
| Called | First-party claim | Third-party claim |
| Based on | Your policy's coverages | Their driver's liability |
| Deductible | Usually applies | Usually does not apply |
| Speed | Often faster | Can wait on a fault decision |
| Pain and suffering | Usually not, except UM/UIM | Yes, in at-fault states |

Many people file with their own insurer for a quick repair and let their insurer recover the money from the other company later.

## Questions to ask any adjuster

- What is my claim number, and what is your direct contact information?
- Have you made a fault decision, and what is it based on?
- What documents do you still need from me?
- Is there a rental car available while my car is repaired?
- What is the policy limit for bodily injury and property damage?
- Can you send your offer and how you calculated it in writing?

Write the answers down with the date and the adjuster's name.

## Tips that work with any insurer

- **Get the claim number and adjuster's name** in writing.
- **Keep communication in writing** where you can.
- **Be careful with recorded statements** to the other driver's insurer.
- **Know your numbers** before you negotiate. Use the [car accident settlement calculator](/car-accident-settlement-calculator/) and the [diminished value calculator](/diminished-value-calculator/).
- **Put your demand in writing** with the [demand letter generator](/demand-letter-generator/).
- **Do not sign a release** until you know the full cost of your injuries.

## Read your declarations page first

Before you call any insurer, find your policy's **declarations page**, the summary sheet that lists your coverages and limits. It answers the most important questions in a minute:

| Coverage on the page | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Bodily injury liability | What your policy pays others you injure |
| Property damage liability | What your policy pays for others' property |
| Collision | Whether your own car repairs are covered, and your deductible |
| Comprehensive | Coverage for non-crash damage like theft or hail |
| MedPay or PIP | Medical bills covered regardless of fault |
| Uninsured / underinsured motorist | Protection when the other driver has little or no insurance |
| Rental reimbursement | Whether a rental car is covered and for how long |

Knowing your own coverage helps you decide whether to file with your insurer, the other driver's, or both.

## If you have a problem with an insurer

Ask for decisions and reasons in writing. Ask to speak with a supervisor if you are stuck. If that does not work, you can file a complaint with your state's department of insurance, which regulates how insurers handle claims. For serious injuries or a denial you think is wrong, a free consultation with a local attorney can help.
