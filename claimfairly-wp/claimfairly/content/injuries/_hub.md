The type of injury you have shapes almost everything about your claim: how long treatment takes, how easy the injury is to prove, which multiplier an insurer is likely to use, and whether you can claim pain and suffering at all in a no-fault state. This section explains the most common car accident injuries one by one, with a settlement calculator preset for each.

## How injury type affects a settlement

| Injury | Easy to prove? | Typical severity | Common insurer argument |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whiplash | Harder, few objective findings | Minor to moderate | "Low speed crash, minor damage" |
| Back strain | Harder | Minor to moderate | "It should have healed in weeks" |
| Herniated disc | Easier with an MRI | Moderate to serious | "The disc problem was already there" |
| Fracture | Easy, shows on X-ray | Serious | "It healed well, no lasting effect" |
| Concussion | Harder, rarely shows on scans | Minor to serious | "Symptoms are from something else" |

## Other injuries we see often

- **Shoulder injuries**, like rotator cuff tears, often from gripping the wheel or the seat belt. They can need surgery and long therapy.
- **Knee injuries**, from hitting the dashboard. Ligament and meniscus tears can be serious even without a fracture.
- **Cuts and scarring**, from glass or airbags. Scars on the face or hands can increase pain and suffering.
- **Chest and rib injuries**, from the seat belt or airbag. Painful and slow to heal, but often valued as moderate.
- **Emotional injuries**, like anxiety about driving or trouble sleeping. These are part of pain and suffering and are stronger with a diagnosis from a professional.

For any of these, use the [car accident settlement calculator](/car-accident-settlement-calculator/) with your total medical bills and the severity that fits best.

## A checklist for any injury claim

1. Medical records from the first visit onward
2. Every bill, with the provider and amount
3. Proof of lost wages or reduced hours
4. A pain or symptom journal
5. Photos of injuries, equipment and scars
6. Receipts for out-of-pocket costs
7. Your doctor's notes on future care and restrictions
8. The police report and vehicle photos

## What every injury claim has in common

No matter the injury, the same things raise or lower the value:

- **Prompt medical care.** A visit within a day or two links the injury to the crash.
- **Consistent treatment.** Gaps give adjusters a reason to discount pain.
- **Written records of daily limits.** A pain or symptom journal is simple and powerful.
- **Clear fault.** Police reports, photos and witnesses matter.
- **Enough insurance.** The at-fault driver's policy limit is often the real ceiling.

## Valuing pain and suffering by injury

Injuries with expensive treatment usually favor the multiplier method. Injuries with long recoveries and modest bills, like whiplash and concussions, often favor the per diem method. Compare both with the [pain and suffering calculator](/pain-and-suffering-calculator/) and read [multiplier vs per diem](/multiplier-vs-per-diem-pain-and-suffering/).

## No-fault states and injury thresholds

In no-fault states, you can usually claim pain and suffering from the other driver only if the injury meets a legal threshold. Fractures and permanent injuries often qualify. Whiplash and short-lived concussions often do not. See [at-fault vs no-fault states](/at-fault-vs-no-fault-states/) and your state on our [state rules pages](/states/).

## When to talk to a lawyer about an injury

- Surgery, a hospital stay or a fracture
- Symptoms lasting more than a few months
- A head injury with ongoing symptoms
- Bills close to or above the other driver's policy limit
- Fault disputed or blamed on you

Most injury lawyers offer free consultations. Bring your estimate from the calculator so the conversation starts with numbers.
