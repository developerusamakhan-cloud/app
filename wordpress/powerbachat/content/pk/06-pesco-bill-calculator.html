This PESCO bill calculator is for households supplied by the Peshawar Electric Supply Company, which serves most of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, including Peshawar, Mardan, Charsadda, Nowshera, Swat, Abbottabad, Mansehra, Kohat, Bannu and Dera Ismail Khan. Enter the units you have used and the tool shows the slab-by-slab charge, the taxes and an estimated total, so the paper bill holds no surprises.

Khyber Pakhtunkhwa is a province of contrasts. Peshawar and D.I. Khan can be as hot as anywhere in Punjab in June, while Swat, Abbottabad and the Galiyat need heating for months. That means PESCO bills have two peaks in many homes: summer cooling and winter heating. A quick estimate during either season helps you keep both in check.

Estimating your PESCO bill

  1. Note the present reading printed on your last bill.
  2. Read the meter now and subtract. That is your units so far.
  3. If the month is not over, divide by the days passed and multiply by 30.
  4. Enter the result above. Tick "Protected consumer" only if all of your last six bills were at 200 units or less.

The per unit column shows something people often miss: the more you use, the more each unit costs on average, because a larger share of your units falls into the expensive slabs. A large share of PESCO households live under or near the 200 unit line for much of the year, so protected status matters more here than almost anywhere. A family in Mardan with fans, lights, a fridge and a TV can stay protected through winter, then lose it in a single June week with an AC running. Winter heating in Swat and Abbottabad

In the hill districts, low gas pressure pushes many families to electric heating in December and January. A 2,000 watt heater running five hours a day uses about 300 units a month on its own, which can double a winter bill. If you have an inverter AC, its heat mode delivers the same warmth for around a third of the units. An electric blanket at night uses a tiny fraction of either. For hot water, a geyser on a timer saves more than people expect.

Load-shedding and backup power

Many PESCO feeders, especially those with high line losses, still face scheduled load-shedding for several hours a day. Most families rely on a UPS with batteries. That backup is not free: every time power returns, the batteries recharge from your meter, and inefficient lead-acid setups lose a fair share of that energy as heat. Use our battery backup calculator to size a system that covers your actual outage hours without oversizing, and read our lithium vs tubular battery comparison before you replace old batteries.

A worked example from Peshawar

A family in Hayatabad reads 210 units on day 17 of the cycle. At that pace they will reach about 370 units. The PESCO bill calculator shows the estimate and, in the breakdown, that the final 70 units fall in the 301 to 400 slab. They decide to run the AC only in the evening and night instead of all afternoon for the rest of the month, and finish near 300 units. Their bill drops by the cost of that top slab plus the tax on it, which is a meaningful amount for one small change.

When the bill does not match the meter

Complaints about wrong readings are common in every company, and PESCO is no exception. If the units on your bill are higher than your meter shows, act before the due date. Take a clear photo of the meter display with the date visible, bring your bill and CNIC to your subdivision office, and ask for a reading correction. Keep a copy of whatever you submit. If the meter itself seems faulty, for example running when the main switch is off, ask for a meter check. Paying a disputed bill in full and arguing later is much harder than getting it corrected first.

Understanding the layout of a PESCO bill

The bill packs a lot onto one page. The top section holds your name, address, reference number and tariff code. The meter box shows the previous and present readings and the units consumed. Below that, the charges section lists the energy cost, fuel adjustment, taxes and any arrears. On one side you will find the twelve-month history table. If you only check three things each month, make them the units consumed, the FPA line and the history table. Our guide on how to read an electricity bill walks through each box in more detail.

Charges outside the calculator

  • Fuel price adjustment: a monthly per-unit charge or credit. See the FPA guide.
  • Arrears, surcharge and income tax: these depend on your account and payment history.
  • Peak and off-peak readings: on connections with a sanctioned load of 5 kW or more.

Lowering a PESCO bill

  • Protect the protected status. If you are close to 200 units, plan the last week of the month carefully.
  • Use the AC heat mode instead of resistance heaters in winter.
  • Insulate the obvious gaps. Thick curtains and door draught stoppers cut heating needs in the hills.
  • Look at solar with batteries. Where load-shedding is long, a hybrid solar system both cuts the bill and keeps the fans running. Our solar calculator is a good first step.

Frequently asked questions

Does the PESCO bill calculator work in all KP districts?

It works for every PESCO household connection. The merged tribal districts are served by TESCO, which has its own TESCO bill calculator page using the same tariff.

Why is my PESCO bill so high in winter?

Electric heating is the usual reason. One heater can add 300 units a month. Check your heating habits first, then compare units month by month.

Are PESCO rates higher than other companies?

No. The base tariff is the same nationwide. Differences come from monthly adjustments and usage.

Can I check a PESCO bill online?

Yes, with your 14-digit reference number. Enter the units from the online bill here to confirm the energy charge.
