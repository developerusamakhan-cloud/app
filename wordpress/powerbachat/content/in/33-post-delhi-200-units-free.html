The Delhi electricity subsidy is one of the best known in India. Many households pay nothing at all for electricity for much of the year, thanks to the 200 units free electricity promise. But the subsidy has two lines, at 200 and 400 units, and understanding both is the difference between a zero bill and a surprise. This guide explains how the scheme has generally worked and how families plan around it.

The two lines that matter

As the Delhi electricity subsidy has generally applied to domestic consumers who opt in:

  • Up to 200 units a month: the bill is fully subsidised. Eligible households pay nothing for energy.
  • 201 to 400 units: the government pays 50 percent of the bill, up to a monthly cap.
  • Above 400 units: no subsidy for that month. The full tariff applies.

The scheme is set by the Delhi government and its terms can change, so always check the subsidy line on your own bill.

Opting in

The subsidy has been offered to consumers who choose to receive it. If you see no subsidy line on your bill despite using under 200 units, check with your distribution company, BSES Rajdhani, BSES Yamuna or Tata Power-DDL, whether your connection is registered for it.

Staying under 200 units

For a small family, 200 units a month is very achievable for much of the year. In summer it is harder. A few habits help:

  1. Read the meter weekly. 200 units a month is about 50 a week.
  2. Use a cooler in the dry weeks. A large cooler uses a fraction of an AC's power and works well in Delhi's May heat.
  3. Run the AC at 25 or 26 degrees with a fan, and only in the room you are in.
  4. Swap old fans for BLDC fans. Fans run almost all year in Delhi, so the saving adds up.
  5. Switch off the geyser after use in winter.

The cost of crossing 200 and 400

Crossing 200 units turns a zero bill into a real one, even with the 50 percent subsidy. Crossing 400 units loses the subsidy entirely for that month, so the bill can jump sharply. For a family near either line, the last week of the billing period is where most of the saving lies.

A worked example

A family in Laxmi Nagar used 180 units in March and paid nothing. In June, with two ACs running, they reach 320 units by the 20th. At that pace, the month will end above 400 units, losing the subsidy. They decide to use only the bedroom AC at night and a cooler during the day for the last ten days. They finish at 395 units, keeping the 50 percent subsidy. The saving is substantial for ten days of slightly different habits.

Fixed charges and other lines

Even with a zero energy bill, a few lines may appear depending on the current rules, such as fixed charges based on your sanctioned load, power purchase adjustments and surcharges. The subsidy is applied according to the scheme's terms. If you are unsure what a line means, our guide on how an electricity bill is calculated explains each one.

Which months to watch

For most Delhi families, November to March are easy subsidy months: fans are off or slow, and only the geyser needs watching. April and October are borderline, with warm afternoons and the first cooler or AC use. May to September is the hard stretch, with dry heat followed by humid monsoon heat. If you know you will cross 200 units in June and July, focus your effort on staying under 400 in those months and under 200 in the shoulder months either side. That pattern alone keeps the Delhi electricity subsidy working for you for most of the year.

Families who share a meter

In many Delhi homes, two families or a family and tenants share one connection. Their combined units push the total over 200 or even 400, so nobody benefits fully from the subsidy. A separate connection for each household, where the building allows it, can turn one large unsubsidised bill into two subsidised ones. Ask your distribution company about the process and documents. The saving over a year is often far more than the cost of the new meter.

Checking your bill each month

Every Delhi bill shows the units, the regular charges and the subsidy credit. Compare the units with your own meter reading, and make sure the subsidy appears when you expect it. If the units are wrong or the subsidy is missing, contact your discom through its app or helpline before the due date. Keeping a photo of your meter on the reading day makes any dispute quick to settle.

Should Delhi homes go solar?

For homes that stay under 200 units most of the year, the subsidy already makes electricity almost free, so rooftop solar pays back slowly. For homes that regularly use 400 units or more in summer, solar can bring them back under the subsidy lines, which multiplies the saving. With the central PM Surya Ghar subsidy, a 2 or 3 kW system is affordable. See our 3kW solar system price page.

Common misunderstandings

  • "200 units free means 200 units free every month regardless." Only if you stay at or under 200 units. Above that, the rules change.
  • "Above 200, I lose everything." Not until 400. Between 201 and 400, a 50 percent subsidy has applied, up to a cap.
  • "The subsidy is automatic." Consumers have needed to opt in.
