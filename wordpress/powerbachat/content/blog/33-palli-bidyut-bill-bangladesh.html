Most homes outside the big cities in Bangladesh get their electricity from a Palli Bidyut Samity, the local rural electricity cooperative under the Bangladesh Rural Electrification Board. If you live in a village, an upazila town or a growing suburb, your Palli Bidyut bill probably arrives on paper each month, or your prepaid meter deducts from a balance. Either way, the numbers can be hard to follow. This guide explains how the bill is worked out, how to check it, and the easiest ways to pay.

Who supplies rural electricity

The Bangladesh Rural Electrification Board, known as BREB or REB, oversees around 80 rural cooperatives called Palli Bidyut Samities, or PBS. Each PBS covers a group of upazilas, reads meters, sends bills, handles complaints and runs local offices. Cities are served by other companies such as DESCO and DPDC in Dhaka, but the tariff for homes is set nationally by the regulator.

The same residential slabs as the cities

The Bangladesh Energy Regulatory Commission sets one residential tariff for the whole country. That means a family in a PBS area pays the same rate per unit, slab for slab, as a family in Dhaka with the same use. The rate climbs as monthly units rise, with a special lifeline rate for very small users and a sharp jump in the top slabs.

Because the whole month is charged in slabs, every extra unit near a slab line costs more than the units before it. Families who stay just under a line save more than they expect.

What else is on the bill

The energy charge is the biggest part, but your bill has a few more lines:

  • Demand charge. A fixed monthly amount based on your sanctioned load in kilowatts. You pay it even if you use very few units. A home with a higher sanctioned load pays more.
  • Meter rent. A small monthly charge for the meter, depending on its type.
  • VAT. Value added tax is added on top of the energy and demand charges, usually at 5 percent for electricity. Check the rate printed on your bill.
  • Late payment surcharge. If a postpaid bill is paid after the due date, a surcharge is added to the next bill.
  • Arrears. Any unpaid amount from earlier months is carried forward.

Prepaid meters in PBS areas

Many PBS areas have moved homes to prepaid meters, and more are being installed. With a prepaid meter you buy credit first and the meter deducts as you use power. The same slabs, demand charge and VAT apply, but they are taken from your balance in small amounts. Some deductions, such as the monthly demand charge and meter rent, may be taken at the first recharge of the month, which is why a recharge sometimes seems smaller than expected.

A worked example

Take a family in a village near Bogura using about 200 units in a normal month and 300 units in summer when fans run all day and a fridge works harder. Their sanctioned load is 2 kW.

The table shows the full bill at each level. Going from 200 to 300 units does more than add 100 units at the same rate. The extra units fall in higher slabs, so the bill rises faster than the units. On top of the energy charge, the family pays a demand charge for their 2 kW load, a little meter rent, and VAT on the total.

If they replace two old ceiling fans with efficient DC fans and fix a fridge door seal, they might save around 30 to 40 units a month in summer. Because those are top slab units, the saving is larger than the average rate suggests. Try your own figures in our electricity bill calculator.

How to check your Palli Bidyut bill

  1. Compare the meter reading. Read your meter on the day the bill arrives. The present reading on the bill should be close to, or a little below, what you see.
  2. Check the units. Present reading minus previous reading should equal the units billed.
  3. Look for estimated readings. If the bill says the reading was estimated, the units may be too high or too low. The next actual reading will correct it.
  4. Check the sanctioned load. If the demand charge seems high, make sure your load on the bill matches your connection.
  5. Check arrears and surcharge. Make sure past payments were recorded.

If something is wrong, take the bill and your last payment receipts to the PBS office or its zonal or sub-zonal office. Ask for a correction in writing and keep the complaint number.

Paying by mobile banking

You no longer need to queue at a bank to pay. Most PBS bills can be paid through mobile banking services such as bKash and Nagad, and through some bank apps. In general terms, you open the pay bill option, choose Palli Bidyut, enter your account number or SMS account number from the bill, and confirm the amount. Prepaid meter users can also buy tokens or credit through these apps in many areas.

Service charges and steps can change, so check the app before paying, and keep the confirmation SMS until the payment shows on your next bill.

A note on the irrigation season

From roughly January to April, rural Bangladesh enters the Boro irrigation season. Thousands of irrigation pump connections switch on, and PBS networks come under heavy load. Irrigation pumps are on their own meters and a separate agricultural tariff, so they do not appear on a home bill. But households may see more load-shedding in these months as the network prioritises irrigation in the evening and night. If you run a home IPS through these months, our IPS calculator helps you size it for longer outages.

If you also own an irrigation pump, keep its meter and bill separate and check that pump units are not being added to your home account by mistake.

Frequently asked questions

Is the rate in a PBS area different from Dhaka?

No. The residential tariff is set nationally by BERC, so the per unit slab rates are the same for PBS and city consumers.

Why do I pay a demand charge if I used few units?

The demand charge is based on your sanctioned load, not your use. It covers the cost of keeping capacity available for your connection.

Can I pay my Palli Bidyut bill with bKash or Nagad?

In most areas, yes. Use the pay bill option, choose Palli Bidyut and enter the account number from your bill.

What should I do if my bill looks too high?

Check the meter reading and units first. If they do not match, go to your PBS office with the bill and ask for a correction.
