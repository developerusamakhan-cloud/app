Quick answer: a WordPress developer builds and customises WordPress sites in code: custom themes, plugin customisation, WooCommerce stores, integrations with other systems, speed optimisation, security fixes and migrations. You need one when your site requires more than a ready-made theme and a few plugins can deliver, or when something is slow, broken or hacked.

"WordPress developer" is one of the most searched job titles in web design, and also one of the most misunderstood. Here is what the job actually involves, from someone who does it every day. If you are new to WordPress itself, start with what is a WordPress website.

What a WordPress developer does

Builds custom themes

Turns a design from Figma into a fast, lightweight theme made just for your site, instead of bending a heavy multipurpose theme into shape.

Customises plugins

Adjusts how plugins behave through hooks and filters, or writes small custom plugins when nothing on the market fits.

Builds WooCommerce stores

Products, payments, shipping, tax and custom checkout features. See WooCommerce development explained.

Connects systems

Links your site to CRMs, email marketing, booking tools, payment gateways and other services through their APIs.

Makes sites fast

Diagnoses slow pages and fixes hosting, caching, images and code. See WordPress speed optimization.

Secures and repairs

Fixes errors, cleans hacked sites and hardens security.

Migrates sites

Moves sites to new hosts or from other platforms to WordPress without losing content or rankings.

Looks after sites

Ongoing updates, backups and improvements through maintenance plans.

Skills that matter

  • PHP, HTML, CSS and JavaScript
  • WordPress hooks, the block editor and theme structure
  • WooCommerce
  • Performance and security
  • Hosting, servers and debugging
  • Clear communication with non-technical clients

Developer, designer or expert?

Designers shape how a site looks and works for users; developers build it. Some, like me, do both. For the differences, read WordPress expert vs WordPress developer.

When your business needs one

  • You want a custom design or features
  • You are building or improving an online store
  • The site is slow, broken or was hacked
  • You need to connect your site to other tools
  • You are moving platform or host
