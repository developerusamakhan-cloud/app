Quick answer: a Shopify store costs a monthly Shopify plan, plus apps, payment processing fees on each sale, an optional premium theme, a domain, and usually a one-time design and setup fee if you hire help, commonly $1,000 to $5,000 for a small store. Apps are where costs quietly grow, so plan them carefully.

"Shopify is only a few dollars a month" is true on the pricing page, and misleading in practice. Here is the full picture, so your first year has no surprises.

1. The Shopify plan

Shopify offers several plans, billed monthly or yearly, with discounts for yearly billing. The entry plan is enough for most new stores. Check Shopify's pricing page for current numbers, as they change.

2. The theme

Free themes are good starting points. Premium themes are a one-time purchase and offer more layouts and features. A custom theme costs more but makes your brand stand out.

3. Apps

Reviews, subscriptions, upsells, filters, bundles, email marketing. Many charge monthly. Ten apps at modest prices can cost more than the plan itself, and many also slow down the store. Choose apps for clear needs, and review them every few months.

4. Payment fees

Every sale has processing fees. Using Shopify Payments usually avoids an extra transaction fee that applies when you use a third-party payment provider. Build fees into your pricing.

5. Design and setup

  • DIY: your time, often 40 to 100 hours for a first store
  • Freelancer: commonly $1,000 to $5,000 for a small store
  • Agency: often $5,000 and up

See how to hire a Shopify developer for what to ask.

6. Domain and email

A domain costs roughly $15 to $25 a year. Business email is a small extra monthly cost.

A realistic first-year budget

For a small store with a freelancer setup, a premium theme and a handful of apps, budget for the one-time build plus twelve months of plan and apps, and keep a margin for marketing. The exact number depends on your products and choices, which is why I always quote after a short conversation.

How to keep costs down

  • Start on the entry plan and upgrade when sales justify it
  • Use theme features before adding apps
  • Replace stacks of apps with a small custom feature when it pays off
  • Pay yearly once you are sure about the plan

Shopify or WooCommerce?

If monthly fees worry you, compare with WooCommerce development, which has no platform fee but needs hosting and care. Design principles for both are in ecommerce website design.
