Back injuries cover a wide range. A lower back strain might clear up in a few weeks. A herniated disc can mean months of therapy, injections or surgery, and pain that changes how you work and live. The value of a back injury claim follows that same range.

## Estimate your back injury claim

The calculator is preset to **moderate**, which fits many back injuries treated with therapy. Choose serious if you had injections or surgery, or severe for permanent limits.

## Common back injuries after a crash

| Injury | Typical treatment | Usual severity range |
|---|---|---|
| Muscle strain or sprain | Rest, medication, physical therapy | Minor to moderate |
| Herniated or bulging disc | Therapy, injections, sometimes surgery | Moderate to serious |
| Compression fracture | Bracing, pain control, sometimes surgery | Serious |
| Nerve involvement (such as sciatica) | Therapy, injections, specialist care | Moderate to serious |

Many back strains improve within weeks. Disc injuries and nerve problems can take months, and some people have long-term pain.

## What insurers look at

- **Imaging.** An MRI showing a disc herniation is strong evidence. Keep in mind that many adults have disc changes without symptoms, and insurers know it.
- **Timing.** Pain and treatment that started right after the crash connect the injury to it.
- **Prior back problems.** Earlier injuries do not end your claim. If the crash made an existing condition worse, that worsening can be claimed.
- **Treatment path.** Therapy, then injections, then surgery shows a real escalation. Each step supports a higher value.

## What can change your number

- **Surgery** usually moves a claim into the serious range
- **Missed work** and any change to the kind of work you can do
- **Future care** your doctor says you will need
- **Fault and your state's rules**, which the calculator applies
- **The at-fault driver's policy limit**, which often caps back injury claims in practice
