This KSEB bill calculator is for domestic consumers of the Kerala State Electricity Board. Enter your monthly units above and you will see the energy charge, the electricity duty and an estimated total, worked out on the current tariff from the Kerala State Electricity Regulatory Commission. It handles the part of the Kerala tariff that surprises most people: the switch to a single rate for the whole bill once you go above 250 units a month.

KSEB reads meters and bills most domestic connections every two months, but the slab table is set per month. So if your bill covers two months, divide its units by two before you enter them, and double the result for the full bill.

How to use the KSEB bill calculator

  1. Take the units from your last bill or from your meter reading.
  2. If the bill covers two months, divide the units by two to get your monthly average.
  3. Enter that number above. The slip shows the estimated bill for one month.
  4. Double it for a two-month bill.

If you are reading the meter in the middle of a cycle, work out your daily average and multiply by 30 for the month.

KSEB slab rates used here

Kerala's domestic tariff works in two stages. Up to 250 units a month, the slabs are telescopic: each block of units is charged at its own rate. Above 250 units, the tariff becomes non-telescopic, which means every unit in the month is charged at the single rate for your total.

That second stage is why a Kerala bill can jump sharply when usage moves from 250 to 251 units. Every unit, including the first, moves to the higher single rate.

Why the 250 unit line matters so much

Most Kerala homes are modest users for much of the year, thanks to a mild climate and good habits. But the hot months from March to May, and the growing number of ACs, push many households above 250 units a month for part of the year. Under telescopic billing, going a little over a slab only makes those few extra units dearer. Under the non-telescopic stage, going over 250 makes the whole month dearer. So the last few days of a hot month are worth watching closely.

A worked example from Kochi

A family in Kakkanad gets a two-month bill for 540 units, an average of 270 a month. The KSEB bill calculator shows that at 270 units, every unit is charged at the single rate for that band. If they had averaged 245 units, the telescopic slabs would have applied and the bill would have been noticeably lower. Over the next cycle they run the AC for fewer hours in the late evening and move ironing to the morning. They finish at 490 units for two months, an average of 245, and their bill falls well beyond the 25 units they saved.

Checking a bill against your meter

Every KSEB bill shows the previous and present readings. When the meter reader visits, note the reading yourself or take a dated photo. If the bill shows more units than your meter, contact your section office before the due date. Bills based on an average reading, for example when the house was locked, are worth checking carefully, because the catch-up in the next cycle can push your monthly average above 250 and into non-telescopic billing. Paying on time through the KSEB app or website also avoids surcharge and gives you a clean record of every reading.

How Kerala's tariff compares

Kerala keeps its lowest slabs cheap for small households, which suits the many homes that use under 150 units a month. The non-telescopic stage above 250 units is designed to make heavier users pay a higher average rate. That is quite different from Tamil Nadu, where the jump happens at 500 units over two months, or Delhi, where subsidy rules shape the bill. If you compare your bill with a relative in another state, compare the units, not just the rupees.

Fixed charges and duty

Alongside the energy charge, a KSEB bill includes a fixed charge that depends on your connection and consumption band, and electricity duty at the state rate. Our estimate includes electricity duty. Fixed charges vary by connection type, so check the line on your own bill. Any fuel surcharge in force is also shown separately. Ways to lower a KSEB bill

  • Stay under 250 units a month if you can. It keeps you on telescopic billing, which is far kinder.
  • Use BLDC fans. They use about half the power of older fans and suit Kerala's humid nights.
  • Set the AC to 25 or 26 degrees with the fan on, and use dry mode on muggy monsoon evenings.
  • Heat water with solar if you have a sunny terrace. Electric geysers add up quickly.
  • Consider rooftop solar. Kerala has strong support for rooftop systems, and the central subsidy reduces the cost. Our solar subsidy calculator shows your share.

Monsoon months and lower bills

Kerala's long monsoon brings cooler days and much less AC use, so June to September bills are often the lowest of the year. That makes these months a good time to judge your home's base load: fridge, lights, fans, pump and appliances. If the monsoon bill is still high, something is running more than it should. A slow leak in the overhead tank that keeps the motor cycling, or an old fridge with a worn seal, are common culprits.

Frequently asked questions

Does the KSEB bill calculator handle two-month bills?

The tariff is set per month. Divide your two-month units by two, enter the result and double the estimate for the full bill.

What is non-telescopic billing in KSEB?

Above 250 units a month, all units are charged at the single rate for your total, instead of each slab at its own rate. It makes the bill jump when you cross 250.

Is the KSEB bill calculator updated?

Yes. We check KSERC orders regularly, and the last check was in [powerbachat_checked].

Why is my bill higher than the calculator?

Fixed charges, any fuel surcharge in force, arrears or meter rent can add to the energy charge and duty that the calculator shows.
