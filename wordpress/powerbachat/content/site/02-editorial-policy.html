This editorial policy explains how PowerBachat sources, checks and corrects the information on the site. People use our calculators to plan budgets and to decide whether to spend serious money on solar, so we hold ourselves to a simple standard: every number should be traceable to a source, every result should show its working, and every mistake should be fixed quickly and openly.

Where our tariff figures come from

Electricity tariffs are set by regulators and published in official notifications. We use those primary documents and nothing else for slab rates, fixed charges and the rules that decide which slab applies.

  • Pakistan: NEPRA tariff determinations and the uniform tariff notified by the federal government, plus K-Electric's notified schedule.
  • India: tariff orders from each state commission, including TNERC for Tamil Nadu, KSERC for Kerala, UPERC for Uttar Pradesh, MERC for Maharashtra, KERC for Karnataka and DERC for Delhi, along with state subsidy notifications.
  • Bangladesh: BERC tariff orders and the published schedules of the distribution companies.

Taxes and duties come from the relevant finance law or the electricity company's own published breakdown. Where a tax depends on details we cannot know about a particular household, the calculator uses the standard domestic case and the page says so.

How often we check

We review every tariff table at least once a month and whenever a regulator announces a change. Fuel and quarterly adjustments in Pakistan change often, so we watch those notifications closely. The date of the last full check is shown at the top of every page, and we only change that date after the check has actually been done.

When a new tariff is notified, we update the data, test the calculators against worked examples from the notification, and then publish. If a change is announced but not yet in force, we say so on the relevant pages and keep showing the rates that apply today.

Solar, battery and IPS prices

Equipment prices are set by the market, not a regulator, so they need a different approach. We collect prices from several established sellers and installers in major cities, look at the typical range rather than the cheapest single listing, and round to sensible figures. Every price table shows the date it was last updated. Prices for the same product can differ by city, brand and season, so we present them as a guide for budgeting and comparing quotes, never as a promise of what you will pay.

How the calculators are tested

Our bill calculators follow the same steps an electricity company follows: they decide the slab system that applies, charge the units in each slab, add fixed charges and adjustments, then apply taxes in the right order. Before any change goes live, we test the calculators against:

  1. Worked examples published in tariff notifications, where available.
  2. Real bills shared by readers and team members, with personal details removed.
  3. Edge cases, such as usage exactly on a slab boundary, the 200 unit protected line in Pakistan and the 500 unit line on TNEB's two-month cycle.

The same rate data feeds every calculator and every table on the site, so a rate updated once is updated everywhere. That removes a common source of errors where one page is updated and another is forgotten.

How we write

We write for the person holding the bill, not for engineers. That means plain words, short sentences and real examples. We explain rules rather than just stating them, because knowing why a slab or a surcharge exists helps people make better decisions. We avoid exaggeration. If solar is not worth it for a particular household, we say so.

We do not accept payment to change what we write about any company, product or installer. If a page ever includes a sponsored placement or a referral link, it will be clearly labelled where it appears.

Corrections

Mistakes happen, especially when tariffs change quickly. When we find an error, or a reader points one out, we:

  • Check it against the primary source the same day where possible.
  • Fix the data or the text, and test the affected calculators again.
  • Add a short note to the page if the error could have affected someone's decision.

If you think something on the site is wrong, please tell us through our contact page. A photo or a copy of the relevant part of your bill or the notification helps us check quickly. Remove your name, address and reference number before sending it.

Estimates, not official bills

However carefully we check, our results are estimates. Electricity companies sometimes add items we cannot see, such as arrears, meter rent, local taxes or one-off adjustments. Your official bill always takes priority, and our disclaimer explains the limits in more detail.
