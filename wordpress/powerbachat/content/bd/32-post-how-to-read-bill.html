Most people glance at the total and the due date and put the paper away, or check the prepaid balance and top up. But a few minutes with the details can catch errors, explain a sudden jump and show where to save. This guide explains how to read an electricity bill in Bangladesh, line by line, whether you get a postpaid bill from BREB, NESCO or another distributor, or use a prepaid meter from DESCO or DPDC.

The account details

At the top of a postpaid bill you will find your name, address, account or consumer number, meter number and tariff category. Check that the tariff category says residential (domestic). If it shows a commercial category for a home, you are being charged the wrong rates. The sanctioned load, in kilowatts, is also shown; it sets your demand charge.

On a prepaid meter, the same details are on your meter card or in your account on the distributor's app or website.

Readings and units

A postpaid bill shows the previous reading, the present reading and the difference, which is your units consumed. This is the most important number to check. Read your own meter when the bill arrives. Your meter should show a number at or slightly above the present reading on the bill. If the bill's reading is higher, you have been over-billed.

On a prepaid meter, note the cumulative kWh reading on the first of each month. The difference between two months is your usage.

Demand charge

The demand charge is a fixed monthly amount based on your sanctioned load. It appears every month regardless of usage. If your sanctioned load is higher than you need, this line is higher than it should be, and you can ask your distributor about reducing it.

VAT

VAT at 5 percent is added to the energy charge and demand charge. It is shown as a separate line.

Arrears, surcharge and the total

Any unpaid amount from earlier bills appears as arrears. If you pay after the due date, a late payment surcharge is added. The bill shows the amount payable by the due date and, often, a higher amount after it. Keep your payment receipts for at least a year in case a payment is not recorded.

Reading a prepaid deduction history

Prepaid customers do not get a monthly bill, but the meter or account shows a history of recharges and deductions. Look for:

  • Recharge amounts and dates, with the amount credited after any deductions.
  • Energy deductions for units used.
  • Demand charge deductions, usually small amounts spread over the month or taken at the start.
  • VAT on both.
  • Any arrears recovery if old dues were on the meter.

A worked example

A BREB customer in Bogura receives a bill for 310 units on a 2 kW load. The energy charge covers the first 75 units at the first slab rate, units 76 to 200 at the second, 201 to 300 at the third and the last 10 at the fourth. The demand charge for 2 kW is added, then 5 percent VAT. Entering 310 in the calculator gives the same energy charge, demand charge and VAT, so the only extra on the bill is a small arrears amount from a late payment last month. Everything checks out.

Seasonal patterns in the history table

Many postpaid bills include a short history of your units for previous months. It is worth a photo each year. You will see usage fall in the cool months and climb from March, peaking in the hot, humid months. If one month stands out far above the pattern without an obvious reason, such as guests or a heatwave, something may be wrong: a faulty pump, a geyser left on or a meter problem. Prepaid customers can build the same picture by noting the kWh reading on the first of each month.

Bills for shared homes

Where two families share one meter, the bill combines their units, which pushes the total into higher slabs. When you read an electricity bill for a shared meter, split the cost by each household's actual usage if you have sub-meters, rather than equally, and consider separate connections where possible. Each household will then pay a lower average price per unit.

A five-minute monthly check

  1. Compare the present reading with your meter.
  2. Check the units against your own estimate.
  3. Confirm the tariff category is residential.
  4. Recalculate the energy charge with the calculator.
  5. Look for unexpected arrears.
  6. Pay before the due date.

When something is wrong

If the reading is wrong, take a dated photo of your meter and visit or call your distributor's office before the due date, with your account number. For a wrong tariff category or sanctioned load, ask for a correction in writing. For a payment not credited, bring the receipt. Problems are much easier to fix in the same month than months later.

Using your bill to save money

Learning to read an electricity bill properly takes one careful sitting, and after that every bill takes a couple of minutes. Once you can read every line, the bill becomes a tool. The history of units shows your seasonal pattern and when you cross 400 units, where the expensive slabs begin. The demand charge tells you whether your sanctioned load is right. And the energy charge tells you what each extra unit really costs you. See the electricity tariff page for every slab, and consider whether an efficient AC, DC fans or a solar system would pay back at your usage.

Frequently asked questions

How do I check my electricity bill in Bangladesh online?

Most distributors offer online bill or account access with your account or meter number. Prepaid customers can usually see deductions in the distributor's app.

What is the demand charge on my bill?

A fixed monthly charge based on your sanctioned load, payable whatever you use.

Why is VAT on my electricity bill?

VAT at 5 percent applies to electricity charges under current rules, and is shown as a separate line.

What should I do if my units look too high?

Compare the bill's reading with your meter. If it is higher, report it to your distributor with a dated photo before the due date.
