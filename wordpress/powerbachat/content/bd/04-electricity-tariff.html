This page sets out the residential electricity tariff in Bangladesh as simply as possible: the price of a unit in each slab, the lifeline rate for very small users, the demand charge and VAT, and what a unit really costs at different levels of use. The tariff is set by the Bangladesh Energy Regulatory Commission (BERC) and applies to every distributor, from DESCO and DPDC in Dhaka to BREB in rural areas. Residential slab rates

[powerbachat_rate_table tariff="bd-berc" plan="standard" caption="Residential electricity tariff (LT-A)"]

The table has two parts. If your home uses 50 units or less in a month, all units are charged at the lifeline rate. If it uses more, the slab table applies from the first unit. In the slab table, each block of units is charged at its own rate, so moving into a higher slab only makes the units in that slab dearer.

The lifeline rate in plain words

The lifeline rate exists so that the poorest households, with a light, a fan and a phone charger, can afford electricity. Every unit is charged at the lowest rate, as long as the month stays at 50 units or less. The moment usage goes to 51 units, the normal slab table applies from the first unit. For homes that sit around 45 to 55 units, the difference is real, and small changes in the last days of the month can keep them on the lifeline rate.

The big step at 400 units

Look at the rates above and below 400 units. The jump is much larger than between the lower slabs. For a family using 380 units a month, the next 50 units cost far more per unit than the 50 before them. That is the most important feature of the electricity tariff in Bangladesh for middle-class households in summer.

Demand charge and VAT

  • Demand charge: a fixed monthly amount per kilowatt of sanctioned load. A typical household with a 2 kW load pays it every month whatever it uses.
  • VAT: 5 percent, added to the energy charge and demand charge.

On prepaid meters, both are deducted from your balance along with the energy charge.

Unit price over the years

Bangladesh's electricity tariff has been revised several times in recent years, as the cost of fuel and imported power rose. The most recent adjustments raised rates across the slabs. When BERC announces a change, it usually takes effect from a stated billing month. We update our tables and calculators as soon as a new order applies, so the numbers here always match the current tariff. If you are comparing an old bill with a new one, check the date to make sure you are comparing the same tariff.

Prepaid and postpaid: same tariff

Whether your meter is prepaid or postpaid, the per unit rates are the same. The difference is when you pay. With prepaid, you pay before you use and see deductions as they happen. With postpaid, you receive a bill after the month. Prepaid customers have sometimes received a small rebate under the rules in force, so check your deduction history to see whether it applies to your account. Our prepaid meter guide explains more.

How to use the tariff to save money

  1. Find your top slab. The units in your highest slab are the most expensive, so they are the best ones to save.
  2. Watch the 400 unit line. In summer, keeping usage under 400 often saves more than any appliance upgrade.
  3. Check your sanctioned load. A load higher than you need raises the demand charge every month.
  4. Use efficient appliances. DC fans, LED lights and an inverter AC lower your steady daily use.

Tariff for shops and other connections

This page covers residential connections. Shops, offices, industries and agricultural connections have their own tariffs with different rates and charges. If you run a small shop from home on a residential meter, the shop's usage is billed at residential slab rates, but it can push the household into the expensive slabs. A separate commercial connection may be worth considering, depending on the load.

Calculating your own bill

To turn the tariff into a bill, use our electricity bill calculator for Bangladesh, or the dedicated DESCO and DPDC calculators. They apply the slabs, demand charge and VAT automatically.

Is solar worth it at today's tariff?

For homes regularly using 300 units or more, especially above 400, rooftop solar can pay back in a reasonable time, because each solar unit replaces an expensive grid unit. It can also provide backup during load-shedding if combined with batteries. See the solar system price and solar panel price pages.

A quick worked example

Take a home using 300 units in a month on a 2 kW load. The first 75 units are charged at the first slab rate, units 76 to 200 at the second, and units 201 to 300 at the third. Add the three amounts, add the demand charge, then add 5 percent VAT. The result is the base bill. Enter 300 in our calculator to see each step with the current rates, and compare it with your own bill or prepaid deduction history.

Doing this once by hand is the quickest way to understand the electricity tariff in Bangladesh, and it makes every later bill easy to check.

Frequently asked questions

What is the electricity tariff in Bangladesh per unit?

It depends on your slab. The table above lists every current rate, with the lifeline rate for homes using 50 units or less.

What is the electricity rate for 300 units?

A 300 unit month is charged across the first slabs. The average price table shows the effective rate including demand charge and VAT.

Is the tariff the same for DESCO, DPDC and BREB?

Yes. All distributors use the same residential tariff set by BERC.

How often does the tariff change?

Only when BERC issues a new order. We update our tables when it does.

What is the lifeline rate?

The lowest rate, applied to every unit when a home uses 50 units or less in a month.

Does the tariff include VAT?

No. VAT at 5 percent is added to the energy charge and demand charge on top of the slab rates.
