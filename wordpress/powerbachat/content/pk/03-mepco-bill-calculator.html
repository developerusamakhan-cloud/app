The MEPCO bill calculator above is built for households supplied by the Multan Electric Power Company, which serves the largest area of any distribution company in Punjab. That includes Multan, Bahawalpur, Rahim Yar Khan, Dera Ghazi Khan, Muzaffargarh, Vehari, Khanewal, Sahiwal, Lodhran and Rajanpur. Enter your units and it shows the slab charges, GST, electricity duty and an estimated total in one place.

South Punjab summers are long and brutal. From May to September, ceiling fans run day and night, coolers and ACs work through the afternoon, and tube wells and water pumps are busy in rural homes. That is why MEPCO bills in July can be three or four times the January bill, and why checking your units halfway through the month is one of the most useful habits a family in Multan can build.

Step by step: estimating your MEPCO bill

  1. Take the previous reading from your last bill. It is printed in the meter reading box near the top.
  2. Read the meter now. Subtract the old reading to get your units so far.
  3. If you are part way through the month, scale it up to 30 days.
  4. Enter the result in the calculator. Turn on "Protected consumer" only if every one of your last six months was at 200 units or below.

The big number on the slip is your estimated payable amount before the monthly fuel adjustment and any arrears. Open the breakdown if you want to see each slab line.

Add a 1.5 ton inverter AC used for eight hours a night and the same home lands near 500 units. Joint family houses in Bahawalpur or Rahim Yar Khan with two or three ACs regularly pass 1,000 units in June and July.

The protected status trap in summer

Many MEPCO customers are protected consumers through winter and spring, then lose that status the moment the first hot month pushes them past 200 units. Because the status depends on the last six months, one summer month above 200 units means paying the higher rates until the following winter has had time to rebuild the history. If your home is close to the line, the first hot weeks of May are when a little care pays off the most. We explain the rule in detail in our protected consumer guide.

A worked example from Bahawalpur

Consider a family of six in Model Town, Bahawalpur. In April their bill showed 190 units, so they were still protected. By May 18 the meter shows 170 units used in the new cycle, with two weeks still to go. At that pace the month ends near 300 units.

Entering 300 in the MEPCO bill calculator with protected status switched off shows the bill they are heading for. Then they try 200 units with protected status on. The difference is large enough to change behaviour: running the AC only in the main bedroom for the last two weeks and leaning on the cooler during the day. Even if they cannot hold the line all summer, knowing the cost of each choice lets the family decide rather than guess.

Load-shedding and backup in MEPCO areas

Many towns and villages in the MEPCO region still see regular load-shedding, especially on feeders with high line losses. A UPS or inverter with batteries keeps fans and lights running, but it also adds to the bill, because the batteries recharge from the grid when power returns. A typical lead-acid setup can add 20 to 40 units a month on its own. If you are buying a backup system, our battery backup calculator shows how long a battery bank will last for your fans and lights, and our comparison of lithium and tubular batteries explains which one wastes less.

Coolers versus ACs in south Punjab

Air coolers are still the backbone of summer in much of south Punjab, and for good reason. A large room cooler uses roughly 150 to 250 watts, while a 1.5 ton inverter AC uses around 800 to 1,400 watts depending on the heat outside and the thermostat. Coolers work well in the dry heat of May and early June. Once humidity rises with the monsoon, they lose much of their effect and people switch to ACs. Planning that switch, and keeping cooler pads clean and wet, can keep the bill lower for weeks.

Charges the calculator leaves out

  • Fuel price adjustment (FPA): a per-unit addition or refund that changes monthly. See our FPA guide.
  • Arrears and surcharges: unpaid amounts and late payment surcharge.
  • Income tax: for higher bills where the consumer is not an active taxpayer.
  • Agricultural and commercial tariffs: tube well and shop connections use different tariffs. This calculator is for homes only.

Lowering your MEPCO bill without suffering

  • Run the AC at 26 degrees with a fan. It is the single biggest saving for most homes.
  • Shade the outdoor AC unit. In Multan's afternoon sun the outdoor unit works much harder. A simple shade that does not block airflow helps.
  • Check the water motor. A worn motor or a faulty float switch can waste dozens of units a month.
  • Look at solar seriously. South Punjab has some of the best sunshine in the country. Our solar calculator shows how quickly a system pays back at your usage.

Frequently asked questions

Does the MEPCO bill calculator include GST?

Yes. It adds 18 percent GST and electricity duty to the energy charge. It does not include the monthly fuel adjustment or arrears, so your printed bill may differ by those amounts.

Is MEPCO more expensive than LESCO or IESCO?

The base rates are identical because all companies use the uniform national tariff. Bills differ only through the monthly adjustments and how many units each home uses.

How do I check my MEPCO bill online?

Use the 14-digit reference number printed on your paper bill. When you have the online copy, enter its units here to confirm the energy charge is right.

What is the cheapest way to cool a home in Multan?

Air coolers in the dry months and an inverter AC set to 26 degrees once the humidity arrives. Avoid running old non-inverter ACs at low temperatures for long hours.
