A $50,000 settlement usually reflects a moderate injury: several months of treatment, some missed work and real disruption to daily life. At this size, fees, costs and liens can take close to half.

The quick answer: with a one third attorney fee and nothing else taken out, you would keep about **$33,335**. Real settlements usually have case costs and medical liens too. The calculator below is already set to $50,000. Change the fee, costs and liens to match your case.

## A realistic $50,000 example

Here is a typical breakdown with a one third (33.33%) fee taken from the full settlement, $2,000 in case costs, and a $6,000 medical lien that the lawyer negotiated down by 25%.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Settlement | $50,000 |
| Attorney fee (one third (33.33%)) | minus $16,665 |
| Case costs | minus $2,000 |
| Medical lien after reduction | minus $4,500 |
| **You keep** | **$26,835** |

If the fee were calculated **after** costs instead, you would keep about **$27,502**, a difference of $667.

## Where the money goes on a $50,000 settlement

On most $50,000 settlements the attorney fee is the biggest deduction, followed by medical liens. Liens are also the easiest to shrink: hospitals and health plans often accept a reduced amount if your lawyer asks, especially when the bills are large compared with the settlement.

## How to keep more of your $50,000 settlement

- Ask whether your fee is calculated before or after costs.
- Ask your lawyer to negotiate every medical lien.
- Request a written settlement statement before you sign the release.
- Check whether your own MedPay coverage already paid some bills.
