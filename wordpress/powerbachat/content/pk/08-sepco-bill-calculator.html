The SEPCO bill calculator above is for households supplied by the Sukkur Electric Power Company, which serves upper and central Sindh, including Sukkur, Larkana, Jacobabad, Shikarpur, Khairpur, Ghotki, Kashmore, Kandhkot, Dadu, Naushahro Feroze and Shaheed Benazirabad (Nawabshah). Enter the units from your meter and it shows each slab charge, the taxes and an estimated total, so you know the number before the paper bill arrives.

This is the hottest part of Pakistan. Jacobabad regularly records some of the highest temperatures on earth, and Sukkur and Larkana are not far behind. For families here, cooling is not a luxury but a health need, especially for children and older relatives. That makes it all the more important to spend each unit wisely.

How to estimate your SEPCO bill

  1. Take the present reading from your last bill.
  2. Read the meter today and subtract to find units used so far.
  3. If the cycle is not over, divide by the days passed and multiply by 30.
  4. Enter that figure. Turn on "Protected consumer" only if all of your last six bills were at or under 200 units.

The big number on the slip is your estimated bill. The breakdown shows which slab each unit falls into and how much tax is added.

Notice how the average price per unit climbs as usage rises. That happens because each extra slab is more expensive than the one before, so heavy users pay more for every unit, not just for the extra ones. In the hottest months, even a modest home with a cooler, two fans, a fridge and lights can reach 250 to 300 units. Add an AC for the night and 450 to 600 units is common. If you are close to 200 units in April, May is the month to watch most carefully.

Staying cool in extreme heat without a shock bill

When it is 48 degrees outside, the advice to "set the AC to 26" can sound like a joke. It still holds, though. An AC working against extreme heat runs almost non-stop, so every degree on the thermostat matters more here than in milder cities. A few things help a lot:

  • Cool one room well rather than several rooms badly. Bring the family into one cooled room for the hottest afternoon hours.
  • Block the sun before it enters. Reflective film or thick curtains on west-facing windows cut heat gain sharply.
  • Whitewash or paint the roof white. A light-coloured roof can lower the temperature of the room below by several degrees.
  • Shade the outdoor unit without blocking its airflow. In direct sun at these temperatures it struggles badly.
  • Use a cooler in the dry heat of May and early June, when it works best, and save the AC for humid nights.

Keeping older relatives and children safe for less

Heatstroke is a real danger in upper Sindh, and the people most at risk are the very young, the elderly and anyone with heart or kidney problems. Protecting them does not require cooling the whole house. A single small inverter AC in one room, used during the hottest hours, uses far fewer units than two old window units running all day. Wet cloths, a pedestal fan near a bowl of ice, plenty of water with a pinch of salt and sugar, and keeping the most vulnerable person out of the top floor all help. Spend your units where they protect health first, then on comfort.

Load-shedding and backup costs

Many SEPCO feeders still see long daily outages. A UPS keeps fans running, which can be essential in this heat, but every recharge comes from your meter. Old batteries and inefficient inverters waste a lot of it. A worked example from Larkana

A family in Larkana reads 180 units used on May 16. Their pace points to around 340 units for the month. The SEPCO bill calculator shows the bill at 340 units, non-protected, and then at 200 units, protected. They cannot realistically get down to 200 in May, so they focus on staying in the 301 to 400 slab rather than drifting into the next one in June. Knowing which battle to fight is half of the saving. In October, when the heat breaks, they will start the six clean months that rebuild protected status.

Disputing a wrong SEPCO reading

If a bill shows more units than your meter, do not wait. On the day the bill arrives, photograph the meter with the date visible, then take the photo, the bill and your CNIC to your subdivision office and ask for a reading correction. Keep a copy of the complaint. Bills based on an estimated reading deserve extra attention, because the next real reading can land a large catch-up in a single month and push you into the top slabs.

Charges the estimate does not cover

  • Income tax on larger bills for non-filers.
  • Agricultural tube well connections, which use a separate tariff.

Frequently asked questions

Does the SEPCO bill calculator work for Jacobabad and Nawabshah?

Yes. Every SEPCO district uses the same national tariff, so the calculator applies to all household connections.

Are SEPCO rates higher because of line losses?

No. Base rates are the same as everywhere else. Areas with high losses may face more load-shedding, but your per unit price is set by the national tariff.

Is solar worth it in upper Sindh?

Usually yes. Sunshine is strong and long, and cooling needs are high exactly when panels produce the most. Our solar calculator shows a payback estimate for your units.

How do I check my SEPCO bill online?

Enter the 14-digit reference number from your paper bill on the official online bill service. Then put its units into this calculator to check the energy charge.
