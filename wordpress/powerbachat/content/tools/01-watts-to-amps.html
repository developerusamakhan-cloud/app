To convert watts to amps, divide the power in watts by the voltage. A 1,000 watt microwave on a 120 volt outlet draws about 8.3 amps, while the same microwave on a 230 volt supply draws about 4.3 amps. The calculator above does this for any wattage and voltage, and also handles AC motors and three phase supplies, where the power factor matters.

Knowing the current helps you avoid tripped breakers, choose the right extension lead and check whether a generator or inverter can run what you plan to plug in.

The watts to amps formula

The formula depends on the kind of circuit:

  • DC: amps = watts ÷ volts
  • AC single phase: amps = watts ÷ (volts × power factor)
  • AC three phase: amps = watts ÷ (√3 × volts × power factor), using the line to line voltage

The power factor is 1 for heaters, kettles, irons and incandescent bulbs, so for most household appliances you can simply divide watts by volts. Motors, compressors and some power supplies have a power factor below 1, often between 0.8 and 0.95, which means they draw a little more current than the simple formula suggests. If the nameplate gives a power factor, enter it in the calculator.

Worked examples

  • 1,500 watts to amps at 120 V: 1,500 ÷ 120 = 12.5 amps. This is a typical space heater or hair dryer on the highest setting.
  • 2,000 watts to amps at 230 V: 2,000 ÷ 230 = 8.7 amps. A 2 kW kettle in the UK or Pakistan draws this much.
  • 600 watts at 12 V DC: 600 ÷ 12 = 50 amps. Low voltage systems need thick cables because the current is so high.
  • 10,000 watts on a 400 V three phase supply, power factor 0.9: 10,000 ÷ (1.732 × 400 × 0.9) = 16 amps per line.

How many watts can a circuit take?

Turn the formula around and you can see how much a circuit can safely supply. A 15 amp circuit at 120 volts can deliver 1,800 watts, and a 20 amp circuit 2,400 watts. For loads that run for three hours or more, such as a heater left on all evening, electricians in North America size circuits so the load uses no more than 80 percent of the breaker rating, which is 1,440 watts on a 15 amp circuit.

On 230 volt supplies the same current carries about twice the power. A UK plug fused at 13 amps can supply up to about 3,000 watts, which is why kettles there boil water faster than in the United States.

Frequently asked questions

How many amps is 1000 watts?

1,000 watts is about 8.3 amps at 120 volts, 4.3 amps at 230 volts and 4.2 amps at 240 volts. On a 12 volt DC system the same power needs about 83 amps.

How many amps is 1500 watts?

1,500 watts is 12.5 amps at 120 volts and about 6.5 amps at 230 volts. That is why a 1,500 watt heater uses most of a 15 amp household circuit in the United States.

Can I convert watts to amps without knowing the voltage?

No. Amps depend on both the power and the voltage, so the same wattage draws different currents in different countries. Check the voltage on the appliance label or use 120 V for North America and 230 V for most other countries.

Why do motors draw more amps than the formula shows?

Motors have a power factor below 1, so part of the current does no useful work, and they also draw a short surge when they start. Enter the power factor from the nameplate for the running current, and allow extra headroom for the starting surge when choosing a generator or inverter.
