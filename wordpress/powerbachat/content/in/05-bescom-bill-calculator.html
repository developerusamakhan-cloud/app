Karnataka is unusual because many households pay little or nothing for electricity under the Gruha Jyothi scheme. If you are covered, the bill you see from BESCOM may be zero or a small amount. This calculator still matters, because it shows what your usage would cost without the scheme and what you pay once you go above your free entitlement.

How to use the BESCOM bill calculator

  1. Take the previous reading from your last bill or the BESCOM app.
  2. Read the meter now and subtract to find units used.
  3. Scale up to a full month if needed.
  4. Enter the result. The slip shows the bill on the regular tariff.

BESCOM domestic rates in the calculator

Karnataka's domestic tariff is simple compared with most states: one rate for the first 100 units and a higher rate above that. A fixed charge per kilowatt of sanctioned load and electricity tax are added.

Because there are only two slabs, the maths is easy to follow. The first 100 units are cheap, and every unit above that costs the same higher rate, with tax on top. That makes the per unit average rise gradually rather than in sudden jumps. Our estimate assumes a 2 kW sanctioned load for the fixed charge. Larger homes with higher loads pay more in fixed charges each month.

How Gruha Jyothi changes your bill

Under Gruha Jyothi, eligible domestic households get free electricity up to a monthly limit. The limit is not a flat 200 units for everyone. It is based on your household's average monthly use over a reference period, plus a small margin, and capped at 200 units. If your average was 120 units, for example, your free entitlement is a little above 120, not 200.

If you use less than your entitlement, the energy charge is waived. If you use more, you pay for the units above it at the regular tariff. And if you go above 200 units in a month, the scheme's limits mean that month is billed differently, so check your bill closely. To estimate your bill as a scheme beneficiary, subtract your entitlement from your units and enter the remainder here as a rough guide to the extra you will pay.

The scheme rewards steady, careful use. A household that keeps its usage stable year after year keeps a sensible entitlement, while a sudden spike can mean paying for many units in that month. Our guide to Gruha Jyothi free electricity explains eligibility and the entitlement rule in more detail.

A worked example from Bengaluru

A family in Jayanagar has a Gruha Jyothi entitlement of about 140 units, based on their past average. In March they use 180 units because of a new AC. The BESCOM bill calculator shows what 180 units would cost on the regular tariff, and entering the 40 extra units gives a rough idea of what they will pay. They decide to keep the AC to the hottest hours, and in April they come in at 150 units, paying for only about ten units above their limit.

Bengaluru's climate and your bill

Bengaluru's mild weather keeps bills lower than in most Indian cities. Many homes manage without an AC for most of the year, and fans plus good ventilation are enough. The biggest loads tend to be geysers in the cool months, water pumps for overhead tanks and, increasingly, home offices with computers running all day. A solar water heater is popular in the city for good reason: it removes the geyser load almost entirely.

Lowering a BESCOM bill

  • Know your Gruha Jyothi entitlement. It is shown on your bill. Staying under it keeps the energy charge at zero.
  • Use a solar water heater if you have a terrace. It is one of the best investments in Bengaluru.
  • Check the water pump. Overhead tank pumps that run too long are a common hidden load.
  • Consider rooftop solar if you use well above your entitlement. Try the PM Surya Ghar calculator.

Apartments, sub-meters and common areas

Many Bengaluru families live in apartment complexes. If your flat has its own BESCOM meter, this calculator applies directly. Common area loads such as lifts, pumps, corridor lights and the clubhouse are usually on a separate connection paid through maintenance charges. If your flat is on a sub-meter run by the association, your charges depend on how the association divides its own bill, which may not follow the domestic slabs. Ask for the per-unit rate they use, then multiply it by your units.

Reading the meter and catching mistakes

BESCOM meters are read monthly, and the bill shows the previous and present readings. Take a dated photo of your meter when the reader visits. If the units on the bill are higher, raise a complaint on the BESCOM helpline or app, or at your sub-division, before the due date. For Gruha Jyothi beneficiaries, an incorrect reading can also push you over your free limit, so it is worth checking every month.

What the estimate leaves out

  • The Gruha Jyothi waiver itself, which depends on your entitlement.
  • Any fuel or power purchase adjustment in force.
  • Arrears, interest on late payment and meter rent.

Frequently asked questions

Does the BESCOM bill calculator include Gruha Jyothi?

It shows the regular tariff. Subtract your entitlement from your units to estimate what you will pay above the free limit.

Is Gruha Jyothi 200 free units for everyone?

No. The free limit is based on your past average use plus a small margin, capped at 200 units a month.

Is the BESCOM bill calculator valid for HESCOM or MESCOM areas?

The tariff structure is similar across Karnataka's companies, but fixed charges and details can differ. Use it as a guide outside BESCOM areas.

What happens if I use more than 200 units under Gruha Jyothi?

The scheme is meant for households using up to 200 units a month. In a month where you exceed that, check your bill carefully, because the waiver may not apply in the usual way. Keeping usage under both your entitlement and 200 units is the safest approach.
