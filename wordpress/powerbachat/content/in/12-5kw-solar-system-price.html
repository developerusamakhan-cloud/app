A 5 kW rooftop system suits larger homes: bungalows with two or three ACs, joint families, or anyone using 500 units or more a month. The 5kW solar system price in India is higher than the popular 3 kW size, and the central subsidy does not grow beyond 3 kW, so it is worth understanding exactly what you get for the extra money. This page covers the cost, the subsidy, what 5 kW produces and runs, and when it makes sense.

Price for a 5 kW system

[powerbachat_subsidy_table sizes="3,5,7,10"]

When you compare the 5kW solar system price in India across vendors, check that every quote uses the same panel type, inverter class and structure, or the comparison is meaningless. For a residential DCR system, the central subsidy stays at ₹78,000, the same as for 3 kW. So the extra 2 kW is paid in full by you. That does not make 5 kW a bad choice, but it does mean the decision should rest on your usage and your state's tariff rather than on the subsidy.

If you do not need the subsidy, for example because you prefer imported high-efficiency panels, non-DCR systems are often cheaper per kW in the open market. Compare both routes using our solar panel price page.

What a 5 kW system includes

  • Panels: about nine panels of around 550 to 580 watts.
  • Inverter: a 5 kW on-grid inverter, three-phase for many larger homes, or a hybrid inverter if you want batteries.
  • Structure: a raised galvanised frame for two or three rows of panels.
  • Roof space: roughly 25 to 35 square metres free of shade.
  • Protection and net meter: breakers, surge protection, earthing and a bi-directional meter.

Output and what it can run

[powerbachat_solar_table country="in" sizes="3,5,7"]

A 5 kW system typically produces around 580 units a month in most of India. On a sunny day it can run two 1.5 ton inverter ACs, a fridge, a water pump, a washing machine and all the fans and lights. In the evening and at night, the house draws from the grid as usual, unless you add batteries.

When 5 kW makes sense

  • Your use is above 450 units a month for much of the year.
  • Your state's upper slabs are expensive. In Maharashtra, for example, units above 300 cost far more than the first 100, so solar that removes them pays back quickly. See our Mahadiscom bill calculator.
  • You use a lot of power during the day, such as a home office, daytime AC or a borewell pump.
  • Your state's net metering is favourable for surplus units.

When 3 kW may be enough

If your use is around 300 to 400 units, or most of it is in the evening, a 3 kW system with the full subsidy often gives better value. The extra 2 kW would mostly export at midday. Our 3kW solar system price page compares the two.

Payback for a 5 kW system

Take a family in Pune using 550 units a month, much of it in the expensive upper slabs. A 5 kW system covers most of that. Because the units it removes are the costliest on their bill, the monthly saving is large, and the payback on the full cost after subsidy can still be only a few years. In a state with low tariffs, the same system would take noticeably longer to pay back. The solar power price calculator runs this for your own units.

On-grid, hybrid or off-grid?

On-grid is the cheapest, qualifies for the subsidy with net metering, and cuts your bill most efficiently, but shuts down in a power cut.

Hybrid adds batteries and a hybrid inverter. It keeps essentials running during outages, at a much higher cost.

Off-grid suits remote homes without reliable supply. It needs a large battery bank and is rarely the best choice in cities.

For battery sizing, use the inverter battery backup calculator.

Single-phase or three-phase?

Many homes that use enough power to need 5 kW of solar already have a three-phase connection. A three-phase inverter spreads the solar output across all three phases, which suits these homes and some discoms prefer or require it at this size. If you are on single phase, ask your discom and vendor whether a single-phase 5 kW inverter is allowed on your connection, or whether you need to upgrade.

Planning for an electric vehicle

More families are buying electric scooters and cars, and charging them at home adds a noticeable number of units each month. A car driven a moderate distance every day can add a few hundred units a month. If an EV is in your plans, sizing for 5 kW now, or choosing an inverter with room to add panels, can save a second installation later. Charging during the day, when solar output is high, makes the most of your system.

Looking after a 5 kW system

A larger system rewards a little care. Clean the panels regularly in dusty months, check the inverter's display or app for error messages, and have the connections and earthing checked once a year. Keep a simple record of monthly generation from the inverter. A sudden drop usually means dirty panels, new shade or a fault, and catching it early protects your savings. Over the system's life, these habits make the 5kW solar system price in India a far better investment.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing 5 kW for the subsidy, when the subsidy is the same as for 3 kW.
  • Ignoring how your state values exported units.
  • Skimping on the inverter, the part most likely to fail.
  • Forgetting cleaning in dusty areas.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 5kW solar system price in India?

See the 5 kW row in the table above for the installed cost, the ₹78,000 subsidy and your share. Hybrid systems with batteries cost more.

Do I get more subsidy for 5 kW than 3 kW?

No. The central residential subsidy is capped at ₹78,000, reached at 3 kW.

How many units does 5 kW produce?

Around 580 units a month on average in most of India.

Can a 5 kW system run two ACs?

Yes, during sunny hours, along with normal household loads.

How much roof space does 5 kW need?

Roughly 25 to 35 square metres of shade-free roof, depending on panel size and layout. Raised structures let you keep using the terrace underneath.

Should I add batteries to a 5 kW system?

Only if you face regular power cuts. Batteries add a lot of cost and do not increase your bill savings much where net metering is available.
