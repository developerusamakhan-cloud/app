This electricity bill calculator works for every household in Bangladesh, whichever company supplies you. DESCO, DPDC, BREB through the Palli Bidyut Samities, NESCO, WZPDCO and BPDB all bill homes on the same residential tariff set by the Bangladesh Energy Regulatory Commission. Choose your distributor above, enter your units for the month, and you will see each slab, the demand charge, VAT and an estimated total.

We built it because most people only see their usage as a falling balance or a printed total, without any sense of how the number is made. Seeing the slabs laid out turns that into something you can manage. It works for both postpaid bills and prepaid meters. For a prepaid meter, the total tells you roughly how much your month's use will deduct from your balance, which helps you plan recharges.

How to use the calculator

  1. Find your units. On a postpaid bill, they are printed as the difference between the previous and present readings. On a prepaid meter, check the monthly usage shown on the meter or in your account history.
  2. If you are part way through the month, divide the units so far by the days passed and multiply by 30.
  3. Choose your distributor and enter the units.
  4. Open the breakdown to see which slab each unit falls into.

The tariff behind the calculator

Bangladesh's residential tariff has a lifeline rate for very small users and a set of rising slabs for everyone else:

If you use 50 units or less in a month, every unit is charged at the lifeline rate. Above 50 units, the normal slab table applies from the first unit, with each block charged at its own rate. A demand charge based on your sanctioned load is added every month, and VAT at 5 percent is applied on top.

The lifeline rule in practice

The lifeline rate is designed for the smallest households, such as a single room with a light, a fan and a phone charger. If your month stays at 50 units or less, every unit is charged at the lowest rate. Go to 51 units and the normal slab table applies from the first unit, so the bill rises by more than the price of one unit. For homes that hover around 50 units, switching off a second light or charging phones less often in the last few days can keep them on the lifeline rate. For everyone else, the 400 unit line matters far more.

The seasonal pattern

Most Bangladeshi homes use the least electricity from December to February, when fans slow down and ACs stay off. From March, usage climbs steadily, and May to September are the heaviest months, with heat and humidity keeping fans and ACs running. Monsoon months can be as heavy as summer, because humidity makes rooms uncomfortable even when temperatures drop. Use the twelve-month history on your bill to see your own pattern, and plan the hot months with the 400 unit line in mind.

What each reading usually means

  • Under 50 units: a very small home with a few lights, a fan and a phone charger. Lifeline rate.
  • 100 to 200 units: a typical flat with fans, lights, a fridge and a TV.
  • 200 to 400 units: a family home with an AC used on hot nights, a water pump and a washing machine.
  • Above 400 units: several ACs, a geyser in winter, or a home business. The expensive slabs start here.

The demand charge explained

Many people never notice this line, because it is small compared with the energy charge in summer. In winter, when usage is low, it becomes a larger share of the bill. The demand charge is a fixed monthly amount based on your sanctioned load in kilowatts. It is charged whether you use one unit or a thousand. Our estimate assumes a typical 2 kW household load. If your sanctioned load is higher, your demand charge is higher. Check the figure on your bill or meter account, and if it is far above what you actually use, ask your distributor about reducing it.

VAT and other lines

VAT at 5 percent is added to the energy and demand charges. On a prepaid meter it is deducted along with your usage. Postpaid bills may also show arrears or a late payment surcharge. Our guide on how to read an electricity bill in Bangladesh explains each line.

A worked example

A family in Mohammadpur uses about 220 units a month in winter. In June, their meter shows 300 units used by the 20th. At that pace they will finish near 450, crossing the 400 line. The electricity bill calculator shows the estimate at 450 and at 395 units. The difference comes mostly from the units above 400. They run the AC only in the bedroom at night for the rest of the month and finish at 396 units, keeping all their units in the cheaper slabs.

The same approach works in any month: read the meter, check the pace and adjust before the line, not after it.

Lowering your bill

  • Stay under 400 units in summer if you can. It is the single biggest saving.
  • Use AC dry mode on humid evenings and keep cooling at 25 or 26 degrees.
  • Replace old ceiling fans with efficient DC fans.
  • Check your IPS. An old IPS battery wastes units every time it recharges. See the IPS calculator.
  • Consider rooftop solar if you have a roof. See the solar system price in Bangladesh.

Frequently asked questions

Does this electricity bill calculator work for BREB?

Yes. Palli Bidyut consumers under BREB use the same residential tariff as city distributors.

Does it work for prepaid meters?

Yes. The tariff is the same. The total shows roughly how much a month's use will deduct from your balance.

What is the lifeline rate?

Homes using 50 units or less in a month pay a low lifeline rate on every unit.

Why is my bill higher than the calculator?

Your sanctioned load may be higher than the 2 kW we assume, or the bill may include arrears or a late payment surcharge. Check those lines first.
