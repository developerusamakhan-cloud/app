A $50,000 settlement sounds like $50,000. It almost never is. Your lawyer's fee comes out first, then case costs, then anyone who paid your medical bills and wants to be paid back. This calculator shows you the real number that lands in your bank account.

[cf_tool name="settlement-take-home"]

## How the take-home math works

There are only four pieces, but the order they come out in matters.

- **Attorney fee.** Most injury lawyers work on contingency, meaning they get a percentage of what you recover instead of an hourly rate. One third (33.33%) is the most common. Many agreements go up to 40% if a lawsuit is filed or the case goes to trial.
- **Case costs.** Money the lawyer spent to build your case: filing fees, medical records, police reports, expert opinions, depositions. These are usually repaid from the settlement on top of the fee.
- **Medical liens.** If your health insurer, Medicare, Medicaid or a hospital paid for treatment, they may have a legal right to be repaid from your settlement. Good lawyers often negotiate these down.
- **What is left** is yours.

### Fee before costs vs fee after costs

This is the detail most people miss. Your fee agreement says whether the percentage is taken from the **full settlement** or from what is **left after costs**.

- **From the full settlement:** fee = settlement x fee %, then costs and liens come out.
- **After costs:** fee = (settlement minus costs) x fee %, which means a smaller fee.

On a $100,000 settlement with $8,000 in costs and a one third fee, the difference is about $2,666. The calculator shows both versions so you can see what your agreement is worth.

## A worked example: $50,000 settlement

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Settlement | $50,000 |
| Attorney fee (33.33% of full amount) | minus $16,665 |
| Case costs | minus $2,000 |
| Medical lien ($6,000, reduced 25%) | minus $4,500 |
| **You keep** | **$26,835** |

That is about 54% of the headline number. It is a normal result, and it is exactly why it helps to know it before you accept an offer.

## What can change your number

- **Negotiating the fee.** Fees are not set by law in most states. Some lawyers will agree to a lower percentage if the case settles early.
- **Lien reductions.** Hospitals and health plans often accept less than the full lien, especially when the settlement is small compared with the bills.
- **Medical payments coverage.** If your own policy has MedPay, it may have paid some bills already without a lien.
- **Taxes.** Money for physical injuries is generally not taxable under federal law, but some parts, like interest or punitive damages, can be. Ask a tax professional if your settlement is large.

## What each deduction usually looks like

| Deduction | Typical size | Can it be reduced? |
|---|---|---|
| Attorney fee | 33% to 40% | Sometimes, if negotiated before signing |
| Case costs | A few hundred dollars to tens of thousands | Ask for estimates and itemized bills |
| Health insurance lien | Varies | Often negotiable |
| Medicare or Medicaid lien | Varies | Can sometimes be reduced through formal processes |
| Hospital or provider lien | Varies | Often negotiable |

## Take-home without a lawyer

If you settle on your own, there is no attorney fee, so the math is simpler: settlement minus any liens you must repay. That does not always mean you keep more. Represented claimants often settle for higher amounts, and a lawyer usually negotiates liens as part of the job. Compare both scenarios with the calculator by setting the fee to 0% and adjusting the settlement amount to what you think you could get alone.

## Structured settlements

Some larger settlements pay part of the money over time instead of in one check. A structure can provide steady income and may have tax advantages, but it is harder to change later. This calculator assumes a single payment. If a structure is offered, ask for the present value of the payments and compare.

## Reading a settlement statement

When your case settles, your lawyer should give you a settlement statement to review and sign before any money is paid out. A simple one looks like this:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross settlement | $75,000 |
| Attorney fee (33.33%) | minus $24,998 |
| Costs: medical records | minus $420 |
| Costs: filing and service | minus $450 |
| Costs: expert report | minus $2,500 |
| Health plan lien (reduced from $9,000) | minus $6,300 |
| **Net to client** | **$40,332** |

Check that the fee percentage matches your agreement, that each cost has a receipt, and that every lien shows its original and reduced amount. Ask about anything you do not recognize.

## Tax basics

Under federal law, money received for physical injuries or physical sickness is generally not taxable, including the part for pain and suffering tied to that injury. Some parts can be taxable: interest on a judgment, punitive damages, and in some cases compensation for emotional distress without a physical injury. If you deducted medical expenses on a past tax return and are now being reimbursed for them, that part may also be taxable. For a large settlement, talk to a tax professional before the money arrives.

## Before you sign

Ask your lawyer for a written **settlement statement** that lists every deduction. You are entitled to understand where every dollar goes. If a number in that statement looks different from what this calculator shows, ask why. There is often a good reason, but you deserve to hear it.
