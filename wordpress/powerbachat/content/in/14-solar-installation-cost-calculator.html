Treat these figures as a fair middle of the market, not a fixed price list. City, roof access and brand choices all move a real quote up or down a little. Cost per kilowatt falls as systems get larger, because fixed costs such as labour, transport, protection gear and paperwork are spread over more panels. A 10 kW system usually costs noticeably less per kW than a 2 kW one, even though it gets no more subsidy than a 3 kW system.

Where the money goes

  • Panels: a large share, but no longer most of the cost. DCR panels cost more per watt than non-DCR.
  • Inverter: the second biggest item and the part most likely to need replacing in the system's life. Brand and service matter.
  • Mounting structure: galvanised steel frames, more expensive when raised high to keep the terrace usable.
  • Balance of system: DC and AC cables, combiner box, breakers, surge protection and earthing.
  • Labour and transport: fitting, cable runs and sometimes civil work for the structure.
  • Net metering: application fees, the bi-directional meter and testing, which vary by discom.

Extras that are easy to miss

When quotes differ widely, these items usually explain it. Ask whether each one is included:

  • A raised structure (elevated by a couple of metres) versus a low one.
  • Walkways or railings for safe cleaning on large roofs.
  • Lightning arrestor, where the roof is exposed.
  • Long cable runs if the inverter is far from the panels.
  • Net meter cost and discom fees.
  • Annual maintenance visits after the first year.

Using the solar installation cost calculator

The tool above sizes a system from your monthly units, assuming typical Indian sunshine and about 22 percent losses from heat, dust and wiring. It then multiplies the size by the current installed cost range per kilowatt and shows the central subsidy. Use the average of your last twelve bills for the best result. If your bill covers two months, halve the units first.

A worked example

A family in Hyderabad averages 260 units a month. The solar installation cost calculator suggests about 2.5 kW. They round up to 3 kW to capture the full subsidy and allow for a future AC. Three quotes arrive: two within the 3 kW range in the table, and one well below it. The cheap quote turns out to use non-DCR panels and a low structure, which would not qualify for the subsidy and would make the terrace unusable. Knowing the typical cost helped them spot the problem straight away.

On-grid versus hybrid costs

An on-grid system is the cheapest and the one the subsidy is designed for. A hybrid system adds a hybrid inverter and batteries so the house keeps running during outages. The batteries alone can add a large amount to the price, and they need replacing far sooner than panels. Size them for real outage hours with our inverter battery backup calculator, rather than trying to store a whole day's output.

Paying for it

You pay the vendor first, and the central subsidy is credited to your bank account after commissioning. If paying upfront is hard, banks have offered concessional loans for small residential systems under PM Surya Ghar. Spread over a few years, the loan payment can be close to the monthly saving on the bill, which makes solar affordable without a large lump sum. Our PM Surya Ghar calculator shows the saving side of that equation.

How to compare quotes

  1. Make sure every quote is for the same size, panel type (DCR or not) and inverter class.
  2. Check the structure height and steel gauge.
  3. Compare warranties: panels, inverter and workmanship.
  4. Confirm registration on the national portal for your discom.
  5. Ask who handles net metering paperwork and what it costs.

Roof types and what they add

Most Indian homes have a flat RCC roof, which is the simplest and cheapest to install on. Sloping roofs, tin sheds and roofs with lots of obstructions need custom structures that cost more. Very old roofs may need repair before a structure goes up, because the frame is anchored for decades. If your terrace is used for drying clothes or as a family space, a raised structure keeps it usable but adds to the steel cost. Ask the installer to visit, check shade at different times of day, and explain how the structure will be anchored and waterproofed.

Running costs after installation

Solar's running costs are low but not zero. Regular panel cleaning, especially in dusty cities, keeps output up. An inverter replacement is likely once in the panels' life. An annual check of connections and earthing keeps the system safe. Include these in your long-term sums, and the total cost of ownership is still far below the grid electricity the system replaces.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cost of solar installation for a home in India?

See the table above for installed costs by size, the subsidy and your share. Most homes choose between 2 and 5 kW.

Does the solar installation cost calculator include the subsidy?

Yes, the central subsidy. State top-ups, where available, reduce the cost further.

Why are some quotes much cheaper?

Usually because of non-DCR panels, a low structure, missing protection devices or excluded net metering fees. Always compare like with like.

How long does installation take?

The fitting itself usually takes one to three days. Approvals and net metering take longer and depend on your discom.

Do I need permission to install rooftop solar?

For a grid-connected system with net metering, yes: the discom must approve feasibility and install the net meter. Housing societies may also need a resolution for common roofs.

Is GST included in solar installation cost?

Complete system quotes normally include GST, but ask the vendor to show it as a separate line so you can compare quotes fairly.

Can I add more panels later?

Yes, if the inverter has spare capacity and your discom approves the higher size. Mention it to the vendor before installation.
