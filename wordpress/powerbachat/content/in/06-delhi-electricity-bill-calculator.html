This Delhi electricity bill calculator is for domestic consumers supplied by BSES Rajdhani, BSES Yamuna or Tata Power Delhi Distribution. Enter your monthly units above and you will see the slab charges on the DERC tariff, a fixed charge for a typical 2 kW connection, electricity tax and an estimated total before the Delhi government subsidy. Below we explain how the subsidy changes that number, because for many Delhi homes the final bill is zero.

Delhi's tariff is one of the lowest in the country for small users, and the subsidy makes it lower still. The catch is that the subsidy depends on staying under certain limits. Knowing exactly where you stand is the whole game.

How to use the Delhi electricity bill calculator

  1. Find your previous reading on the last bill or in your discom's app.
  2. Read the meter now and subtract. That is your units for the month so far.
  3. Scale to a full month if needed: divide by days so far and multiply by 30.
  4. Enter the units. The slip shows the bill on the regular tariff, before subsidy.

DERC domestic rates used here

Delhi uses telescopic slabs, so each block of units is charged at its own rate. A fixed charge per kilowatt of sanctioned load and electricity tax are added.

How the Delhi subsidy works

The Delhi government subsidises domestic electricity for households that opt in. As the scheme has generally worked:

  • Up to 200 units a month: the bill is fully subsidised, so eligible households pay nothing for energy.
  • 201 to 400 units: a 50 percent subsidy applies, subject to a monthly cap.
  • Above 400 units: no subsidy, and the full tariff applies.

That makes 200 and 400 units the two lines that matter most for a Delhi family. Going from 200 to 201 units turns a zero bill into a real one. Going above 400 loses the subsidy altogether for that month. The rules, the cap and the opt-in process are set by the government and can change, so check the subsidy line on your own bill. A worked example from Dwarka

A family in Dwarka with a 2 kW connection used 190 units in February and paid nothing. In May the meter shows 160 units by the 18th. At that pace they will reach about 270 units, which means a subsidised but non-zero bill. The Delhi electricity bill calculator shows the full tariff at 270 units, and the subsidy halves part of it. They weigh it up and decide a few hours less AC is worth it in May, but accept a bill in June and July when the heat is worse. Knowing the numbers lets them choose, rather than finding out at the end of the month.

Summer in Delhi

Delhi's summer is long and hot, with dry heat in May and humid heat after the monsoon arrives. Most families cross 200 units in at least a few months, and many cross 400 in June and July. A few habits make a big difference:

  • Use coolers in the dry weeks of April and May. They use far less than an AC.
  • Keep the AC at 25 or 26 degrees with a fan, and service it before the season.
  • Block afternoon sun on west-facing windows.
  • Replace old fans with BLDC fans. They run all summer, so the saving is large.

Choosing the right sanctioned load

The fixed charge on a Delhi bill depends on your sanctioned load, and it is charged every month whether you use the power or not. Some homes carry a higher sanctioned load than they need, often from an old application or a past renovation. Check the load printed on your bill against what you actually run at the same time. If it is much higher, ask your discom about reducing it. On the other hand, if your real load regularly exceeds the sanctioned figure, the discom may revise it upwards, so keep it realistic.

Peak demand and time-of-day rates

Delhi has introduced time-of-day pricing for some categories, with higher rates during peak hours and lower rates at other times. Whether and how it applies to your domestic connection depends on your load and current DERC orders. If your bill shows separate peak and off-peak units, run heavy appliances such as the washing machine, iron and water pump outside peak hours.

Is rooftop solar worth it in Delhi?

For homes that use under 200 units, the subsidy already makes electricity almost free, so solar pays back slowly. For homes that regularly use 400 units or more in summer, the story changes. Every unit solar produces above the subsidy limits saves money at full tariff, and Delhi's net metering rules are supportive. See the 3kW solar system price and our PM Surya Ghar calculator.

Checking your bill

Each Delhi bill shows the previous and current readings, the energy charge, fixed charge, PPAC, surcharges, tax and the subsidy credit. If the subsidy line is missing when you expected it, check that you have opted in. If the units look wrong, raise it with your discom through its app or helpline before the due date. Frequently asked questions

Does the Delhi electricity bill calculator include the subsidy?

It shows the bill before subsidy. Under the scheme as it has generally worked, bills up to 200 units are fully subsidised and 201 to 400 units get a partial subsidy, so your actual bill will be lower.

Which companies does this cover?

BSES Rajdhani, BSES Yamuna and Tata Power Delhi Distribution. NDMC areas have their own billing.

Why is my Delhi bill higher than the calculator?

The power purchase adjustment and pension trust surcharge are added on top of the base tariff. Arrears or a higher sanctioned load also increase the bill.

When were the rates last checked?

We last checked the DERC domestic tariff in [powerbachat_checked].

Do tenants get the Delhi subsidy?

The subsidy is linked to the electricity connection. Tenants with their own meter can generally benefit, but the process for opting in and any documents needed are set by the scheme, so check with your discom.
