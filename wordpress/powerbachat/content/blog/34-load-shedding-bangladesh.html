Power cuts are part of life for many families, especially in the hot months when everyone runs fans and ACs at the same time. Some years are calm, others bring two or three outages a day. Load shedding in Bangladesh is hard to predict from the outside, but a home that has a simple plan copes far better than one that scrambles for candles every evening. This guide walks through that plan, step by step.

Why the power goes off

Load-shedding is a planned cut. When the grid cannot supply everyone at once, the distributor switches off feeders in turns so the whole system does not trip. The shortfall usually comes from a few places:

  • Generation: some power plants may be down for maintenance or running below capacity.
  • Fuel supply: many plants run on gas, oil or coal, and when fuel is short or too expensive to import, output drops.
  • Peak demand: evenings in summer, when ACs, fans, lights and irrigation pumps all run together, are the hardest hours for the grid.

Not every outage is load-shedding. A blown transformer, a storm or a fault on your line can also cut supply. Planned cuts tend to follow a pattern; faults do not. Knowing which one you are dealing with helps you decide whether to wait or call the distributor.

Check the schedule from your distributor

Who supplies your area decides where you look. In Dhaka it is usually DESCO or DPDC; in rural areas it is often a Palli Bidyut Samity under BREB; other regions are served by other companies. During periods of heavy cuts, distributors have sometimes published area schedules on their websites, Facebook pages or through local offices. These schedules are not always followed to the minute, but they show the rough pattern.

Start with the official sites, such as DESCO or BREB, and note the hotline number printed on your bill. Then keep your own log for a week: write down when the power went off and when it came back. After a few days you will usually see the real pattern for your feeder, which is more useful than any published list.

Decide what really needs backup

The biggest mistake is trying to back up everything. Backup power is expensive per unit, so keep the list short. For most homes, the essentials are:

  1. One or two ceiling fans, ideally in the rooms where people sleep or study.
  2. A few LED lights, one per occupied room.
  3. The Wi-Fi router and ONU, so internet keeps working.
  4. Phone and laptop charging.

Things that usually should not be on backup: the AC, the iron, the rice cooker, the water heater and the water pump. They pull too much power and will drain a home battery quickly. The fridge is a special case covered below.

Sizing an IPS in rough terms

An IPS has two parts that matter: the inverter, rated in VA, and the battery, rated in amp-hours (Ah). The inverter decides how many things can run at once. The battery decides how long they can run.

Add up the watts of your essentials. Two ordinary fans at about 75 watts each, four LED bulbs at about 10 watts each and a router at about 10 watts come to roughly 200 watts. A 600 to 1,000 VA IPS handles that comfortably. If your fans are efficient DC models, the total can drop to around 120 watts, which makes everything easier.

For running time, a single 12 volt tubular battery of around 150 to 200 Ah, used sensibly, can usually carry a 200 watt load for roughly 3 to 4 hours. If your cuts are short, a smaller battery will do. If they are long and frequent, think about a second battery, or about whether the battery gets enough charging time between outages. Keeping food safe in the fridge

A fridge holds its cold surprisingly well if you leave the door shut. A closed, well-sealed fridge usually keeps food cold for a few hours, and a full freezer holds out much longer than a half-empty one. A few habits help:

  • Keep a few bottles of water in the freezer. They act as ice packs during an outage.
  • Decide what you need before opening the door, and close it quickly.
  • Cook fish and meat soon after a long cut rather than leaving them for another day.
  • Use a voltage stabiliser or guard on the fridge. When power returns, the voltage can swing, and that is hard on the compressor.

Running a fridge on an IPS is possible but costly in battery terms, because the compressor needs a strong starting surge. Most families are better off keeping the fridge off backup and protecting it from voltage swings instead.

Phones, internet and work

Charge phones fully whenever the power is on, not when the battery is already low. A good power bank of 10,000 to 20,000 mAh can recharge a phone two or three times and costs far less than extra IPS capacity. If you work or study from home, put the router on the IPS and charge your laptop when power is available, so the laptop can run on its own battery during a cut.

Timing the water pump

In buildings with an underground tank and a roof tank, the water pump is the household's quiet emergency. If the power is off when the roof tank runs dry, there is no water until supply returns. Fill the roof tank as soon as power comes back, especially before the evening peak when cuts are most likely. Check that the float switch works so the pump stops on time; a faulty float wastes units every day, cut or no cut.

Keep a bucket or two of stored water in the bathroom and kitchen during heavy load shedding in Bangladesh. It sounds old fashioned, but it saves a lot of stress.

Safety: generators, candles and wiring

Backup can be dangerous if it is set up carelessly.

  • Generators: never run a petrol or diesel generator indoors, in a closed garage or near a window. The exhaust contains carbon monoxide, which has no smell and can kill people while they sleep. Keep it outside, well away from openings.
  • Connection: do not connect a generator or IPS straight into the main wiring without a proper changeover switch. Power can flow back into the line and injure linemen working on it.
  • Candles and kerosene lamps: keep them away from curtains and mosquito nets, and never leave them burning in a room where people sleep. Rechargeable LED lanterns are safer and cheap.
  • Battery room: lead acid batteries release gas while charging. Keep them in a ventilated spot, check water levels if they are flooded types, and keep children away.

Worked example: a family in Mirpur

Rina's family lives in a three room flat in Mirpur. Last summer they had two cuts most days, each lasting about an hour, mostly in the evening. Here is the plan they settled on.

  • Essentials: two fans at about 75 watts, four LED bulbs at 10 watts and the router at 10 watts, about 200 watts in total.
  • Backup: an 800 VA IPS with one 12 volt tubular battery of around 150 Ah. At 200 watts this gives them roughly 3 hours, enough for two one hour cuts with some margin.
  • Fridge: left off the IPS, with three frozen water bottles inside and a voltage guard on the plug.
  • Water: the pump runs as soon as power returns in the afternoon, so the roof tank is full before the evening.
  • Phones: a 20,000 mAh power bank, charged every night.

Later they replaced the two backed up fans with DC models. Their load fell to about 130 watts, and the same battery now lasts well over 4 hours. The IPS also adds to the normal bill a little, because it keeps topping up the battery; check your monthly units in the prepaid meter guide if your balance seems to fall faster than expected.

A simple checklist

  1. Find out who your distributor is and save its hotline number.
  2. Log outages for a week to learn your feeder's pattern.
  3. List your real essentials and add up their watts.
  4. Size the IPS and battery for that list, not for the whole house.
  5. Freeze water bottles, fill the roof tank and charge power banks before the evening peak.
  6. Keep generators outside and candles away from anything that can burn.

Load shedding in Bangladesh changes from year to year with fuel supply and demand. Official notices from your distributor are the best source for the current situation, and your own log is the best guide to what happens on your street.

Frequently asked questions

How can I find the load-shedding schedule for my area?

Check your distributor's website, Facebook page or local office, and call the hotline on your bill. Published schedules are not always exact, so a week of your own notes will show the real pattern for your feeder.

What size IPS do I need for two fans and some lights?

For about 200 watts of fans, lights and a router, a 600 to 1,000 VA IPS with one tubular battery of around 150 to 200 Ah usually gives 3 to 4 hours. Efficient DC fans stretch that further.

Can I run my fridge on an IPS?

It is possible with a larger inverter, but the compressor's starting surge and long running time drain the battery fast. Most homes keep the fridge closed during cuts and protect it with a voltage guard instead.

Is it safe to use a generator during power cuts?

Yes, if it runs outdoors, far from windows and doors, and connects through a proper changeover switch. Never run one inside the home or in a closed space, because of the risk of carbon monoxide poisoning.
