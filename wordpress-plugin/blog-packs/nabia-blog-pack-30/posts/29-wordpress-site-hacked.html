Quick answer: if your WordPress site is hacked, do not panic and do not start deleting files. Change all passwords and enable two-factor login, take a snapshot of the hacked site, then restore a clean backup or clean the infected files, find and close the entry point (usually an outdated plugin), harden the site and ask Google to review it if it shows a warning.

Finding spam pages on your site or a red warning in Chrome is stressful. I have cleaned up many hacked WordPress sites, and nearly all of them were back to normal within a day. Here is the plan.

Signs your site was hacked

  • Visitors are redirected to spam or scam sites
  • Google shows pages in strange languages or about products you do not sell
  • New admin users you did not create
  • A "This site may be hacked" label or a browser warning
  • Your host suspended the account for malware

The first hour

  1. Change passwords for WordPress admins, hosting, FTP/SFTP and the database. Turn on two-factor login.
  2. Remove unknown admin users.
  3. Tell your host. They can often help and confirm what they see.
  4. Take a snapshot of the hacked site for investigation, then work on recovery.

Clean-up options

Option A: Restore a clean backup

The fastest route if you have a backup from before the hack. Restore it, then immediately update everything, because the hole that let the attacker in is still there. This is why good backups with history matter.

Option B: Clean the site

Replace WordPress core, theme and plugin files with fresh copies, scan the uploads folder and database for malicious code, and remove anything unknown. This is detailed work; a mistake can leave a backdoor behind.

Find the cause

Usually one of: an outdated or abandoned plugin, a nulled premium plugin, a weak or reused password, or an insecure shared server. If you do not close the entry point, the attacker will be back.

Harden the site

Follow WordPress security: 15 steps: updates, firewall, two-factor login, fewer plugins, tested backups.

Recover in Google

In Google Search Console, check the Security Issues report and request a review once the site is clean. Remove spam URLs and make sure they return a "not found" status.
