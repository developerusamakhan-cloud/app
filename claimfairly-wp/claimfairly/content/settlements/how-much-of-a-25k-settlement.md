A $25,000 settlement is common for soft tissue injuries like whiplash or a back strain with a few months of treatment. It is also exactly the minimum bodily injury limit in many states, so it is often the most the at-fault driver's policy will pay.

The quick answer: with a one third attorney fee and nothing else taken out, you would keep about **$16,668**. Real settlements usually have case costs and medical liens too, so most people keep less. ## A realistic $25,000 example

Here is a typical breakdown with a one third (33.33%) fee taken from the full settlement, $500 in case costs, and a $4,000 medical lien that the lawyer negotiated down by 30%.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Settlement | $25,000 |
| Attorney fee (one third (33.33%)) | minus $8,333 |
| Case costs | minus $500 |
| Medical lien after reduction | minus $2,800 |
| **You keep** | **$13,368** |

If the fee were calculated **after** costs instead, you would keep about **$13,534**, a difference of $167. That is why it is worth reading your fee agreement closely. Our guide on [attorney fees and medical liens](/attorney-fees-and-medical-liens/) explains each deduction in detail.

## How the fee percentage changes a $25,000 settlement

Before costs and liens:

| Fee | Attorney fee | Left for you |
|---|---|---|
| 30% | $7,500 | $17,500 |
| 33.33% | $8,333 | $16,668 |
| 35% | $8,750 | $16,250 |
| 40% | $10,000 | $15,000 |

## What kind of case settles for about $25,000?

Claims that settle around $25,000 usually involve medical bills somewhere between $5,000 and $10,000, a few weeks of missed work at most, and an injury that healed within a few months. Rear-end crashes with clear fault and consistent physical therapy are typical. If your bills are much higher than that, $25,000 may be low, or it may simply be the policy limit. To see where your own claim may land, enter your bills, lost wages and injury severity in the [car accident settlement calculator](/car-accident-settlement-calculator/), which also applies your state's fault rule.

## When $25,000 is the policy limit

If the other driver carries only $25,000 per person, a $25,000 offer may be a "policy limits" offer, meaning the insurer is offering everything the policy allows. If your damages are higher, ask about **underinsured motorist (UIM)** coverage on your own policy, which can pay the difference up to your UIM limit. Many insurers require your consent before you accept a limits offer from the other driver, so talk to your own insurer first. Accepting too fast can put your UIM claim at risk.

## How long a $25,000 settlement usually takes

Claims of this size often settle within three to six months of finishing treatment, because the records are short and fault is usually clear. After you sign the release, payment usually arrives within two to six weeks. Liens can add time. Read [how long a car accident settlement takes](/how-long-does-a-car-accident-settlement-take/) for the full timeline.

## Is $25,000 a fair number for your claim?

Run through these before you accept:

- Does it cover all medical bills, including future care your doctor recommended?
- Does it cover lost wages and out-of-pocket costs?
- Is there a reasonable amount left for pain and suffering? Check with the [pain and suffering calculator](/pain-and-suffering-calculator/).
- Did the insurer assign you any fault, and do you agree with it?
- Is it close to the other driver's policy limit?
- After fees and liens, is the amount you keep enough to cover what the crash cost you?

If several answers are "no," read [should you accept the first settlement offer](/should-you-accept-the-first-settlement-offer/) before you respond.

## How to keep more of your $25,000 settlement

- Ask whether your fee is calculated before or after costs.
- Ask your lawyer to negotiate every medical lien.
- Ask for an itemized list of costs with receipts.
- Request a written settlement statement before you sign the release.
- Check whether your own MedPay coverage already paid some bills without a lien.

## Three versions of the same $25,000 settlement

| Scenario | Fee | Costs | Lien paid | You keep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No lawyer, lien paid in full | $0 | $0 | $4,000 | $21,000 |
| Lawyer, settled before a lawsuit (33.33%) | $8,333 | $500 | $2,800 | $13,368 |
| Lawyer, settled after a lawsuit (40%) | $10,000 | $1,000 | $2,800 | $11,200 |

This comparison assumes the same $25,000 gross settlement in all three cases, which is not always realistic. Represented claimants often settle for more, and lawyers usually negotiate liens down. The point is to see how each factor moves your share, not to suggest which path is right for you.

## When the money arrives

At this size, the biggest risk is that liens and bills quietly eat most of the check. Confirm every lien is paid through the settlement, so no provider comes back to you later.

A few practical steps help at any settlement size:

1. **Confirm every lien is paid** and get written confirmation that each account is closed.
2. **Keep your settlement statement** and the release with your tax records.
3. **Set aside money for any remaining care**, especially if your doctor expects future treatment.
4. **Check the tax question** if any part of the settlement is for interest, punitive damages or non-physical injuries.
5. **Watch for scams.** People who receive settlements are sometimes targeted with investment pitches. Take your time.

## Words you will see on your settlement paperwork

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| Gross settlement | The total amount the insurer agrees to pay, before anything is deducted |
| Net to client | What you receive after the fee, costs and liens |
| Contingency fee | The lawyer's percentage, paid only if you recover money |
| Case costs | Expenses the lawyer paid to build your case |
| Lien | A legal right to be repaid from your settlement, often by a health plan or hospital |
| Subrogation | Your insurer's right to recover what it paid on your behalf |
| Release | The document that ends your claim in exchange for payment |
