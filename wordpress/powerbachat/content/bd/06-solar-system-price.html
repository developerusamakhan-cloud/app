What is included

  • Panels: usually large 540 to 580 watt panels. See today's rates on our solar panel price page.
  • Inverter: a grid-tie inverter, or a hybrid inverter that can also charge batteries.
  • Mounting structure: a galvanised steel frame, often raised on flat roofs.
  • Cabling and protection: DC and AC cables, breakers, surge protection and earthing.
  • Installation: labour, transport and commissioning.

Adding batteries for load-shedding

Many families in Bangladesh want solar mainly for backup during load-shedding. A hybrid system with batteries keeps fans, lights and the fridge running when the grid is off, and can store daytime solar for the evening. Batteries add significantly to the solar system price in Bangladesh and need replacing far sooner than panels, so size them for your real outage hours. Our IPS calculator shows backup time for any battery and load, and the solar battery price page lists current battery prices.

How quickly does solar pay back?

Payback depends on three things: what the system costs, how many units it produces, and what those units would cost from the grid. Because the tariff rises steeply above 400 units, solar pays back fastest for homes that regularly use more than that. A home using 500 units a month in summer saves most on the units above 400, which are the most expensive. The calculator above estimates payback from your own usage.

Net metering in Bangladesh

Bangladesh has a net metering framework for rooftop solar, which allows surplus solar units to be exported to the grid and credited against your imports. The process, eligibility and paperwork are handled through your distributor. If your system will produce more than you use during the day, ask your distributor about net metering before you install, so the system is set up correctly from the start.

A worked example from Dhaka

A family in Bashundhara uses around 520 units a month from April to September and 280 in winter. A 4 kW system produces roughly 400 to 450 units a month on average. In summer, it brings their grid usage down below 400, removing the most expensive units entirely. In winter, it covers most of their use. With a modest battery for load-shedding, their total cost is higher, but so is the value: the house keeps running during outages, and the bill falls sharply.

Solar home systems in villages

Bangladesh is known worldwide for its rural solar home systems, small panels with a battery that brought light to millions of households without grid power. As the grid has expanded, many of these households now have both. Small systems are still useful as backup, and a 100 to 200 watt panel with a good battery can keep lights, fans and phones running through outages.

On-grid, hybrid or off-grid?

On-grid systems cost the least and cut your bill most efficiently, but shut down during load-shedding for safety. Hybrid systems add batteries and keep essentials running during outages, which is why they are popular in Bangladesh despite the higher cost. Off-grid systems, with no grid connection, suit places without reliable supply and need a larger battery bank. For most city homes with frequent outages, a hybrid system with a modest battery is the practical middle ground.

Storms, cyclones and roof safety

Bangladesh's storms and coastal cyclones put real force on rooftop structures. Panels act like sails in strong wind, so the frame must be properly anchored to the roof slab and built from adequately thick galvanised steel. Ask the installer how the structure is fixed, how it handles wind, and how the roof will be kept waterproof where bolts go in. In coastal districts, ask about corrosion-resistant fittings, because salt air shortens the life of cheap hardware.

Getting a fair quote

  1. Ask for an itemised quote: panel model, inverter model, structure, cables and protection.
  2. Check the warranty on panels, inverter and workmanship.
  3. Make sure the installer checks for shade and roof strength.
  4. If you want batteries, ask for the battery type, capacity and expected life.
  5. Compare at least three quotes for the same specification.

What drives the price up or down

Within each size, the solar system price in Bangladesh varies with a few choices: the panel brand and type, whether the inverter is grid-tie or hybrid, the height and strength of the structure, the length of cable runs, and the installer's service and warranty. Roofs that are hard to reach, need extra waterproofing or sit in coastal areas cost more to work on. A quote at the low end of the range is fine if it uses good equipment; one far below it usually leaves something out. Ask what the price includes before comparing totals.

When in doubt, ask two installers to quote exactly the same equipment list. Any remaining difference is their labour and service, which is easier to judge.

Looking after your system

Clean the panels regularly, especially in the dry season. Check the inverter for error messages. Keep batteries in a cool, ventilated place and follow the maintenance advice for your battery type. An annual check of wiring and earthing keeps the system safe.

Frequently asked questions

What is the solar system price in Bangladesh for a home?

See the table above for complete systems from 1 kW to 10 kW. Most homes choose between 2 and 5 kW.

How many units does a 1 kW solar system produce in Bangladesh?

Around 100 to 110 units a month on average, depending on location, shade and cleaning.

Does the price include batteries?

No. The table is for grid-connected systems. Batteries for backup are extra.

Can solar run my AC?

A system of 3 kW or more can run an inverter AC during sunny hours, along with other loads.

Do I need permission to install rooftop solar?

For a grid-connected system with net metering, you need to apply through your distributor. For a standalone system, check building rules and, in apartments, get the owners' agreement for the roof.
