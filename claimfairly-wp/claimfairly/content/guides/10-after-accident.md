Right after a crash, your heart is pounding and it is hard to think. That is normal. This checklist is here so you do not have to remember everything. Save it on your phone now, before you need it.

## At the scene

1. **Check for injuries and call 911** if anyone is hurt. Your health comes first, always.
2. **Get to safety.** If the cars can move and it is safe, pull out of traffic. Turn on your hazard lights.
3. **Call the police**, even for a minor crash if your state or the situation calls for it. A police report is one of the most useful documents in any claim.
4. **Exchange information** with the other driver: name, phone, address, license number, plate number, insurance company and policy number. A photo of their license and insurance card is easiest.
5. **Take photos** of both cars from several angles, the damage up close, the road, traffic signs, skid marks and weather.
6. **Get witness names and numbers.** People leave fast, and independent witnesses carry a lot of weight.
7. **Do not admit fault** or apologize. Stick to facts. Fault is decided later, with all the evidence.

## Within 24 to 72 hours

1. **See a doctor**, even if you feel fine. Adrenaline hides pain, and some injuries like whiplash and concussions show up a day or two later. A visit soon after the crash also links your injuries to the accident.
2. **Report the crash to your insurer.** Most policies require prompt notice.
3. **Report to the other driver's insurer** and get a claim number.
4. **Start a folder** (paper or digital) for everything: the police report number, photos, bills, receipts, and a list of calls with dates and names.
5. **Write down what happened** while it is fresh, in your own words.

## During the claim

- **Follow your treatment plan.** Gaps in treatment are one of the most common reasons adjusters cut injury claims.
- **Keep every receipt**, including prescriptions, parking and mileage to appointments.
- **Track missed work** with dates and hours, and ask your employer for a letter.
- **Keep a short pain journal.** A few lines a day about pain, sleep and what you could not do.
- **Be careful with recorded statements** to the other driver's insurer. You can politely decline or ask to answer questions in writing.
- **Stay off social media** about the crash, your injuries or your activities.
- **Do not sign a release** or accept a check marked "final" until you know the full cost of your injuries.

If your car was repaired, check the [diminished value calculator](/diminished-value-calculator/) too. It is money many people never ask for.

## When to call a lawyer

Consider a free consultation if any of these apply:

- You were seriously hurt or still in treatment
- Fault is disputed
- The other driver is uninsured or has low limits
- The insurer is delaying, denying or pressuring you
- A filing deadline is getting close
