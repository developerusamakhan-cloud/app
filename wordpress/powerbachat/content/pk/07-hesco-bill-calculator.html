Our HESCO bill calculator is for households supplied by the Hyderabad Electric Supply Company. HESCO covers Hyderabad and much of southern Sindh outside Karachi, including Mirpurkhas, Thatta, Badin, Sanghar, Tando Allahyar, Tando Muhammad Khan, Matiari, Jamshoro, Umerkot and Tharparkar. Type in the units from your meter above and you will see the energy charge for each slab, GST, electricity duty and an estimated total.

Southern Sindh has a long hot season and a humid one. Hyderabad's famous evening breeze helps, but from April to October most homes rely on fans for most of the day and ACs or coolers at night. Many areas also face regular load-shedding, which brings its own costs through UPS charging. All of this makes it worth knowing your bill before it lands.

Getting an estimate you can trust

  1. Find the present reading on your last HESCO bill.
  2. Read the meter now and subtract. That is your usage so far this cycle.
  3. Scale to a full month if needed: units so far divided by days, times 30.
  4. Enter the result above. Switch on "Protected consumer" only if each of your last six bills was at or below 200 units.

The slip shows the estimated amount first and your average cost per unit underneath. Open the breakdown to see which slab every unit lands in.

Look at the per unit column as well as the totals. It rises with usage because more of your units fall into the costly slabs. Many HESCO households are small, with fans, lights, a fridge and a TV, and stay near 150 to 250 units even in summer. That puts them right on the protected line, where one heavy month costs far more than it seems. Larger homes in Latifabad, Qasimabad or the cantonment with two ACs easily pass 500 units in June.

Humidity, coolers and ACs

Air coolers work by evaporating water, and they cool well in dry heat. Near the coast in Thatta and Badin, and during the monsoon in Hyderabad, the air is already humid, so a cooler mostly adds moisture and feels sticky. In these conditions a small inverter AC used for a few hours in the bedroom often gives better comfort per unit than a cooler running all day. Set it to 26 degrees with the ceiling fan on low, and close the curtains on the sunny side of the room in the afternoon.

Load-shedding and what it adds to your bill

Several HESCO feeders face long outages, particularly where line losses are high. A UPS with batteries keeps the fans turning, but it recharges from the grid every time power returns. Old lead-acid batteries and cheap inverters can waste a good share of that energy as heat, quietly adding 20 to 50 units a month. If your backup setup is more than three or four years old, it may be costing more than you think. Our battery backup calculator helps you size a replacement properly, and the lithium vs tubular battery guide compares running costs.

A worked example from Hyderabad

A family in Qasimabad has been protected all winter. On May 15 their meter shows 140 units used in the new cycle. At that rate they will finish near 280 units, well past the 200 line. The HESCO bill calculator shows the estimate with protected status off, and then at 200 units with it on. The gap is large. They decide to run the AC only after midnight for the rest of the month and lean on fans in the evening breeze. They finish at 205 units, just over the line, which is a reminder of how tight the margin is. Next month they start careful habits from day one, but the status is already lost for six months. That is why reading the meter early in the month matters so much.

If the units on your bill look wrong

Over-billing complaints are common, and the fix is usually simple if you move quickly. Photograph your meter display with your phone's date visible on the same day the bill arrives. Compare the present reading on the bill with the photo. If the bill shows more units than the meter, take the photo, the bill and your CNIC to the HESCO subdivision office and ask for a correction before the due date. Ask for a written note of your complaint. If a meter seems to run even with the main switch off, request a meter test. Bills marked as estimated or "average" are worth checking every time, because the catch-up month that follows can push you into a much higher slab.

Lines the calculator leaves out

  • Fuel price adjustment. Changes every month. Our FPA guide explains it.
  • Arrears and surcharges. Specific to your account.
  • Income tax on larger bills for non-filers.
  • Agricultural connections. Tube wells use a different tariff from the household slabs here.

Practical ways to lower a HESCO bill

  • Watch the first two weeks of May. That is when most protected homes lose their status.
  • Replace old tube lights and fans. Modern DC inverter fans use about half the power of old ones, which matters when fans run 18 hours a day.
  • Keep the outdoor AC unit shaded and clean. Dust and heat make it work much harder.
  • Consider solar. Southern Sindh gets strong sunshine nearly all year. Start with our solar calculator and the solar panel price today page.

Frequently asked questions

Is this HESCO bill calculator correct for Mirpurkhas and Badin?

Yes. All HESCO districts use the same national tariff, so the calculator works for every household connection in the region.

Why does my HESCO bill not match the estimate?

The estimate covers the energy charge, GST and electricity duty. The monthly fuel adjustment, arrears or a surcharge usually explain the difference. If the units themselves look wrong, take a dated photo of your meter to the subdivision office.

Is HESCO the same as K-Electric?

No. K-Electric supplies Karachi. If you live in Karachi, use our K-Electric bill calculator instead.

How can I check my HESCO bill online?

Use the 14-digit reference number printed on your paper bill with the official online bill service, then compare its units with this calculator.
