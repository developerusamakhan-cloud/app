This TNEB bill calculator is for domestic consumers in Tamil Nadu. It works the way your EB bill actually works: on units used over a two-month billing cycle, with the first 100 units free and a different slab table once you cross 500 units. Enter the units from your meter above and you will see each slab, the free units and the estimated amount for the cycle.

Most people still say TNEB, though the distribution company is now called TNPDCL, after the old TANGEDCO was restructured. The tariff itself is set by the Tamil Nadu Electricity Regulatory Commission, and it is the same across the state, from Chennai to Coimbatore to Madurai.

How to use the TNEB bill calculator

  1. Find the previous reading on your last EB card or bill, or in the TNEB app.
  2. Read your meter today and subtract. That is how many units you have used in this cycle so far.
  3. If the cycle is not finished, estimate the full two months. Divide by the days so far and multiply by about 60.
  4. Enter the result above. The slip shows the estimated bill for the whole two-month cycle.

The most important number to watch is 500. If you are close to it, the decision you make in the last week of the cycle can change the whole bill.

TNEB slab rates in the calculator

Tamil Nadu uses two different slab tables depending on how much you use in the cycle. If your total is 500 units or less, one table applies. If it goes above 500, a costlier table applies to the entire bill. In both, the first 100 units are free for domestic consumers.

Why the cycle is two months

Tamil Nadu bills domestic connections every two months, so the slab limits are set for 60 days rather than 30. A home using 300 units a month will see about 600 units on its bill, which puts it above the 500 unit line. That catches many people out, especially those who moved from another state where bills are monthly. When you compare your usage with friends elsewhere, halve your TNEB units first.

A worked example from Chennai

A family in Velachery has a cycle that started on 5 April. On 20 May, their meter shows 430 units used. With about two weeks left, the pace points to around 570 units for the cycle. The TNEB bill calculator shows the bill at 570 units on the higher table and at 500 units on the lower one. The difference is large enough that they switch the second AC off for the last two weeks and use the bedroom AC only after midnight. They finish at 496 units and stay on the cheaper table.

It is not always possible to stay under 500 in a Chennai summer, but knowing exactly where the line sits makes the choice yours rather than the meter's.

Reading the meter and avoiding estimated bills

Tamil Nadu meters are read by an assessor every two months. If the reading is missed or the meter is inaccessible, the bill may be based on an average, which can push the next cycle above 500 units when the real reading catches up. To protect yourself, photograph the meter display with the date on the day of the visit, and keep the photo until the bill arrives. If the units on the bill do not match, raise it with your section office straight away. Many consumers now also pay through the TNEB app or online portal, which shows the reading used for each bill.

Summer, monsoon and your cycle

Chennai and the coastal districts are hot and humid from March to June, and AC use pushes many homes over 500 units in those cycles. The north-east monsoon from October to December brings cooler weather and lower bills. If you can only stay under 500 units in some cycles, aim for the shoulder months either side of summer, where a little care is often enough. Inland cities like Coimbatore and Madurai have their own patterns, so let your own bill history guide you.

Fixed charges, duty and other lines

For domestic consumers, the bill is mostly the energy charge from the slabs. Depending on your connection and current orders, a few other lines can appear, such as a fixed charge for higher sanctioned loads, a deposit adjustment or arrears. Our estimate covers the energy charge from the current slab table. If your printed bill is higher, check those extra lines first. Lowering a Tamil Nadu EB bill

  • Read the meter around day 45. That leaves two weeks to act if you are heading past 500.
  • Set the AC to 25 or 26 degrees. Chennai's humidity makes a fan plus a moderate setting feel cooler than the number suggests.
  • Swap old ceiling fans for BLDC fans. In a state where fans run most of the year, the saving is real.
  • Watch the motor. A sump-to-tank pump that runs too long is a common hidden load.
  • Look at rooftop solar. With the central subsidy, a small system often keeps a home under 500 units for good. Try the PM Surya Ghar calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Are the first 100 units really free in Tamil Nadu?

Yes, for domestic consumers the first 100 units in each two-month cycle are free. The TNEB bill calculator applies this automatically.

Why did my EB bill jump when I crossed 500 units?

Above 500 units in a cycle, a different slab table applies to the whole bill, and the cheaper rate for units 101 to 200 is lost. That makes the 501st unit very expensive.

Is the TNEB bill calculator updated for the latest tariff?

Yes. We check the TNERC rates regularly. The last check was in [powerbachat_checked], and the date is shown under the calculator.

Can I use this for a commercial connection?

No. Commercial and other categories use different tariffs. This calculator is for domestic connections only.
