Volts alone cannot be turned into watts. Voltage is the electrical pressure, and watts are the power that flows, so you also need the current in amps: watts = volts × amps. Enter the voltage and the current in the calculator above and it gives the power straight away.

For example, a 120 volt outlet supplying 10 amps delivers 1,200 watts, and a 5 volt USB port supplying 2 amps delivers 10 watts. The same voltage can deliver very different power depending on how much current flows.

The volts to watts formula

For DC circuits and for AC loads such as heaters and kettles:

watts = volts × amps

For AC motors, compressors and other loads with a power factor below 1, multiply by the power factor as well: watts = volts × amps × power factor. For three phase supplies, see our amps to watts calculator, which adds the three phase factor of 1.732.

Worked examples

  • Phone charger: 9 volts × 2 amps = 18 watts, a common fast charging rating.
  • Laptop charger: 20 volts × 3.25 amps = 65 watts.
  • USB C at full power: 20 volts × 5 amps = 100 watts.
  • Car accessory socket: 12 volts × 10 amps = 120 watts, the limit for many 10 amp fused sockets.
  • 120 volt outlet: 120 volts × 15 amps = 1,800 watts.

Why the voltage matters

For the same power, a higher voltage needs less current. That is why long distance power lines run at very high voltages and why 230 volt countries can use thinner household wiring than 120 volt countries for the same appliances. It is also why 12 volt systems in boats, caravans and solar setups need thick cables: 1,200 watts at 12 volts means 100 amps.

Never plug a device into a higher voltage than it is rated for. An appliance rated 120 volts only can be damaged on a 230 volt supply even though the plug may fit with an adapter. Many chargers and laptops accept 100 to 240 volts, which the label will show.

Frequently asked questions

How many watts is 120 volts?

It depends on the current. 120 volts at 1 amp is 120 watts, at 10 amps it is 1,200 watts, and at 15 amps it is 1,800 watts.

How many watts is 12 volts?

Also depends on the current: 12 volts at 1 amp is 12 watts, and 12 volts at 10 amps is 120 watts.

Can I convert volts to watts without amps?

Only if you know the resistance instead. Then watts = volts × volts ÷ ohms. For a 230 volt heater element of 26.5 ohms, that is about 2,000 watts.

Is a higher voltage more powerful?

Not by itself. Power depends on voltage and current together. A higher voltage can deliver the same power with less current, which reduces losses in the wires.
