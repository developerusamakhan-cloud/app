How the household tariff is structured

Pakistan uses a slab system for homes. Your monthly units are divided into blocks, and each block has its own rate. The rates rise as you move up the blocks. There are two sets of rates:

  • Protected rates for homes that used 200 units or less in each of the last six months.
  • Non-protected rates for everyone else.

The aim is to keep basic electricity affordable for small households while charging heavy users more. In practice, it also creates sharp jumps that are worth understanding, especially at the 200 unit line.

These slabs are telescopic: units are charged at the rate of the slab they fall into, not all at the highest rate. A home using 450 units pays the first slab rate on its first 100 units, the second rate on the next 100, and so on, with only the last 50 units at the 401 to 500 rate.

The protected rates are far below the normal ones. That gap is why one month over 200 units is so costly for a protected home: the status ends, and the next six months are billed on the normal table. Our guide to the protected consumer rule explains how it works in practice.

What is added to the per unit price

The slab rate is the starting point. Several more charges are added, and together they make the real electricity price in Pakistan per unit noticeably higher than the tariff table suggests.

  • Fuel price adjustment (FPA or FCA). A monthly per-unit charge or refund that reflects actual fuel costs from an earlier month. Our fuel price adjustment guide explains it.
  • Quarterly tariff adjustment (QTA). A per-unit adjustment that appears for a few months when costs are revised.
  • GST at 18 percent, charged on the energy cost and most adjustments.
  • Electricity duty, a small percentage set by the province. Our estimates use 1.5 percent.
  • Income tax, on larger bills for consumers who are not active taxpayers.

Peak and off-peak pricing

Households with a sanctioned load of 5 kW or more are usually billed on time-of-use meters. These meters record peak and off-peak units separately, and peak units cost more. Peak hours are set in the evening and change with the season, typically starting earlier in winter and later in summer. The exact hours are printed on your bill. If you are on a time-of-use meter, moving heavy loads out of the peak window is one of the easiest ways to lower your average price without using less electricity.

Is the price the same in every city?

Yes for the base slab rates. A unit costs the same in Lahore, Quetta and Karachi before adjustments. The monthly fuel adjustment can differ, particularly for Karachi, where K-Electric's adjustment is worked out separately. For city-level detail see our LESCO unit price and K-Electric unit price pages.

How often the tariff changes

Base rates usually change once or twice a year, after the regulator reviews costs and the government notifies a new tariff. The fuel adjustment changes every month, and quarterly adjustments come and go. That is why your bill can move even when your units stay the same. When a new tariff is notified, we update the tables on this page, the calculators and every article that uses them, so all of our numbers stay consistent.

A short glossary of tariff terms

  • Unit: one kilowatt-hour (kWh), the energy a 1,000 watt appliance uses in one hour. Your meter counts units.
  • Slab: a block of units charged at one rate, such as 101 to 200 units.
  • Telescopic billing: each slab is charged only on the units inside it, so moving into a higher slab affects only the extra units.
  • Protected consumer: a home that stayed at 200 units or less for six months in a row and pays lower rates.
  • Sanctioned load: the maximum load your connection is approved for, in kW. It decides your meter type.
  • FPA or FCA: the monthly fuel adjustment, charged or refunded per unit.

Once these terms are clear, the electricity price in Pakistan per unit becomes much easier to reason about, and so does your bill.

Turning the tariff into savings

  • Know your top slab. Units in your highest slab are the most expensive, so they are the best ones to save.
  • Protect the 200 line if you are close to it, especially in the first hot month of the year.
  • Shift loads off-peak if you have a time-of-use meter.
  • Price solar against your real rate, not the first slab. Our solar calculator does this for you.

Frequently asked questions

What is the electricity price in Pakistan per unit right now?

It depends on your slab and whether you are protected. The tables above list every current rate. GST, duty and the fuel adjustment are added on top.

What is the per unit price for 300 units?

A 300 unit bill is charged across the first three slabs. See the 300 row in the average price table for the effective rate including taxes.

Why does my real price per unit differ from the table?

Because taxes and the monthly fuel adjustment are added, and because your units are spread across several slabs. Divide your total by your units to see your real rate.

Do commercial connections pay the same rates?

No. Shops, offices and industry use separate tariffs. This page covers household connections only.
