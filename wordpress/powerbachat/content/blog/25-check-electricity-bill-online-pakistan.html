The paper bill gets lost, arrives late, or lands at a house you have just moved out of. The good news is that you can check electricity bill online in Pakistan in a couple of minutes, as long as you have one number from an old bill. This guide shows where that number is, which official sites to use, what to look at once the bill is on your screen and how to pay without falling for a fake site.

The one number you need

Every online bill lookup starts with the number that identifies your connection. Which number depends on who supplies your power.

For WAPDA distribution companies (DISCOs)

If your power comes from LESCO, IESCO, MEPCO, FESCO, GEPCO, PESCO, HESCO, SEPCO, QESCO or TESCO, you need the reference number. It is printed near the top of the bill, close to your name and address. It is a long number, usually 14 digits, and on many bills it is split into small groups with spaces. Some bills also show a letter after the digits. When you type it online, enter the digits exactly as printed and leave out the spaces.

Do not confuse the reference number with the consumer ID or the meter number. They are printed nearby but are not what the bill lookup asks for. If a site rejects your number, this mix-up is the most common reason.

For K-Electric in Karachi

K-Electric uses its own account number instead of a reference number. You will find it near the top of your KE bill. It is also shown in KE's text messages and in its app if you have registered there.

If you have no old bill at all, ask your landlord, check your bank or wallet app's payment history (the number is usually saved there), or visit the customer service centre of your power company with your CNIC and address.

Where to check electricity bill online

Stick to official sources. For the DISCOs, the bills are generated by the Power Information Technology Company, and the official PITC bill site lets you view and print a duplicate bill for any of them. You pick your company, enter the reference number and the latest bill opens, laid out just like the paper copy.

K-Electric customers should use the K-Electric website or the official KE app. Both let you view your latest bill with the account number, and the app can also send reminders.

These sites change their layout from time to time, but the steps stay the same: choose your company, enter your number, view the bill, then print or save it as a PDF.

What to check on the bill

Opening the bill is the easy part. Mistakes do happen, so spend two more minutes reading it. Our guide on how to read your electricity bill line by line covers every field, but these are the ones that matter most.

Units consumed

Compare this month's units with the same month last year. Most bills show a 12 month history table. A sudden jump with no new appliance is worth questioning. A sudden drop is also a warning sign, because a low reading now can turn into a large catch up bill later.

Previous and present reading

Take a photo of your meter on the day you check the bill and compare it with the present reading printed on it. The meter will be a little ahead because days have passed, but it should not be behind. If the printed reading is higher than what your meter shows now, the reading was estimated or entered wrongly.

Reading date and billing days

Look at the reading date. If it moved later than usual, the bill covers more days, so you will see more units. On a slab tariff, a few extra days can push you into a higher slab or past an important threshold. Our page on how the 200 unit rule works explains why that line matters so much.

Arrears and adjustments

Arrears are unpaid amounts carried forward. If you paid last month and still see arrears, find your payment receipt or bank record and take it to your company's complaint office. Also check the adjustment lines. The monthly fuel charge is one of them, and our explainer on the fuel price adjustment shows why it changes every month.

Due date

The bill shows a due date and a higher amount payable after that date. Paying even one day late adds the surcharge, so note the due date as soon as you open the bill.

Worked example: checking a bill that looks too high

Say Ahmed in Lahore usually uses about 250 units in August. This year his bill shows 330 units. Here is how he checks it in a few minutes.

  1. He opens the official PITC site, selects LESCO, enters his reference number and views the duplicate bill.
  2. He sees the reading date moved five days later than last month, so this bill covers about 35 days instead of 30.
  3. He works out his daily use: 330 units over 35 days is about 9.4 units a day. Last year, 250 units over 30 days was about 8.3 units a day.
  4. So most of the jump is the longer billing period. The rest is a real rise, which matches the extra AC hours during a hot month.
  5. He photographs his meter. The reading is slightly ahead of the printed present reading, as it should be, so the reading itself looks genuine.

Ahmed's bill is probably correct, but the longer period pushed him into a higher slab. If he had found the meter behind the printed reading, he would have filed a complaint with the photo as evidence. Paying your bill online

Once you have checked the bill, paying online is simple. Most banking apps in Pakistan have a utility bill section where you pick your electricity company and enter the reference number or KE account number. Mobile wallets such as JazzCash and Easypaisa offer the same. Many people also pay through ATMs.

A few habits save trouble:

  • Save the payment receipt or screenshot until the next bill shows the payment.
  • Pay a few days before the due date, not on the last evening, in case the app or bank is down.
  • Save your number as a biller in your app so you do not have to type it every month.

Avoid fake bill check sites

Search for a way to check electricity bill online and you will find dozens of unofficial sites. Many just copy the official bill and add ads. Some are worse: they ask for your CNIC, phone number or bank details, or send you to a payment page that is not linked to your power company.

Follow simple rules. View bills only on the official PITC site, the K-Electric website or app, or your company's own site. Never enter card or bank details on a bill viewing site. Pay only through your bank, a known wallet or an official counter. A bill lookup never needs your CNIC or a password.

Frequently asked questions

Can I check my bill without the reference number?

Not on the official sites. Find the number on an old bill, in your bank or wallet payment history, or ask your power company's customer service centre with your CNIC and address.

Is a duplicate bill printed from the PITC site valid for payment?

It is generally accepted at banks and counters, and most people now pay digitally anyway. If a particular counter refuses it, pay through your banking app or wallet instead.

Why is my new bill not showing online yet?

Bills appear online only after they are generated for your batch. If the reading was taken recently, wait a few days and try again.

Where do I complain about a wrong reading?

Start with your power company's complaint office or helpline, with a photo of the meter and the bill. If that does not resolve it, NEPRA handles consumer complaints against distribution companies, and its website lists the current process.
