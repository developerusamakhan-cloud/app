This page is about PowerBachat: who we are, why the site exists and how we try to earn your trust. The short version is simple. Electricity bills in South Asia have become one of the biggest monthly worries for ordinary families, and the information needed to understand them is scattered across regulator notifications, company websites and rumours on WhatsApp. We wanted one calm place where anyone could check a bill, see what a unit really costs and decide whether solar makes sense, without signing up for anything.

Why we built it

It started with a bill that did not make sense. The units looked normal, but the total had jumped. Working out why meant reading a tariff notification, finding the right slab, adding the fuel adjustment, the fixed charge and several taxes, then checking whether the protected status had been lost. It took an evening. Most people do not have an evening to spare, and they should not need one to understand what they owe.

So we built calculators that do that work in seconds, and we wrote plain explanations to sit beside them. Every calculator shows its working: the slabs, the rate in each slab, the fixed charges and the taxes. If our number differs from your bill, you can see exactly where, and that usually points to the reason.

What you will find here

  • Bill calculators for the main electricity companies in Pakistan, India and Bangladesh, from LESCO and K-Electric to TNEB, BESCOM, DESCO and DPDC.
  • Unit rate pages that lay out the current slabs and show what a typical bill looks like at different levels of use.
  • Solar tools that size a rooftop system from your own units, estimate the cost and the payback, and, in India, work in the PM Surya Ghar subsidy.
  • Backup calculators for batteries, inverters and IPS sets, because power cuts are part of life for many of our readers.
  • Guides that explain the things bills rarely explain: fuel price adjustments, protected consumer rules, prepaid meters, time of use meters and more.

One country at a time

You will notice that the site only shows the country you are in. Someone in Lahore does not need TNEB slabs, and someone in Chennai does not need the NEPRA protected consumer rule. We use your approximate location to choose the right country automatically, and pages with /pk/, /in/ or /bd/ in the address always show that country. Our privacy policy explains exactly how that works and what we do not keep.

How we keep the numbers honest

Every rate on the site comes from the regulator's own notification or the electricity company's published schedule, not from news reports or other calculators. We record the date we last checked, show it on the site, and update the tables when a new notification is issued. Solar and battery prices are market prices, so they move more often; we show the date they were last updated next to every price table.

We are open about the limits too. Our results are estimates. Your official bill can include items we cannot know about, such as arrears, meter rent, a particular local tax or a one-off adjustment. When there is a difference, the official bill is the final word. Our editorial policy explains how we source, check and correct our figures, and our disclaimer sets out the limits plainly.

Independent and free

PowerBachat is independent. We are not part of any regulator, distribution company, government body or solar installer, and no company pays to change what we write about it. The calculators are free and will stay free. If we ever show advertising or recommend a product with a referral arrangement, we will say so clearly on the page where it appears.

We do not ask you to create an account. Calculations run in your browser, and the units you type are not stored on our servers. If you choose to join our tariff alerts, we keep your email address only for that purpose, and every message has a one-click unsubscribe link.

Who writes the content

The pages and guides are written and checked by the PowerBachat team: people who read tariff notifications closely and who also pay electricity bills in the same cities as our readers. We write in plain English because the subject is already complicated enough. When a rule is uncertain or a policy is still being finalised, we say so instead of guessing.

Where to start

If you came here with a bill in your hand, start with the bill calculator for your country and company. If you are thinking about solar, start with the solar calculator, which sizes a system from your actual units rather than a sales brochure. If something about your bill puzzles you, the guides section probably has an answer, and if it does not, tell us.
