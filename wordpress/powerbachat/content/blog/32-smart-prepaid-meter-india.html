Across many Indian states, discom staff are replacing old electricity meters with new digital ones that talk to the utility over a mobile network. In a lot of homes, the new device is a smart prepaid meter: you pay first, then use the power, much like a prepaid mobile connection. The change has caused plenty of confusion and some anger. This guide explains what these meters do, how recharge and alerts work, and how to check that yours is reading correctly.

What a smart meter actually is

A smart meter measures the units you use, just like the old meter did. The difference is that it records your use in short intervals and sends the data to the discom automatically. Nobody needs to visit your house to take a reading, and the utility can see usage almost in real time.

The large rollout comes from the central government's Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme, often called RDSS, which aims to cut losses at discoms and improve billing. Under it, states plan to install crores of smart meters on homes, shops, transformers and feeders. The pace and approach differ a lot from state to state.

Prepaid mode versus postpaid mode

The same smart meter can usually work in either mode. In postpaid mode, you get a bill at the end of the cycle as before. In prepaid mode, you keep a balance in your account, and the meter deducts the cost of your use as you go.

Some discoms have switched new connections and government buildings to prepaid first, while existing homes stay postpaid for a while. Others offer a small discount on the energy charge to encourage prepaid. Policies change, so check with your discom what mode you are on and whether you can choose.

How recharge works

Recharging is usually done online. Common options are:

  • The discom's own consumer app or website.
  • UPI apps and wallets that support electricity bill payments.
  • Bank net banking under bill payments.
  • Discom cash counters or authorised agents in some areas.

The recharge is credited to your consumer account, not stored on a card in the meter. It normally reflects within minutes, but delays of a few hours can happen. Do not wait until the last moment to top up.

What gets deducted from the balance

Your balance pays for more than the units. It typically covers the energy charge for your slab, a daily share of the fixed charge based on your sanctioned load, electricity duty and any other levies your state applies. That is why the balance drops a little even on days you hardly use power.

Slabs still apply. Most discoms work out the slab on a monthly basis and adjust deductions as your use rises through the month. Alerts and disconnection

The meter and the discom system send messages as your balance falls. Most users get a low balance alert by SMS, app notification or both, often when the balance can cover only a few more days of typical use.

What happens when the balance reaches zero depends on your state's rules. In general terms:

  • Many discoms allow a small grace or emergency credit before cutting supply.
  • Disconnection is often not done at night, on Sundays or on public holidays. The protected hours vary by state.
  • Once you recharge, supply is restored remotely, usually within minutes.
  • Any negative balance is recovered from your next recharge.

Because these rules are set by each state regulator and discom, check your discom's notice or call its helpline for the exact grace period and protected hours.

The "meter runs fast" myth

The most common complaint is that bills jumped after the new meter came. Sometimes there is a genuine fault, but often the reasons are simpler:

  • The old meter was slow. Old electromechanical meters can under-record as they age. A new accurate meter shows the true use.
  • Estimated bills stopped. If meter readers skipped visits, you may have been billed on averages for months. Real readings can be higher.
  • Season change. Many smart meters were installed in spring, right before summer AC and cooler use began.
  • Daily fixed charge visible. In prepaid mode you see the fixed charge leaving every day, which feels new even though you paid it before.

That said, faults do happen. If your use pattern has not changed and the readings still look wrong, ask for a test.

How to check your readings yourself

  1. Read the display. The meter scrolls through screens. Note the cumulative kWh reading and the date.
  2. Compare with the app. The app reading should match the meter display, allowing for a short delay.
  3. Do a switch-off test. Turn off every load in the house at the main switches inside. The meter's pulse light should stop blinking. If it keeps blinking, there may be a leakage or a fault.
  4. Do a known load test. Run one appliance of known wattage, such as a 1,000 watt heater, for exactly one hour with everything else off. The reading should rise by about one unit.
  5. Ask for a check meter. If you still doubt the meter, write to your discom. Many states allow a check meter to be fitted in series, or a lab test, sometimes for a fee that is refunded if the meter is faulty.

A worked example

A family in Lucknow has a 2 kW connection and uses about 200 units a month. With a smart prepaid meter, they keep around one month's worth of usage as balance. In June their use climbs towards 300 units because of a cooler and an inverter AC. They set the app to send a low balance alert, recharge on the day the alert arrives, and check the daily usage graph. It shows the AC running through the night, so they set it to 26 degrees with a timer. Use drops by around 30 units a month. Practical tips

  • Keep a buffer of at least a week of normal use in your balance.
  • Turn on auto-pay or reminders if your discom supports them.
  • Check the daily usage graph once a week. It is the easiest way to spot a faulty fridge or a pump left on.
  • Make sure your sanctioned load matches your real use. A load that is too high raises fixed charges; one that is too low may trigger penalties.
  • Save every recharge receipt until it shows in your account.

Frequently asked questions

Is a smart prepaid meter compulsory?

Policies differ by state and connection type. Some discoms are moving all consumers to prepaid, others make it optional. Check with your discom.

Will my power be cut at night if my balance runs out?

Many states protect night hours and holidays from disconnection, but the rules vary. Your discom's notice gives the exact timing.

Does the new meter charge more per unit?

No. The tariff is set by the state regulator, not by the meter. Some discoms even give a small discount for prepaid.

How do I complain about a faulty meter?

Raise a written complaint through the discom app, website or office and ask for a check meter or lab test. Keep the complaint number.
