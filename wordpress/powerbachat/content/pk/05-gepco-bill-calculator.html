The GEPCO bill calculator on this page is for households supplied by the Gujranwala Electric Power Company. GEPCO serves Gujranwala, Sialkot, Gujrat, Narowal, Hafizabad and Mandi Bahauddin, one of the most densely populated and busiest belts in Punjab. Enter the units from your meter above and you will see the energy charge for each slab, the taxes on top, and an estimated total that should sit very close to your printed bill.

Homes in this region come in every size. There are small family houses in old city mohallas, large new houses in Gujrat and Kharian built with money sent home from abroad, and plenty of homes in Sialkot and Wazirabad that double as small workshops. The bill tells a different story in each, but the maths behind it is the same.

How to use it in two minutes

  1. Find the present reading on last month's bill.
  2. Read your meter today and subtract. That is how many units you have used so far.
  3. Scale to a full month if you are mid-cycle: divide by days so far, multiply by 30.
  4. Enter the number above. Switch on "Protected consumer" only if your last six bills were each at 200 units or below.

The slip shows your estimated payable amount and your average price per unit. Tap the breakdown link to see how many units fall into each slab.

Big houses, empty rooms

A pattern we see often in the GEPCO area is a large house where some of the family lives abroad. Rooms stay closed for months, yet the fridge in the guest kitchen, a second freezer and a few always-on lights keep running. None of these look like much, but together they can add 60 to 100 units a month, all billed at your highest slab. Walk through the house once with fresh eyes. Switch off what nobody uses, and empty the extra freezer when the family is away.

A worked example from Sialkot

A family in Sialkot Cantt reads 260 units used on the 18th day of the cycle. At that pace the month ends around 430 units. The GEPCO bill calculator puts the last 30 units in the 401 to 500 slab. By moving the evening iron and washing machine to the next morning and raising the AC to 26 degrees, they finish at 395 units and stay in the lower slab. The saving is not dramatic in one month, but repeated across a five-month summer it adds up to a useful sum.

Winter fog and the quieter months

From December to February, the GEPCO belt sits under thick fog for days at a time. ACs are off and fans rest, so most households fall back into the first two slabs. These are the months to rebuild protected status if you lost it in summer. Six clean months under 200 units, from roughly October to March, is achievable for many smaller homes. The catch is heating: a single oil-filled radiator left on overnight can undo it. If you need warmth, an electric blanket or the heat mode of an inverter AC uses far fewer units.

Reading the history box on your bill

Every GEPCO bill carries a small table of your units for the previous twelve months. It is the most useful part of the bill and the part most people ignore. It tells you when your usage rises, how big your summer peak is, and whether you have stayed at or under 200 units for six months in a row. Before you buy a new AC, a UPS or a solar system, take a photo of that table. Every sensible decision about your bill starts from it, and any good installer should ask to see it.

Home businesses and the household tariff

Sialkot is known for sports goods, surgical instruments and leather. A lot of small finishing and stitching work happens at home. If machines run on the household meter, they push the family into higher slabs fast. Depending on the load, a separate commercial or industrial connection can be cheaper overall and keeps the household history clean for protected status. The rules depend on your load, so check with your GEPCO subdivision before you decide.

What the calculator does not include

  • Fuel price adjustment. A per-unit charge or refund that changes every month. Read our fuel price adjustment guide.
  • Arrears and late payment surcharge. These depend on your own account.
  • Income tax on larger bills for consumers who are not active taxpayers.
  • Time-of-use pricing. Connections with a sanctioned load of 5 kW or more often have separate peak and off-peak readings.

Practical ways to lower a GEPCO bill

  • Read the meter weekly. It is the cheapest habit that saves money.
  • Replace non-inverter ACs first. The oldest AC in the house usually wastes the most.
  • Check the deep freezer. Old freezers with worn seals run almost constantly.
  • Go solar for big houses. Our solar calculator sizes a system from your bill, and the cost of solar panels page shows what to budget.

Frequently asked questions

Is the GEPCO bill calculator free?

Yes. There is no sign-up and no limit. It runs in your browser and does not store your numbers.

Do Sialkot and Gujranwala have different rates?

No. The whole GEPCO region uses the same uniform tariff, which is also used by LESCO and every other company.

How accurate is the estimate?

The energy charge, GST and electricity duty should match your bill closely. The monthly fuel adjustment and any arrears explain most differences.

Where can I see my GEPCO bill online?

Use the 14-digit reference number from your paper bill on the official online bill service. Then check its units in this calculator.
