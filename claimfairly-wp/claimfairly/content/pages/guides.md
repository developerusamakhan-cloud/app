## How to use these guides

Every guide here answers one question that comes up during a real car accident claim: what your car lost in value, how fault changes the money, what an adjuster's first offer is based on, how long a settlement takes, and how much of it you actually keep. Each one is written in plain English, cites its sources, and shows the math when there is math involved.

If you are new to claims, a good order is:

1. **Right after the crash:** [what to do after a car accident](/what-to-do-after-a-car-accident/), so nothing important is missed in the first week.
2. **While the claim is open:** [how adjusters calculate settlement offers](/how-insurance-adjusters-calculate-settlement/) and [how long a settlement takes](/how-long-does-a-car-accident-settlement-take/).
3. **When an offer arrives:** [whether to accept the first settlement offer](/should-you-accept-the-first-settlement-offer/) and [how to write a demand letter](/how-to-write-a-car-accident-demand-letter/).
4. **Before you sign:** [how attorney fees and medical liens reduce your settlement](/attorney-fees-and-medical-liens/).

## Guides and calculators work together

Reading tells you how a claim is valued. The calculators put your own numbers through the same method. The [settlement calculator](/car-accident-settlement-calculator/) gives a low to high range for an injury claim, the [diminished value calculator](/diminished-value-calculator/) shows what the insurer 17c formula pays for your repaired car, and the [take-home calculator](/settlement-calculator-take-home/) shows what is left after fees, costs and liens. Nothing you type is stored.
