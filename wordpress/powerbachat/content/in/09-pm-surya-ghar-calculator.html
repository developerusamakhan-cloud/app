How the subsidy is worked out

Under the PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana, residential households get central financial assistance for rooftop solar based on system size:

  • ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW.
  • ₹18,000 for the third kW.
  • A maximum of ₹78,000 for systems of 3 kW or more.

So a 1 kW system gets ₹30,000, a 2 kW system ₹60,000, and a 3 kW system ₹78,000. A 5 kW system still gets ₹78,000, because the subsidy stops growing after 3 kW. Some states add their own top-up on top of the central amount, and some special category states and union territories have had different terms, so check your state's portal too.

Using the PM Surya Ghar calculator well

The result is only as good as the units you enter. Use the average of your last twelve bills if you can. If your board bills every two months, as in Tamil Nadu or Kerala, halve the two-month units first. If you mainly want to cut summer bills, use the average of the hottest months instead.

The calculator then estimates the system size by dividing your monthly units by what 1 kW typically produces in India, around 115 to 125 units a month after losses, and rounding up to the nearest half kilowatt.

Who can apply

The scheme is for residential households with their own electricity connection and a roof where panels can be installed. You apply through the national PM Surya Ghar portal, choosing your state and distribution company. Housing societies and resident welfare associations can also get support for systems on common areas, under separate terms.

Key conditions to know before you apply

  • DCR panels are required. The subsidy is for systems using solar panels with domestic content (DCR). These cost more per watt than imported panels. Our solar panel price in India page compares DCR and non-DCR prices.
  • Use a registered vendor. Installation must be done by a vendor empanelled for your discom on the portal.
  • Net metering is part of the process. The discom installs a bi-directional meter, and the system is inspected before commissioning.
  • The subsidy is paid after installation. You pay the vendor first. Once the system is commissioned and verified, the subsidy is credited to your bank account.

Step by step: from application to subsidy

  1. Register on the national portal with your electricity consumer number and mobile.
  2. Apply for rooftop solar and wait for the discom's feasibility approval.
  3. Choose a registered vendor and get the system installed.
  4. Submit the installation details and apply for net metering.
  5. After the net meter is installed and the discom inspects the system, a commissioning certificate is issued.
  6. Submit your bank details. The subsidy is then transferred to your account.

Concessional bank loans have also been available for small residential systems under the scheme. If you need finance, ask your bank about loans linked to PM Surya Ghar.

Documents to keep ready

  • A recent electricity bill showing your consumer number and the name on the connection.
  • Identity proof and, if the connection is not in your name, proof of ownership or the owner's consent.
  • A cancelled cheque or bank passbook page for the account where the subsidy will be paid.
  • Photos of the roof and, later, of the installed system for the commissioning report.

Having these ready before you start makes the portal steps quicker and avoids delays at the inspection stage.

How much can you save?

A 3 kW system produces roughly 350 units a month in most of India, more in sunny states and in the dry season, less in the monsoon. For a home using 300 units a month, that can bring the bill close to zero in many months, with net metering crediting the surplus. The PM Surya Ghar calculator estimates your saving from your own state's slab rates, and the payback period from your share of the cost after subsidy.

Because the subsidy covers a large share of a small system's price, payback for a 2 or 3 kW system is often just a few years. After that, the panels keep producing for twenty-five years or more.

Which size should you choose?

  • Under 150 units a month: 1 to 2 kW is usually enough.
  • 150 to 300 units: 2 to 3 kW, the sweet spot for subsidy per kW.
  • Above 300 units: 3 kW captures the full subsidy. Bigger systems cost more without extra subsidy, but can still pay back well in states with steep tariffs.

Common mistakes

  • Buying non-DCR panels and then finding the system does not qualify.
  • Using a vendor that is not registered on the portal for your discom.
  • Oversizing in a state where net metering credits are limited.
  • Ignoring shade from water tanks or nearby buildings.

Frequently asked questions

How much subsidy do I get for a 3 kW system?

₹78,000 under the central scheme: ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third.

Does the PM Surya Ghar calculator include state subsidies?

No. It uses the central subsidy only. If your state adds a top-up, your net cost will be lower.

Is the subsidy paid to the vendor or to me?

It is credited to your bank account after the system is installed, inspected and commissioned.

Can I install more than 3 kW?

Yes, but the central subsidy stays at the 3 kW maximum. Larger systems make sense if your use is high and your state's net metering rules are favourable.

Does the scheme really give 300 free units?

Not directly. The idea is that a rooftop system of the right size can generate around 300 units a month, which for many homes covers most of the bill. The units come from your own panels, supported by the subsidy that lowers their cost.

Can tenants use the PM Surya Ghar calculator?

Yes, to estimate size and savings. To apply, you need the property owner's consent and a roof you can use, so discuss it with your landlord first.
