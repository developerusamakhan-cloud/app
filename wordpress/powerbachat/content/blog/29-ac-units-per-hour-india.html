The 1.5 ton AC is the most common size in Indian homes, and the most common question about it is simple: how many units will it add to my bill? The honest answer is a range, because consumption depends on the model, the room and the weather outside. This guide gives realistic figures, shows how they turn into monthly units, and explains why the same AC can cost very different amounts in different states.

What "1.5 ton" actually means

Ton is a measure of cooling, not of electricity. A 1.5 ton unit removes roughly 5 kW of heat from a room when running at full capacity. How much electricity it needs to do that depends on its efficiency. An efficient model may need well under 1.5 kW of power to deliver that cooling, while an old, inefficient one may need close to 2 kW.

That is why two ACs of the same size can have very different running costs.

Units per hour: rough ranges

One unit is one kilowatt hour. These are typical figures for real use in a bedroom set to around 24 to 26°C, averaged over an hour once the room has cooled down. They are rough guides, not lab values.

  • 5 star inverter model: roughly 0.7 to 1.1 units per hour.
  • 3 star inverter model: roughly 0.9 to 1.3 units per hour.
  • 3 star fixed speed model: roughly 1.2 to 1.5 units per hour.
  • Old or unrated fixed speed model: roughly 1.6 to 2 units per hour, sometimes more.

On a very hot afternoon in a room under a tin or concrete roof, every model will sit near the top of its range. On a mild night with the door closed and curtains drawn, it will sit near the bottom.

Inverter vs fixed speed

A fixed speed AC runs its compressor at full power until the room reaches the set temperature, then switches off, then switches back on when the room warms up. An inverter AC slows its compressor down instead of stopping it, holding the temperature with much less power once the room is cool.

The difference matters most for long use. For a bedroom AC running all night, an inverter model commonly uses 25 to 35 percent fewer units than a comparable fixed speed one. For an AC that only runs an hour or two at a time, the gap is smaller. Our article on inverter AC vs non inverter AC goes into how the savings stack up over a season.

Star rating and ISEER

In India, AC efficiency is rated by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency using a figure called ISEER, the Indian Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio. It compares the cooling an AC delivers over a typical Indian year with the electricity it uses. A higher ISEER means more cooling per unit.

The star rating is based on ISEER bands. The thresholds are revised from time to time, so a 5 star model from several years ago may only qualify as 3 or 4 star today. When comparing models, look at the ISEER value and the annual energy figure printed on the label rather than the stars alone. The label's yearly units figure assumes a standard number of running hours, so treat it as a comparison tool rather than a forecast of your own use. You can check the current labelling rules on the BEE website.

Monthly units for typical use

Multiply units per hour by hours of use and days in the month. A few common patterns:

  • Night use only, 8 hours a day: roughly 170 to 260 units a month for an efficient inverter model, and 290 to 360 units for a 3 star fixed speed model.
  • Heavy use, 12 hours a day: roughly 250 to 400 units for an inverter model, and 430 to 540 units for a fixed speed one.
  • Light use, 4 hours a day: roughly 90 to 160 units, depending on model and weather.

A single 1.5 ton AC can easily double a typical home's summer consumption. Two ACs can triple it.

Why the slab matters more than the AC

Most Indian tariffs charge more per unit as your monthly use rises. The first slab is cheap, sometimes subsidised, and each slab above costs more. When you add 250 or 300 AC units on top of your normal use, all of those extra units are billed at your highest rates. Some states also have rules where crossing a threshold changes the rate for every unit, not just the extra ones.

So the true cost of running an AC is not the average rate on your bill. It is the rate of your top slab. If you are not sure how slabs work in your state, read our explainer on how an electricity bill is calculated.

Worked example

Consider a family in Maharashtra that normally uses about 150 units a month. In May they run one AC in the bedroom for 8 hours a night.

  • Option A, 3 star fixed speed: about 1.3 units per hour, so 1.3 × 8 × 30 = 312 units. Total for the month: about 460 units.
  • Option B, 5 star inverter: about 0.9 units per hour, so 0.9 × 8 × 30 = 216 units. Total: about 370 units.

Practical ways to cut AC units

  • Set it to 24 to 26°C. Each degree lower adds noticeably to consumption. Use a ceiling fan on low alongside the AC to feel cooler at a higher setting.
  • Clean the filters every two weeks in peak season. Dusty filters make the compressor work harder.
  • Get the outdoor unit in shade with free airflow. An outdoor unit baking in direct afternoon sun runs less efficiently.
  • Seal the room. Close doors, draw curtains during the day, and block gaps under doors.
  • Service it before summer. Low gas or a dirty coil can push units per hour up sharply without you noticing.
  • Use the timer or sleep mode. The room usually stays comfortable for an hour or two after the AC switches off in the early morning.

If you have rooftop solar, running the AC during the day uses your own solar units first. Our solar power price calculator can help you size a system that covers summer cooling.

Choosing a new 1.5 ton AC

If the AC will run more than five or six hours a day for several months a year, an inverter model with a high ISEER is almost always worth the extra price. For a guest room AC used a few days a month, a cheaper model may make more sense. Check the label, compare the annual energy figures, and remember that installation quality, especially pipe length and gas charging, affects efficiency as much as the brand.

Frequently asked questions

How many units does a 1.5 ton AC use in one night?

For 8 hours, roughly 6 to 9 units for an efficient inverter model and 10 to 12 units for a 3 star fixed speed model, depending on weather and room size.

Is a 5 star AC worth it over a 3 star?

If you use the AC for long hours, usually yes, because the saved units come off your most expensive slab. For light use, the payback can be slow.

Does a 1.5 ton AC use 1.5 units per hour?

Not necessarily. Tonnage is cooling capacity, not power. Efficient models often use around 1 unit per hour or less in normal use.

Does running the AC at 18°C cool the room faster?

No. It cools at the same speed but keeps running longer, so it simply uses more units. Set your comfortable temperature from the start.
