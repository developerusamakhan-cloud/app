Quick answer: the best church websites share twelve features: service times at the top, a Plan your visit page, kids and youth information, real photos, beliefs in plain language, a sermon library, a simple events calendar, online giving, small group sign-ups, tap-to-call and maps, fast mobile pages and easy editing for volunteers.

Church websites serve two very different audiences: members who want this week's information, and visitors deciding whether to come at all. The features below serve both, without turning the site into a bulletin board.

For first-time visitors

1. Service times at the top

The single most searched piece of information. Put times and address in the first screen of the homepage.

2. A Plan your visit page

Parking, what to wear, how long the service lasts, what happens for children and where to go when you arrive. This page turns a nervous visitor into a confident one.

3. Kids and youth information

Parents want to know their children will be safe and happy. Explain check-in, age groups and what kids do during the service.

4. Real photos

Your building, your people, your services. Visitors want to picture themselves there.

5. Beliefs in plain words

A short, friendly summary first, with a link to a fuller statement for those who want detail.

For members

6. A sermon library

Recent messages as video or audio, organised by series and topic, with the newest at the top.

7. A simple events calendar

Only upcoming events, easy for staff to add. Old events left online make the whole site look neglected.

8. Online giving

One trusted platform, one clear button, and a short note on how gifts are used.

9. Small groups and serving

A clear list of groups and ministries with a simple sign-up form for each.

For everyone

10. Tap to call and maps

Phone numbers and addresses should open the phone dialler and maps app with one tap.

11. Fast on phones

Most visitors use a phone, often on mobile data. Compress images and avoid heavy sliders and autoplay videos.

12. Easy for volunteers

Whoever updates the site should be able to add a sermon or event in minutes, without breaking the design.

Where most church websites go wrong

  • Long welcome letters before any practical information
  • Internal language visitors do not understand
  • Outdated events and staff pages
  • Giving buried several clicks deep
