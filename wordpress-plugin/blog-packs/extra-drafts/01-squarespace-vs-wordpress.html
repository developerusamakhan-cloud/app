Quick answer: choose Squarespace if you want a polished, low-maintenance site you can run yourself, especially for a creative, hospitality or personal brand. Choose WordPress if you need more features, plan to grow through blogging and SEO, or want to own your site outright and move it whenever you like.

I build on both, and I like both. The trouble starts when people pick one for the wrong reasons: a friend recommended it, or an advert promised a website in an afternoon. Here is how I explain the difference to clients.

The real difference in one sentence

Squarespace is a well-furnished apartment you rent: beautiful from day one, but you cannot knock down walls. WordPress is a house you own: more work to set up, but you can build anything and take it with you.

Design

Squarespace templates are elegant out of the box. Typography, spacing and image galleries look good even if you have no design experience, and the editor makes it hard to break the layout. WordPress can look just as good or better, but the result depends on the theme and the person building it. A custom WordPress design lets you stand out from the many sites that use the same Squarespace templates.

Ease of use

Squarespace wins for beginners. Everything lives in one dashboard and there are no plugins to update. WordPress has a steeper start, but a well-built site with a clear editor and a short video walkthrough is easy to manage. Most of my WordPress clients update their own content every week.

Cost

Squarespace charges a monthly or yearly plan that includes hosting and security. WordPress software is free, but you pay for hosting, any premium plugins and ongoing care. Over a few years the totals are often similar. The difference is what you get for it: Squarespace gives convenience, WordPress gives flexibility. My guide on how much a website costs breaks down the numbers.

SEO and blogging

Both platforms let you edit page titles and descriptions, create clean URLs and submit a sitemap. If your plan is to grow through content, WordPress has the edge: better blogging tools, more control over speed and structured data, and plugins like Yoast or Rank Math that guide you as you write. For a small local business with a handful of pages, Squarespace is perfectly capable.

Ecommerce

Squarespace Commerce works well for small catalogues, digital products and simple subscriptions. For larger stores, complex products or full control over checkout, WooCommerce on WordPress or Shopify are better fits. I compare those two in Shopify vs WooCommerce.

Ownership and moving later

With WordPress you own the files and database and can change host or developer at any time. Squarespace sites cannot be moved to another platform as they are. Blog posts export reasonably well, but pages and design need rebuilding. That is not a reason to avoid Squarespace, just something to know before you invest years of content in it.

Who should choose which

  • Squarespace: photographers, artists, restaurants, coaches, consultants and small service businesses with up to about 20 pages who want minimal upkeep.
  • WordPress: businesses that blog regularly, need bookings, memberships, directories or custom features, or plan to grow a lot.
