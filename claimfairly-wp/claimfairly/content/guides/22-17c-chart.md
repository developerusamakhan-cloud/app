When an insurer offers a diminished value payment, the number often comes from the **17c formula**, a method named after a paragraph in a Georgia class action settlement involving State Farm. It takes your car's value, caps the loss at 10 percent, then cuts it down for damage and mileage. Because every step is fixed, you can predict the result exactly.

We built the 17c diminished value chart below, in three tables, so you can look up what the formula pays without doing the math. They are also a fast way to see the formula's biggest weakness: how quickly it shrinks toward zero. If you want the background on how the formula works, read our guide to [the 17c formula](/17c-formula-diminished-value/). If you just want your own number, use our [diminished value calculator](/diminished-value-calculator/).

## The formula in one line

**Payment = car value × 10% × damage multiplier × mileage multiplier**

| Damage level | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Severe structural damage | 1.00 |
| Major structural and panel damage | 0.75 |
| Moderate structural and panel damage | 0.50 |
| Minor structural and panel damage | 0.25 |
| No structural damage, panels only | 0.00 |

| Mileage | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| 0 to 19,999 | 1.0 |
| 20,000 to 39,999 | 0.8 |
| 40,000 to 59,999 | 0.6 |
| 60,000 to 79,999 | 0.4 |
| 80,000 to 99,999 | 0.2 |
| 100,000 or more | 0.0 |

## What the charts reveal

### 1. The 10 percent cap is the ceiling for every car

No matter how new the car or how bad the crash, the formula never pays more than 10 percent of the value. A $50,000 car with severe frame damage and 5,000 miles still tops out at $5,000.

### 2. Mileage wipes out the payment

At 100,000 miles, every row is zero. Many cars on the road today are reliable well past that mark, and buyers still pay less for one with a crash history. The formula ignores that.

### 3. Cosmetic damage pays nothing

A car with only panel damage gets a zero damage multiplier, so the formula pays nothing, even though a crash still appears on the vehicle history report that buyers check.

### 4. The car's value is the input you can most easily fix

The whole calculation starts from the car's value. If the insurer uses a low value, every number that follows is low. Check it against Kelley Blue Book and Edmunds using your exact trim, options and mileage.

## Is the 17c number what you are owed?

Not necessarily. The 17c method came out of one class action settlement in Georgia. It is not a law that applies in every state, and nothing requires you to accept it. Diminished value is meant to measure the actual drop in market value after the repair. Independent appraisers estimate that using sales data and dealer quotes for cars with and without a crash history, and their results are often higher than the 17c number, especially for newer cars, luxury cars and cars past 100,000 miles.

## How to use these charts in a claim

1. **Find your row and column** to see what the insurer formula will likely offer.
2. **Treat that number as a floor**, not as your full loss.
3. **Get evidence of the real loss.** An independent diminished value appraisal, or written trade-in offers from dealers for your car with its accident history compared with its clean value.
4. **Send a written demand** with the evidence attached. Our [demand letter generator](/demand-letter-generator/) has a diminished value version.
