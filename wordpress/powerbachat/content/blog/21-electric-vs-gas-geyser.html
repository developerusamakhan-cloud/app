Every winter, families in Pakistan face the same question: with gas pressure falling and electricity expensive, how should we heat water? An electric geyser is easy to install and works whenever there is power, but it can add a surprising number of units to the bill. A gas geyser is cheap to run when gas is available, but supply is unreliable in many cities. This guide compares the two honestly and shows how to cut water heating costs whichever you choose.

How much an electric geyser uses

A typical storage electric geyser has a heating element of around 1,500 to 2,000 watts. While it heats, a 2,000 watt element uses one unit every 30 minutes. How many hours it actually heats depends on the tank size, the water temperature you set, how much hot water you use and how well the tank is insulated.

As a rough guide, an electric geyser left switched on all day in winter can use 100 to 200 units a month, sometimes more for a large family. Used carefully, heating only before morning showers, the same geyser might use a third of that. Our guide on what one unit of electricity is shows how to work this out for your own model.

What that costs

Winter is usually when household usage is lowest, so geyser units can be the difference between staying in a lower slab or crossing into the next. Gas geysers: cheap when the gas is there

Where piped gas pressure is good, a gas geyser is usually the cheaper way to heat water. The catch is supply: in many cities, gas pressure drops sharply in winter, exactly when hot water is needed most, and households are left with lukewarm water or none. LPG cylinders are an alternative, but they cost considerably more than piped gas.

Gas geysers also need proper ventilation and regular checks. A poorly installed gas geyser in a closed bathroom is a serious safety risk because of carbon monoxide. Always install them outside or with a proper flue, and service them regularly.

Electric vs gas: side by side

  • Running cost: gas is usually cheaper when piped gas is available; electric is expensive if left on for long periods.
  • Reliability: electric works whenever there is power; gas depends on winter pressure.
  • Safety: electric needs proper earthing and a working thermostat; gas needs ventilation and regular servicing.
  • Installation: electric is simpler; gas needs a gas line or cylinder and a safe location.

Many families use both: gas when pressure is good and electric as a backup, switched on only when needed.

Ways to cut water heating costs

  1. Use a timer on the electric geyser. Heat water for an hour before showers instead of leaving it on all day. This alone can cut its units by more than half.
  2. Lower the thermostat. Water hot enough for a comfortable shower is usually well below the maximum setting.
  3. Insulate the tank and pipes. Old geysers lose heat quickly through thin insulation.
  4. Use an instant geyser for a single bathroom. It heats water only when you use it, though it draws high power while running.
  5. Consider a solar water heater. Where there is roof space, it heats water for free on sunny days and is one of the best investments for winter.

Solar water heaters

A solar water heater uses the sun to heat water in an insulated tank on the roof. In most of Pakistan, winter days are sunny enough for it to provide most of a family's hot water, with an electric backup for cloudy days. It costs more upfront than a geyser but has very low running costs. If you are already thinking about rooftop solar panels, see our solar calculator for the electricity side as well.

A worked example

A family in Rawalpindi used a 2,000 watt electric geyser left on from morning till night in January, and their bill jumped by around 150 units. The next winter, they put the geyser on a timer for an hour in the morning and half an hour in the evening, lowered the thermostat and wrapped the tank in insulation. Their geyser units fell to around 50 a month. Combined with a gas geyser in the kitchen when pressure allowed, their winter bill dropped back into the lower slabs.

Checking your own geyser's units

The easiest way to see what water heating really costs you is to read your meter. Note the reading at night, leave the geyser off until the next night and read it again. Then repeat with the geyser on its usual routine. The difference between the two days is roughly what the geyser uses per day. Multiply by 30 and you have a good monthly estimate, which you can enter in our electricity bill calculator to see its effect on your bill.

Do this once in early winter. It takes two days and often surprises people: an old tank with a faulty thermostat can use far more than the label suggests, because the element keeps switching on to replace heat lost through poor insulation.

Peak hours and water heating

Homes on a time of use meter pay more for units used in the evening peak. If you have this kind of meter, heat water in the morning or late at night and avoid running the geyser between sunset and the end of peak hours. Even on an ordinary meter, heating water before the evening rush means less load on the house wiring when lights, fans and the kitchen are all running. Thermostat and element maintenance

Hard water leaves scale on the heating element, and a scaled element takes longer to heat the same water. Draining the tank once a year and checking the element can restore its efficiency. A thermostat that sticks in the on position is both wasteful and unsafe, so if the water is ever too hot to touch or the tank keeps heating after the light should switch off, have it checked by an electrician before next winter.

Frequently asked questions

How many units does an electric geyser use?

A 2,000 watt electric geyser uses one unit every 30 minutes while heating. Left on all day in winter, it can use 100 to 200 units a month.

Is a gas geyser cheaper than an electric geyser?

Usually yes, when piped gas pressure is good. With LPG cylinders or poor gas supply, the comparison is closer.

Is a timer worth it?

Yes. Heating only before use can cut an electric geyser's consumption by more than half.

Are instant geysers efficient?

They avoid standby losses, but they draw high power while running. They suit single bathrooms with short showers.
