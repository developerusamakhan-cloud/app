Quick answer: choose Wix if you want a small site you can build and edit yourself with hosting included. Choose WordPress if you plan to grow with lots of content, need specific features, or want full ownership of your site. Squarespace suits creatives who want polish out of the box, and Webflow suits design-led businesses that want custom visuals without a heavy plugin setup.

I build on all four platforms, and I am often asked to move clients from one to another. That usually happens because the first choice was made on price alone, or because someone said one platform is "the best". There is no best. There is only the best fit for how you work.

Wix vs WordPress at a glance

  • Setup: Wix is ready in minutes. WordPress needs hosting, a theme and some configuration.
  • Editing: Wix is visual drag and drop. WordPress uses a block editor or a builder like Elementor, which is easy once set up well.
  • Cost: Wix is a monthly subscription including hosting. WordPress software is free; you pay for hosting, premium plugins and maintenance.
  • Ownership: On Wix you rent your site; you cannot move it elsewhere. With WordPress you own the files and can move host any time.
  • Features: Wix has an app market that covers most small business needs. WordPress has tens of thousands of plugins for almost anything.
  • SEO: both cover the basics. WordPress gives more control for content-heavy growth.

When Wix is the right choice

  • You want to make edits yourself, often, without worrying about updates
  • Your site is under about 15 pages and mostly stays that way
  • You need bookings, a small store or a simple members area quickly
  • You do not want to think about hosting, security or backups

A well designed Wix site can look just as professional as anything else. Most Wix sites look "Wix-y" because they use a template as is, not because of the platform.

When WordPress is the right choice

  • Content and Google traffic are central to your growth: blogs, guides, resources
  • You need specific functionality: complex forms, memberships, directories, WooCommerce
  • You want the freedom to change host, developer or design without rebuilding
  • You have or will have someone to handle updates

WordPress runs a large share of all websites, which means plenty of developers, plugins and answers when you need help. I specialise in WordPress development, but I will still tell you when it is overkill.

Where Squarespace fits

Squarespace is the platform I recommend most to photographers, restaurants, artists and small studios. The templates are elegant, typography is lovely by default and the editor keeps you from making a mess. It is less flexible than Wix for apps and less powerful than WordPress for content, but for a beautiful, low maintenance site it is hard to beat.

Where Webflow fits (Webflow vs WordPress)

Webflow is loved by designers because you can build custom layouts and animations visually, with clean code underneath. It has a good CMS for blog posts and case studies, and hosting and security are handled for you. The trade-offs: higher plan costs as you grow, fewer ready-made integrations than WordPress, and ecommerce that is more limited. For a design-led startup or agency site, Webflow is excellent. For a content-heavy business or a store, WordPress usually wins.

What about cost over three years?

Build costs are often similar with a freelancer. The difference is in running costs. Builders like Wix, Squarespace and Webflow bundle hosting and updates into a monthly fee. WordPress is cheaper to host but needs care. If you add a maintenance plan, WordPress and a mid-tier builder plan end up in a similar range. I break down build prices in how much does a website cost.

Can you switch later?

Yes, but it is a rebuild, not a button. Moving from Wix to WordPress means recreating the design and moving content, then redirecting old addresses. I do these moves regularly; the redirects are the part that protects your Google rankings.

My recommendation process

When someone asks me which platform to pick, I ask four questions: Who will edit the site? How much will it grow? Do you need any unusual features? What is your monthly budget for running it? The answers usually make the choice obvious.
