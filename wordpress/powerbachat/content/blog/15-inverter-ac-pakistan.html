The AC is the single biggest reason electricity bills in Pakistan explode every summer. So when it is time to buy a new one, the question is always the same: is an inverter AC really worth the extra money? This guide compares inverter and non-inverter ACs in practical terms, explains how much each uses, and shows how to run either one for fewer units.

How the two types work

A non-inverter AC has a compressor that runs at full speed or stops completely. It cools the room, switches off when the thermostat is satisfied, then switches back on at full power when the room warms up. That on-off cycle wastes energy and causes temperature swings.

An inverter unit varies the compressor's speed. It runs hard at first to cool the room, then slows down to hold the temperature steady. Because it avoids repeated full-power starts and runs gently most of the time, it uses noticeably less electricity over a night, and the room stays at a steadier temperature.

How much does each use?

Real use depends on the room, the heat outside, the thermostat setting and the unit's efficiency. As a rough guide for a 1.5 ton unit in a Pakistani summer:

  • Non-inverter: often around 1.5 to 2 units an hour when the compressor is running, with long running periods in peak heat.
  • Inverter: often around 0.8 to 1.2 units an hour on average over a night, depending on settings and heat.

Over eight hours a night for a month, that difference can add up to 100 units or more. Our guide on what one unit of electricity is shows how to work this out for your own AC.

What that saving is worth

Because Pakistan's tariff charges more for each unit as usage rises, the units it saves come off the top of your bill, where they are most expensive. For a home sitting in the 300 to 500 unit range in summer, saving 100 units a month can reduce the bill by a large amount. Is the extra price worth it?

Inverter models cost more than similar non-inverter units. For an AC that runs many hours a day through a long summer, the extra cost is usually recovered within a season or two from lower bills. For an AC used only occasionally, the payback is slower. In most of Pakistan, where ACs run for five months or more, the inverter type is the better buy.

Choosing the right tonnage

An AC that is too small runs flat out and never quite cools the room. One that is too large cools quickly but cycles more and controls humidity poorly. As a rough guide:

  • 1 ton: a small bedroom.
  • 1.5 ton: a typical bedroom or small lounge, the most common choice.
  • 2 ton: a large lounge or a room with heavy sun exposure.

Top-floor rooms, west-facing rooms and rooms with large windows need more cooling. Ask the dealer to consider your room, not just its area.

Settings that cut units

  • 26 degrees with a fan feels comfortable for most people and uses far less than 18 to 20 degrees.
  • Use the sleep mode or a timer to raise the temperature slightly in the early morning.
  • Close doors and curtains so the AC cools only the room you are in.
  • Clean filters every two to three weeks in summer.
  • Service the AC before May so it starts the season efficient.

ACs and solar

Variable-speed ACs pair well with rooftop solar, because their gentle, steady draw matches solar output during the day. A 5 kW system can run one or two of them on a sunny afternoon. If you are planning both, see our 5kW solar system price page.

Old ACs: repair or replace?

Many homes still have a ten or fifteen year old window or split unit. These older machines often use far more than their label suggests, because worn compressors, leaking gas and dirty coils all reduce efficiency. If an old unit needs a gas refill every season, struggles to cool on hot afternoons or trips the breaker, replacing it usually pays back faster than repeated repairs. If it still cools well, a proper service, with coil cleaning and a gas check, can restore much of its efficiency for a modest cost.

Installation matters as much as the model

A good AC badly installed wastes power. The outdoor unit should sit in shade with space for air to flow, not boxed in behind a grill or against a wall. Copper piping between the indoor and outdoor units should be as short as practical and properly insulated. The indoor unit should be placed where it can circulate air across the room, not tucked into a corner behind a cupboard. Ask the installer to check the gas pressure after installation and to show you how to clean the filters.

What about load-shedding?

During a power cut, an AC is usually the first thing to go, because few home UPS systems can run one. Plan for it: pre-cool the bedroom before the scheduled cut, keep curtains closed, and rely on fans on the UPS during the outage. Protected consumers and ACs

For households near the 200 unit line, a new AC can end protected status in a single month. If you are protected, plan AC use carefully, especially in May. Our guide to the protected consumer rule explains why one heavy month matters so much.

In short, pick the right size, install it well, keep it clean and run it at a sensible temperature. Those four habits save more than any brand choice.

Frequently asked questions

How many units does an inverter AC use in a night?

A 1.5 ton inverter AC often uses around 7 to 10 units over an eight-hour night in summer, depending on heat and settings.

Is an inverter AC worth it in Pakistan?

For ACs that run many hours through the summer, yes. The savings usually recover the extra cost within a season or two.

What temperature should I set?

26 degrees with a ceiling fan is a good balance of comfort and cost.

Does a bigger AC cool faster and save money?

Not usually. An oversized unit cools quickly but cycles more and controls humidity poorly. Choose the tonnage that fits the room.

Does an inverter AC work on a UPS?

Only with a large inverter and battery bank. For most homes, run the AC on grid or solar and keep the UPS for fans and lights.
