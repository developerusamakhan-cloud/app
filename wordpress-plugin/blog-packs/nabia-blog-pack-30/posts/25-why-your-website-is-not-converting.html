Quick answer: websites usually fail to convert because of a vague headline, slow mobile pages, no clear next step, hidden prices, long forms, no real proof, the wrong traffic or a broken form. Fix anything broken first, then work through the list one change at a time and measure.

"We get visitors, but nobody contacts us" is the most frustrating website problem, because it feels invisible. It is rarely one big issue. It is usually several small ones adding up. Here are the eleven I find most often in audits.

1. The form is broken

Fix: submit every form on your site today and confirm the email arrives. I find silently broken forms surprisingly often.

2. The headline does not say what you do

Fix: replace slogans with a plain sentence: what you do, for whom, where.

3. It is slow on phones

Fix: compress images, remove heavy plugins and check hosting. See WordPress speed optimization.

4. There is no clear next step

Fix: choose one main action and make it the most visible button on every page.

5. Prices are hidden

Fix: show prices, starting prices or how pricing works. People who cannot estimate cost often leave.

6. Forms ask too much

Fix: ask only for name, email and message. Get the rest on the call.

7. No real proof

Fix: add reviews with names, photos of real work and logos of clients, close to the call to action.

8. The traffic does not match the offer

Fix: check which searches and campaigns bring visitors. Traffic from the wrong country or the wrong intent will never convert.

9. Service pages are too thin

Fix: give each service its own page with who it is for, what is included, process, price guidance and FAQ.

10. Contact details are hard to find

Fix: phone, email and WhatsApp in the header or footer on every page, with tap-to-call on mobile.

11. The site looks outdated

Fix: a dated design quietly lowers trust. Sometimes a refresh of typography, photos and layout is enough; see web design trends worth using.

Measure before and after

Know your current website conversion rate, change one thing, wait a few weeks, and measure again. That is how you learn what works for your audience.
