Quick answer: a logo can cost anything from $0 with an online generator to $2,500+ with an agency. For most small businesses, a professional logo from an experienced freelance designer lands between a few hundred dollars and about $1,500, depending on how much research, how many concepts and which extra brand pieces you need.

As a graphic designer I get two kinds of logo enquiries. People who want "something quick and simple", and people who have used a cheap logo for two years and now feel it holds them back. The second group always wishes they had started differently, so let me explain what you are paying for.

Logo price ranges and what you get

  • Free to $50 (generators and templates): fast, but not unique. Other businesses may use the same icon, and you often cannot trademark it.
  • $50 to $250 (budget marketplaces): quick concepts, limited research, quality varies a lot. Check that you get vector files and full rights.
  • $300 to $1,500 (experienced freelancers): research into your market, several original concepts, refinement rounds and a proper set of files. This is the sweet spot for most small businesses.
  • $2,500+ (agencies): deeper strategy, workshops, naming help and wide brand systems for larger companies.

What a good logo design service includes

  1. A short discovery. Who your customers are, how you want to be seen, and who your competitors are.
  2. Original concepts. Usually two to three different directions, not three colours of the same idea.
  3. Refinement. Adjusting the chosen direction until it feels right.
  4. Versions. Full colour, one colour, white, a horizontal layout, a stacked layout and a small icon for social profiles and favicons.
  5. Files. SVG, PNG and PDF at minimum, organised clearly.
  6. Ownership. Full rights transferred to you once paid.

Logo or full brand identity?

A logo is one piece. A brand identity adds your colour palette, fonts, image style and templates, so your website, social media, invoices and signage all look like they belong together. If you are launching a new website at the same time, doing both together saves money and gives a much more consistent result.

Signs of a good logo

  • It still works tiny, like a phone app icon or a social profile picture
  • It works in one colour, for stamps, embroidery or dark backgrounds
  • It fits your industry without copying the clichés everyone else uses
  • You can describe it in one sentence

How to brief a logo designer

Share your business name and a one line description, three words that describe how you want customers to feel, competitors you want to look different from, logos you like and dislike, and where the logo will be used most. A good brief saves a whole round of revisions.

Common mistakes

  • Asking family and friends to vote. Ask your customers, or trust the brief.
  • Too much detail. Complex logos turn into blurs at small sizes.
  • Only receiving a JPG. You will need a vector file later, guaranteed.
  • Following a trend so closely the logo looks dated in two years.

How your logo connects to your website

Your logo sets the tone for your whole site: colours, fonts and even the style of photos. That is why I prefer to design branding and website design together when possible. If your site already exists, a refreshed logo plus updated colours can make it feel new without a full rebuild. For bigger changes, see when it is time for a website redesign.
