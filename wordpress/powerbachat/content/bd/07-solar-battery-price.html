Tubular, flat plate and lithium

Tubular lead-acid batteries are the standard for IPS and many solar systems in Bangladesh. They handle daily charging and discharging better than cheaper flat plate batteries, need distilled water topping up, and last a few years with care.

Flat plate lead-acid batteries cost less, but wear out faster with deep daily use. They are best for occasional backup.

Lithium (LiFePO4) batteries cost the most upfront but give far more usable energy per battery, charge faster, need no maintenance and last many more cycles. For daily load-shedding or solar use, they often cost less over their life.

Usable capacity: the number to compare

A battery's label shows its total capacity, but you should only use part of it. Lead-acid batteries last much longer if you use about half their capacity before recharging. Lithium batteries can use around 90 percent. So a 12 V 100 Ah lithium battery gives about as much usable energy as a 12 V 200 Ah tubular battery. When you compare prices, compare usable energy and expected life, not just the amp-hour number on the label.

Choosing the right size

  1. List what must run during a cut: usually fans, lights, a router and phone charging.
  2. Add up the watts. Two fans and a few LED lights come to around 200 to 250 watts.
  3. Decide how many hours you need. Look at your area's typical load-shedding.
  4. Use the IPS calculator to find the battery size that covers those hours with some margin.

Buying a much bigger battery than you need wastes money and energy, because larger lead-acid banks take longer to charge and lose more in the process.

Batteries for solar systems

In a solar system, the battery charges from the panels during the day and powers the house at night or during outages. Solar batteries cycle every day, so durability matters more than in an IPS that only works during cuts. Tubular batteries designed for solar, or lithium batteries, are the right choice. How long batteries last

  • Flat plate: often one to two years with regular use.
  • Tubular: often three to five years with good care.
  • Lithium: often eight years or more with daily cycling.

Heat, deep discharge and low water levels shorten lead-acid life sharply. In Bangladesh's hot months, keeping the battery in a cool, ventilated place makes a real difference.

Cost per year of use

The fairest way to compare batteries is cost per year of useful life. Divide the price by the number of years you expect it to last. A tubular battery that costs much less than a lithium one but lasts a third as long may cost more per year. For daily heavy use, lithium usually wins. For occasional backup, tubular can still be the better value.

Batteries and your electricity bill

An IPS battery recharges from the grid every time power returns. Lead-acid batteries waste a share of that energy as heat, and old batteries waste more. A typical IPS can add 20 to 50 units a month to your bill. Our electricity bill calculator shows what those units cost at your slab. Replacing a worn battery often lowers the bill as well as restoring backup time.

Buying tips that save money

  • Check the manufacturing date. A lead-acid battery that has sat in a shop for many months may have lost some life. Ask for recent stock.
  • Read the warranty carefully. Many batteries have a full replacement period followed by a pro-rata period. Know which applies.
  • Keep the invoice and warranty card. Without them, claims are difficult.
  • Exchange your old battery. Dealers usually give a discount, and old lead-acid batteries must be recycled properly.
  • Match the battery to your IPS or charge controller. Lithium batteries need equipment that supports them.

Following these steps often matters more than a small difference in the solar battery price in Bangladesh between two shops.

Making your battery last

  • Check water levels in tubular batteries monthly and top up with distilled water only.
  • Keep terminals clean and tight.
  • Avoid draining the battery completely on a regular basis.
  • Keep it off the floor, in a cool, ventilated spot, away from sunlight.
  • Do not mix old and new batteries in one bank.

Recycling old batteries

Lead-acid batteries contain lead and acid that are harmful if dumped. Return old batteries to the dealer when you buy a new one, or to an authorised collector. Most dealers accept them and offer a discount, which also keeps them out of informal recycling that can poison soil and water.

Frequently asked questions

What is the solar battery price in Bangladesh?

See the price table at the top of this page for current tubular and lithium battery prices, with the date of the last update.

Is lithium worth the higher price?

For daily use, usually yes, because it gives more usable energy and lasts much longer. For occasional backup, tubular may be better value.

How many batteries does my IPS need?

Most home IPS units use one or two 12 V batteries. Check your IPS's rated battery voltage, then size capacity with the IPS calculator.

Can I use a car battery for an IPS?

It is not recommended. Car batteries are designed for short bursts, not deep daily discharge, and wear out quickly in an IPS.

How do I know my battery needs replacing?

Backup time falls noticeably, the battery heats up while charging, or it needs water much more often. A dealer can test it for you.

Do batteries come with a warranty?

Most branded batteries do, usually with a full replacement period followed by a pro-rata period. Keep the invoice and warranty card.
