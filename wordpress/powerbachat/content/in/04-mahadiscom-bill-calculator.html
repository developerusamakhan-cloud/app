This Mahadiscom bill calculator is for residential consumers of the Maharashtra State Electricity Distribution Company, usually called MSEDCL or Mahadiscom. It covers most of the state, including Pune, Nagpur, Nashik, Aurangabad, Kolhapur, Thane district outside the city areas served by others, and rural Maharashtra. Enter your monthly units above and you will see the slab charges, the fixed charge, electricity duty and an estimated total.

One point first: Mumbai city is not supplied by MSEDCL. BEST, Adani Electricity and Tata Power serve most of Mumbai, each with its own tariff. If your bill carries one of those names, this calculator is not the right one.

How to use the Mahadiscom bill calculator

The calculator takes about a minute once you have a reading. Here is the routine we suggest for a reliable number:

  1. Find the previous reading on your last bill or in the MSEDCL app.
  2. Read the meter now and subtract to get your units so far.
  3. Scale to a full month: divide by the days so far and multiply by 30.
  4. Enter the result. The slip shows each slab, the fixed charge, duty and the estimated bill.

MSEDCL rates in the calculator

A Maharashtra residential bill is built from several parts. The energy charge rises steeply across the slabs. A wheeling charge per unit is added for using the distribution network, and our slab rates include it so you see one combined rate per unit. A monthly fixed charge applies, and electricity duty is charged at a higher percentage than in most states.

[powerbachat_rate_table tariff="in-msedcl" plan="standard" caption="MSEDCL residential rates per month (energy plus wheeling)"]

Because duty is charged as a percentage on top, every rupee of energy charge costs a little more than a rupee by the time it reaches your bill. The steep rise between the first slab and the second is the defining feature of the Maharashtra tariff. Units 101 to 300 cost close to double the first 100, and higher slabs climb again.

Fuel adjustment charge (FAC)

MSEDCL bills often carry a fuel adjustment charge, a per-unit amount that reflects changes in the cost of power purchase. It changes from time to time and can be positive or occasionally zero. Our estimate leaves it out because it varies, so check the FAC line on your own bill. Our guide on how an electricity bill is calculated explains it alongside the other lines.

A worked example from Pune

A family in Kothrud uses about 280 units a month most of the year, rising to 420 in April and May. The Mahadiscom bill calculator shows that at 280 units, their top units sit in the 101 to 300 slab. At 420, a good share moves into the 301 to 500 slab, where the rate is much higher. They decide to keep the summer peak near 300 by running the AC only in the bedroom and using a BLDC fan in the hall. The saving in April and May is large enough to pay for the new fan in one season.

Why the 300 unit mark matters

On the Maharashtra tariff, the jump from the second slab to the third is the most expensive step most families face. If your bill regularly sits just above 300 units, the units above that line cost a great deal more than those below it, with duty on top. Reading the meter in the third week of the month and holding back a little in the last week can keep you inside the cheaper slab.

Lowering an MSEDCL bill

  • Know your top slab. Units above 300 are the most expensive, so they are the ones to save.
  • Use BLDC fans and LED lights. They cut the steady daily load that pushes you across slabs.
  • Put the geyser on a timer. In winter, a geyser left on can easily add 100 units a month.
  • Consider rooftop solar. With Maharashtra's steep slabs, solar pays back faster than in most states. See the 5kW solar system price and the solar subsidy calculator.

Winter, summer and the Deccan climate

Much of MSEDCL's area sits on the Deccan plateau, where summers from March to May are hot and dry and winters are mild. Vidarbha, around Nagpur, gets some of the hottest summer days in India, and AC use there pushes bills deep into the upper slabs. Coastal Konkan is humid for most of the year. Pune and Nashik are milder, and many homes manage the summer with fans and a single AC. Use your own bill history to see which months put you over 300 units, and plan those months more carefully than the rest.

Three-phase connections and higher loads

Larger homes and bungalows often have a three-phase connection with a higher sanctioned load. Their fixed charge is higher than the single-phase figure we use in this estimate, so add the difference shown on your own bill. The slab rates for residential use are the same, so the energy part of the Mahadiscom bill calculator still applies.

Checking the bill for errors

MSEDCL bills include the previous and current readings, often with a photo of the meter taken by the reader. Check that the photo matches your meter and that the reading is not marked as average or estimated. If something is wrong, raise a complaint through the MSEDCL app, website or your sub-division office before the due date, and keep the complaint number. An estimated reading followed by a large catch-up can push one month into very high slabs.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Mahadiscom bill calculator valid in Mumbai?

Not for Mumbai city, which is served by BEST, Adani Electricity and Tata Power. It is valid for MSEDCL areas across the rest of Maharashtra.

Does the calculator include the wheeling charge?

Yes. Our slab rates combine the energy charge and the wheeling charge per unit.

Why is electricity duty so high in Maharashtra?

The duty rate is set by the state government and is higher than in many states. Our estimate includes it.

When were these rates last checked?

Why does my MSEDCL bill show a photo of the meter?

The photo is taken by the meter reader as proof of the reading used for your bill. Compare it with your own meter. If the photo is unclear or shows a different number, raise a complaint before the due date so the reading can be corrected.
