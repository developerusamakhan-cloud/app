The honest answer is: it depends on your injuries more than anything else. A simple property damage claim can wrap up in weeks. An injury claim usually cannot settle until you have finished treatment, and a disputed claim that goes to court can take years.

Here is a realistic timeline, stage by stage.

## Typical timelines

| Type of claim | Usual time to settle |
|---|---|
| Property damage only | 2 to 8 weeks |
| Minor injury, clear fault | 3 to 6 months |
| Moderate injury with therapy | 6 to 12 months |
| Serious injury or disputed fault | 1 to 2 years |
| Lawsuit filed | 1 to 3 years or more |

These are rough ranges, not promises. Your state, insurer and case details all matter.

## Stage by stage

### 1. Reporting and investigation (days to weeks)

You report the claim, the adjuster reviews the police report, photos and statements, and decides on fault. Clear rear-end crashes move fast. He-said-she-said crashes take longer.

### 2. Property damage (weeks)

Repairs or a total loss payout usually happen early and separately from the injury claim. You do not have to wait for your injury claim to settle to get your car fixed.

### 3. Medical treatment (weeks to months)

This is the longest stage for most people, and it is the one you should not rush. Most injury claims settle once you reach **maximum medical improvement**, meaning you have healed or your doctor can predict what future care you need. Settle before that and you may leave money on the table, because a settlement is final.

### 4. Demand and negotiation (1 to 3 months)

You or your lawyer gather records and bills and send a [demand letter](/demand-letter-generator/). The insurer responds, usually with a lower offer, and the back and forth begins.

### 5. Payment (2 to 6 weeks after agreement)

You sign a release, the insurer issues the check, and if you have a lawyer, the money goes through their trust account so fees, costs and liens can be paid. Lien negotiations can add a few weeks.

## What slows a settlement down

- **Ongoing treatment** or an unclear prognosis
- **Disputed fault**
- **Missing paperwork:** records, bills, wage proof
- **Medical liens**, especially Medicare and Medicaid, which have their own processes
- **Damages near the policy limit**, where insurers look more carefully
- **Filing a lawsuit**, which adds discovery, depositions and court schedules

## What speeds it up

- Treat consistently and keep every bill
- Send complete, organized documents at once
- Respond to the adjuster quickly and in writing
- Know your numbers before you negotiate. Run the [settlement calculator](/car-accident-settlement-calculator/) so you can recognize a reasonable offer when it arrives

## Watch the deadline

While you wait on treatment and negotiation, your state's statute of limitations keeps running. It ranges from 1 year (Tennessee, for most injury claims) to 6 years in a few states. Check yours in the [state rules table](/states/). If the deadline is getting close and you have not settled, talk to an attorney about filing to protect your claim.
