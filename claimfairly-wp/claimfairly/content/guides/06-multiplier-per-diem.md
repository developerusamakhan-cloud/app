There is no bill for sleepless nights, missed soccer games or the fear you feel merging onto the highway now. But pain and suffering is a real part of an injury claim, and there are two common ways to turn it into a number: the **multiplier method** and the **per diem method**.

## The multiplier method

**Pain and suffering = medical expenses x a multiplier (usually 1.5 to 5)**

The multiplier rises with the seriousness of the injury:

| Injury | Typical multiplier |
|---|---|
| Minor: strains, bruises, a few weeks of care | 1.5 to 2 |
| Moderate: months of therapy, missed work | 2 to 3 |
| Serious: fractures, injections, surgery | 3 to 4 |
| Severe: permanent injury or disability | 4 to 5 |

**Example:** $12,000 in medical bills for a moderate injury. $12,000 x 2 to 3 = **$24,000 to $36,000**.

**Why insurers like it:** it ties pain to something measurable. **Where it falls short:** if you had a painful injury but little expensive treatment, the result can be unfairly low.

## The per diem method

**Pain and suffering = daily rate x number of days in recovery**

The daily rate is often based on your daily earnings, on the idea that dealing with pain for a day is at least as hard as working a day.

**Example:** you earn $52,000 a year, about $200 per working day, and it took 150 days to recover. $200 x 150 = **$30,000**.

**Why it helps:** it reflects how long you suffered, not just what the treatment cost. **Where it falls short:** it breaks down for permanent injuries, and adjusters often push back on the daily rate.

## Which one should you use?

| If your claim looks like this | Try this method |
|---|---|
| Big medical bills, quick recovery | Multiplier |
| Small bills, long recovery | Per diem |
| Permanent injury | Multiplier |
| Both methods land close | Use either and say so |

You do not have to pick only one. Many people calculate both, see where they land and use the range to set a reasonable demand. Our [pain and suffering calculator](/pain-and-suffering-calculator/) does exactly that.

## Making your number believable

Adjusters do not pay for pain they cannot see. The evidence that helps most:

- **Medical records** that describe pain levels and limits, not just diagnoses
- **A pain journal** with short daily notes
- **Photos** of injuries, casts and equipment
- **Things you missed:** work, trips, family events, workouts
- **Statements** from family or coworkers about how you changed

Specific beats dramatic. "I couldn't lift my daughter for eight weeks" is more convincing than "my life was ruined."

## Things that can limit pain and suffering

- **No-fault states** may require a serious injury before you can claim it.
- **Some states cap** non-economic damages in certain types of cases.
- **Shared fault** reduces it like any other damages.
- **Policy limits** cap the total, no matter what the pain is worth.
