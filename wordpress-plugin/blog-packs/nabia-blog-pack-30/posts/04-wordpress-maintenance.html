Quick answer: WordPress maintenance means updating WordPress, your theme and plugins, taking backups, checking security, keeping the site fast and testing that forms and checkout still work. Do the critical tasks weekly, the checks monthly and a bigger review a few times a year, or hand it to someone with a maintenance plan.

WordPress is free, flexible and runs a large share of all websites (new to it? Read what is a WordPress website). The price of that flexibility is that you, not the platform, are responsible for keeping everything up to date. Here is the routine I use for client sites.

The schedule

Weekly tasks

Update core, themes and plugins

Most WordPress hacks use known holes in outdated plugins. Update one thing at a time and look at the site afterwards. For stores and busy sites, test updates on a staging copy first. I explain safe updating in how to update WordPress plugins without breaking your site.

Check backups

A backup you have never checked is a hope, not a backup. Make sure it ran, that it is stored off your server, and that it includes both files and database. More in WordPress backup: how to do it properly.

Security scan

Use a security plugin or your host's scanner to look for malware and unexpected file changes.

Monthly tasks

  • Submit every form and confirm the email arrives
  • Place a test order if you sell online
  • Run your homepage through Google PageSpeed Insights and note the score
  • Check Google Search Console for errors and security issues
  • Delete plugins and themes you no longer use
  • Clean spam comments and old revisions from the database

Quarterly tasks

  • Review content: prices, team, services, opening hours
  • Remove user accounts that no longer need access
  • Test restoring a backup to a staging site
  • Check for broken links

Yearly tasks

  • Make sure your server runs a supported PHP version
  • Review your hosting plan: is it still fast enough?
  • Check whether your theme is still maintained by its developer
  • Renew your domain and confirm auto-renew is on

Tools that make it easier

A good backup plugin or host-level backups, a security plugin with a firewall, an uptime monitor that emails you when the site goes down, and a caching plugin. Many managed WordPress hosts include several of these.

Mistakes I see most

  • Updating everything at once on a live store, then not knowing what broke it
  • Backups stored only on the same server as the site
  • Abandoned plugins that have not been updated in years
  • Admin accounts called "admin" with weak passwords
  • Ignoring a "PHP update required" warning until the host forces it

DIY or a maintenance plan?

If you enjoy this and have an hour a month, you can absolutely do it yourself with the schedule above. If your site brings in leads or sales and you would rather not think about it, a plan is cheaper than an emergency fix. Read WordPress website maintenance plans: what to look for to compare options.
