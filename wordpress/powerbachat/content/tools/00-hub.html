These converters answer the everyday electrical questions that come up when you buy an appliance, size a generator, pick a battery or compare air conditioners. Each one does the maths both ways as you type, shows the formula it used, and has a reference table you can read without the calculator.

They work for homes on 120 volt supplies, as in the United States and Canada, and on 230 volt supplies, as in the United Kingdom, Europe, Australia and South Asia. Where the answer depends on the circuit, such as single phase or three phase, or on the power factor of a motor, you can set it in the converter.

How we keep these accurate

Every converter uses the standard definitions: one horsepower is 745.7 watts, one ton of cooling is 12,000 BTU per hour, one BTU per hour is 0.293 watts, and power in watts equals volts multiplied by amps, adjusted for power factor and three phase circuits where needed. The live calculator and the tables run on the same formulas, so the two always agree.

These tools are for estimates and planning. Wiring, breakers and fuses must follow your local electrical code, so ask a licensed electrician before you change a circuit.

If you are working out what electricity costs rather than how much power something draws, our bill calculators for Pakistan, India and Bangladesh turn units into a full bill.
