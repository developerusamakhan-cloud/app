For many families in Dhaka, the first summer with an air conditioner comes with a nasty surprise: the monthly bill doubles, sometimes more. The AC electricity bill depends on a few things you can control and a few you cannot. This guide explains how many units a 1 ton or 1.5 ton AC uses, why the slab system makes those units expensive, and what actually brings the cost down.

How many units does an AC use?

An AC's power draw depends on its size, its efficiency and how hard it has to work. As a rough guide for a typical split AC:

  • 1 ton: around 0.9 to 1.2 kilowatts while the compressor runs.
  • 1.5 ton: around 1.3 to 1.7 kilowatts while the compressor runs.

The compressor does not run every minute. Once the room is cool, a non-inverter AC switches on and off, and an inverter AC slows down. In a Dhaka summer, with hot nights and high humidity, the compressor often runs 60 to 80 percent of the time, more in a top floor flat or a room with a west facing window.

One unit is one kilowatt for one hour. So a 1.5 ton AC drawing 1.5 kilowatts for 8 hours a night, with the compressor working about 70 percent of the time, uses roughly 1.5 × 8 × 0.7, which is about 8.4 units a night. Over 30 nights, that is around 250 units. A 1 ton AC used the same way comes to around 170 units. See what one unit of electricity means.

Why the slabs matter more than the AC

Bangladesh's residential tariff is stepped. The first units of the month are charged at a low rate, and each slab above costs more per unit. The rates are set by the regulator, the Bangladesh Energy Regulatory Commission, and they change from time to time, so check the current figures in our electricity tariff guide.

Here is the important part. Your AC's units are added on top of everything else in the house, so they land in the highest slabs. A family that uses 200 units without an AC and 450 units with one is not paying an average rate for the AC; it is paying the top rates for most of those extra 250 units. Crossing 400 units is where the step is steepest.

Temperature setting: the cheapest saving

Every degree you lower the thermostat makes the compressor work longer. The government has in past summers asked people to keep ACs at 25 degrees or above, and that is sensible advice for the bill as well as for the grid. At 25 or 26 degrees with a ceiling fan on low, most people feel just as comfortable as at 20 degrees without a fan, and the AC uses noticeably fewer units.

A few more habits help:

  • Use dry mode on humid nights when the temperature is not extreme. Much of Dhaka's discomfort is moisture.
  • Use the sleep or timer function so the AC eases off in the early morning when it is cooler.
  • Close curtains on sunny windows during the day so the room does not soak up heat before you switch on.
  • Keep the door shut. Cooling a corridor or a second room through an open door wastes a lot of units.

Inverter AC: when it pays off

An inverter AC adjusts its compressor speed instead of switching fully on and off. In long daily use, such as 6 to 10 hours a night through the summer, it commonly uses something like 25 to 40 percent fewer units than a similar non-inverter model.

The saving is largest when the AC runs for long hours and when your extra units fall in the expensive slabs, which is exactly the situation of most Dhaka households. If you use the AC only an hour or two a day, the higher purchase price takes longer to recover. Our article on inverter vs non-inverter ACs goes through the numbers in more detail.

Servicing and the outdoor unit

A neglected AC can use much more power for the same cooling. Clean the indoor filter every two to four weeks during heavy use; it takes five minutes. Have a technician service the unit before summer, cleaning the coils and checking the gas pressure. Low gas makes the compressor run almost nonstop while the room never quite cools.

The outdoor unit needs air. If it sits in a narrow balcony cage packed with clutter, or in direct afternoon sun with no shade, it struggles. Give it space on all sides, and if possible a simple shade above it that does not block airflow.

Worked example: a 1.5 ton AC in a Dhaka flat

Karim's family lives in a two bedroom flat in Dhanmondi. In the cooler months they use about 200 units a month for fans, lights, fridge, TV, water pump and a rice cooker. In May they install a 1.5 ton non-inverter AC in the main bedroom.

  1. AC use: 8 hours a night at 22 degrees. With the compressor working most of the time, the AC uses around 9 units a night, or about 270 units a month.
  2. New total: 200 + 270 = 470 units. The family has crossed the 400 units line, and every unit above it is charged at the top residential rate.
  3. Bill effect: using the table above, compare the bill at 200 units with the bill at about 470 units. The difference is what the AC really costs them each month, and it is far more than double the per unit rate they were used to.

Now they change three things: they set the AC to 26 degrees with the fan on, use the timer to switch it off at 5 am, and clean the filter every fortnight. AC use drops to roughly 6 units a night, about 180 units a month. Their total becomes 380 units, just under the 400 line.

The difference between 470 and 380 units is only 90 units, but they were the most expensive 90 units of the month. On the table, the bill drop is clearly bigger than 90 units at an average rate would suggest. You can try your own numbers in the electricity bill calculator, or use the DESCO bill calculator if you are a DESCO customer.

If they later replace the AC with an inverter model, the same habits could bring AC use down further, possibly to around 120 to 150 units a month. That is when an inverter model starts to justify its price.

Quick ways to keep the AC electricity bill down

  • Set 25 to 26 degrees and use a ceiling fan with it.
  • Cool one room with the door closed.
  • Clean the filter often and service the unit before summer.
  • Watch your meter in the last ten days of the month if you are close to 400 units.
  • Choose an inverter AC if you use it for long hours.
  • Cut other waste too; our guide to reducing your summer bill covers fans, pumps and standby loads.

A sensible AC electricity bill is not about doing without cooling. It is about keeping the AC's units out of the most expensive slab where you can.

Frequently asked questions

How many units does a 1 ton AC use in a month?

Used about 8 hours a night through a Dhaka summer, a 1 ton non-inverter AC typically uses roughly 150 to 200 units a month. Higher settings, a well shaded room and an inverter model can bring that down a lot.

Does a 1.5 ton AC cost much more to run than a 1 ton?

It draws more power while running, but in a room that is too large for a 1 ton unit, the smaller AC runs nonstop and may not cool properly. Match the size to the room, then control the hours and temperature.

Is 25 degrees really enough in Dhaka's heat?

For most people, yes, especially with a ceiling fan on low and dry mode on humid nights. Moving air makes 25 or 26 degrees feel cooler than the number suggests.

Why did my bill rise so much more than my AC's units?

Because the AC's units sit on top of your normal use and fall into the highest slabs, often above 400 units. Those units cost more each than the ones at the start of the month.
