DCR and non-DCR panels

This is the most important distinction in the Indian market. DCR stands for domestic content requirement: panels whose solar cells as well as modules are made in India. Non-DCR panels use imported cells, even if the module is assembled here.

  • DCR panels are required for the PM Surya Ghar residential subsidy. They cost more per watt.
  • Non-DCR panels are cheaper per watt and widely used for commercial systems and homes that do not claim the subsidy.

For most homes, DCR panels plus the subsidy work out cheaper than non-DCR panels without it, at least up to 3 kW. Above that, the subsidy stops growing, so the calculation can change. Our solar subsidy calculator shows the numbers.

Who 1 kW and 2 kW systems suit

Small systems are a good fit for more homes than people think. A 1 kW system suits a small flat or house using around 100 to 130 units a month, or a family that simply wants to cut a slice off a bigger bill. A 2 kW system suits a typical home without heavy AC use, at around 200 to 250 units a month. Both get generous subsidy per kilowatt, so the share you pay is small. They also need little roof space: about 6 to 8 square metres per kilowatt.

If your use is likely to grow, for example with a new AC, ask the vendor for an inverter that can take more panels later. That keeps the upgrade cheap.

Warranties worth checking

  • Product warranty: covers manufacturing defects in the panel, often ten years or more for good brands.
  • Performance warranty: promises a minimum output after many years, commonly 25 years or more.
  • Inverter warranty: usually shorter, often five years or more, sometimes extendable.
  • Workmanship warranty: from the installer, covering wiring, structure and leaks.

Ask for every warranty in writing, with the name of the company that honours it in India.

Panel technology in plain words

  • Mono PERC: the standard technology for the last few years, reliable and widely available.
  • TOPCon (N-type): newer, usually slightly more efficient and better in heat, increasingly common.
  • Bifacial: collects some light from the back, useful on raised structures over a light-coloured terrace.

In practice, the difference between good brands is smaller than the difference between a good and a poor installation. Choose a brand your installer can supply with proper paperwork and warranty, then focus on the inverter and the fitting.

What else goes into a system's price

Panels are now well under half the cost of a home system. The inverter, mounting structure, cables, protection devices, labour and net metering paperwork make up the rest. That is why two quotes with the same panels can differ widely. Our solar installation cost calculator breaks the full cost down by size.

How to judge a quote

  1. Ask for the exact panel model, wattage and whether it is DCR.
  2. Compare the per watt figure with the table above.
  3. Check the inverter brand and its local service.
  4. Ask for warranty terms in writing: product, performance and workmanship.
  5. Confirm the vendor is registered on the national portal if you want the subsidy.

Buying panels yourself versus through an installer

Some people buy panels directly from a distributor to save money, then hire a local electrician to fit them. It can work for off-grid setups, but for a subsidised grid-connected system it usually causes problems: the vendor must be registered, the panels must be DCR with proper documents, and the installation must pass the discom's inspection. Buying the complete system from a registered vendor is simpler and protects your subsidy.

Why prices change

Indian panel prices follow global cell and wafer prices, duties and policy on imported cells, and local demand. DCR prices are also shaped by how much domestic cell capacity is available. Prices often firm up before summer, when demand peaks, and soften later in the year. A small change in panel price makes only a modest difference to the total system cost, so it is rarely worth delaying a good installation for it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the solar panel price in India per watt?

See the price table at the top of this page. It lists DCR and non-DCR panels with the date of the last update.

What is the 1 kW solar panel price in India?

See the 1 kW row in the cost table, which shows the installed price for a complete system, the ₹30,000 subsidy and your share.

Are DCR panels better?

Not necessarily better in performance, but they are required for the residential subsidy. Choose DCR if you plan to claim it.

Which brand is best?

All major brands make reliable panels. Pick one your installer supports with proper warranty and paperwork.

How many panels do I need for 1 kW?

About two panels of 540 to 550 watts. With smaller panels you need more.

Do panel prices include installation?

No. The price table shows panels only. Installed system costs, including inverter, structure and fitting, are in the cost table.

How long do solar panels last in India?

Good panels typically carry performance warranties of 25 years or more, and many keep working well beyond that with basic cleaning.
