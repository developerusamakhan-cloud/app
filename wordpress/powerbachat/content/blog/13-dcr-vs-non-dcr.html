Anyone shopping for rooftop solar in India soon meets two confusing labels: DCR and non-DCR. Installers quote both, the prices differ noticeably, and the choice decides whether you get the government subsidy. This guide explains DCR vs non-DCR panels in plain language: what the terms mean, why one costs more, and how to pick the right one for your home.

What DCR means

DCR stands for domestic content requirement. A DCR panel uses solar cells made in India, assembled into modules made in India. A non-DCR panel may be assembled in India, but its cells are imported. The cells are the core of a panel, so the DCR label tells you where the most important part was made.

Government schemes that support rooftop solar, including the PM Surya Ghar residential subsidy, require DCR panels. The aim is to build India's own cell manufacturing.

Why DCR panels cost more

Domestic cell production is still growing, and imported cells have been cheaper. That difference shows up in the panel price: DCR panels usually cost noticeably more per watt than non-DCR panels of similar wattage. You can see today's rates side by side on our solar panel price in India page.

Do they perform differently?

This is the question installers hear most, and the honest answer is reassuring. Not in any way that matters for most homes. Both DCR and non-DCR panels come in mono PERC and newer TOPCon technologies, with similar efficiency and warranties from good brands. The difference is mainly where the cells come from and what that does to price and subsidy eligibility. Choose on brand reputation, warranty and installation quality, not on DCR status alone. A well-installed panel of either type will outperform a poorly installed one for its whole life.

The subsidy changes the maths

The central subsidy for home rooftop solar is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, up to ₹78,000. It is only available with DCR panels. For small systems, the subsidy is much larger than the extra cost of DCR panels, so DCR plus subsidy is clearly cheaper:

Above 3 kW, the subsidy stops growing while the DCR premium keeps adding up with every extra kilowatt. For large systems, the gap narrows, and some buyers choose non-DCR panels and skip the subsidy, especially when they want a particular high-efficiency model.

DCR vs non-DCR: which should you choose?

  • Home system up to about 3 kW: DCR panels with the PM Surya Ghar subsidy are almost always the better deal.
  • Home system of 4 to 10 kW: compare both quotes. DCR with the capped subsidy is often still competitive, but check the numbers.
  • Commercial or off-grid systems: non-DCR panels are common, because the residential subsidy does not apply.
  • Housing societies: check the terms for common-area systems, which have their own rules.

How to make sure your panels really are DCR

  1. Ask for the exact panel model and the manufacturer's DCR certificate before you pay.
  2. Check that the model appears on the approved list used by the subsidy portal.
  3. Make sure the invoice names the DCR model and serial numbers.
  4. Use a vendor registered on the national portal for your discom.

If a quote is far below others for the same size, the panels may be non-DCR. That is not wrong in itself, but it means no subsidy, so compare the full cost after subsidy rather than the headline price.

Common mistakes

  • Buying non-DCR panels and then applying for the subsidy.
  • Assuming "made in India" on the module means DCR. Only domestically made cells qualify.
  • Comparing a DCR quote before subsidy with a non-DCR quote, which makes DCR look expensive.
  • Choosing a large system purely for the subsidy, which stops at 3 kW.

A worked example

A family in Lucknow wants a 3 kW system. One installer quotes non-DCR panels at a lower price. Another quotes DCR panels for more. At first glance the non-DCR system looks cheaper. But the DCR system qualifies for the ₹78,000 central subsidy, and after it is credited, the family's share is clearly lower than the non-DCR quote. In the DCR vs non-DCR comparison for a home system of this size, DCR wins comfortably once the subsidy is counted.

A different family in Pune wants 8 kW to cover two ACs and a home office. Their DCR quote gets the same ₹78,000, but the premium on eight kilowatts of DCR panels is larger. Warranty and after-sales service

Whichever type you choose, the warranty and the vendor's service matter more over 25 years than a small difference in panel price. Ask for the product warranty, the performance warranty and the inverter warranty in writing, and check which company in India honours each one. A vendor who will still answer the phone in five years is worth more than one who offers the lowest quote today.

What about future prices?

As India adds more cell manufacturing capacity, the price gap between DCR and non-DCR panels is expected to narrow, though nobody can say exactly when. For a home buyer today, the decision should rest on today's prices and today's subsidy. A good system installed now starts saving immediately, which usually outweighs waiting for a small future price change.

Questions to ask your installer

  • Is the panel DCR, and can I see the certificate and the model's listing?
  • What is my total cost after the central subsidy, and when will the subsidy be credited?
  • Which inverter will you use, and who services it locally?
  • Who handles the net metering application and inspection?

Clear answers to these four questions settle most DCR vs non-DCR decisions quickly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between DCR and non-DCR panels?

DCR panels use solar cells made in India; non-DCR panels use imported cells. Only DCR panels qualify for the residential subsidy.

Are DCR panels better quality?

Not necessarily. Quality depends on the brand and model. Both types include reliable options.

Can I get the PM Surya Ghar subsidy with non-DCR panels?

No. The residential subsidy requires DCR panels.

Do DCR panels need different inverters?

No. DCR and non-DCR panels work with the same on-grid and hybrid inverters, as long as the system is designed for the panel wattage and voltage.

Is DCR worth it for a 5 kW system?

Often yes, but compare both quotes after subsidy. See the 5kW solar system price page.
