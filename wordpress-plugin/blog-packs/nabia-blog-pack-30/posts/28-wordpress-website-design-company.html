Quick answer: when choosing a WordPress website design company or freelancer, compare live work, who actually does the work, process, what the price includes, ownership of your domain and site, speed and security practices and support after launch. Agencies bring bigger teams at higher prices; experienced freelancers bring direct contact at lower cost.

Searching for a WordPress website design company returns everything from large agencies to solo designers with a polished site. All of them can be good. Here is how to compare them fairly.

1. Live work, not only screenshots

Ask for three live sites similar to yours. Open them on your phone: speed, clarity, forms. Screenshots hide performance problems.

2. Who does the work?

Agencies sometimes sell with a senior team and deliver with juniors or subcontractors. Freelancers usually do everything themselves. Ask directly who will design, build and support your site.

3. The process

Look for discovery, a sitemap, a clickable prototype, development on a staging site, testing and training. A clear process means fewer surprises.

4. What the price includes

Pages, revisions, copywriting help, images, SEO basics, training, support. Put quotes side by side and compare scope, not just totals.

5. Ownership

Your domain, hosting account, website files and admin login should belong to you. Avoid providers who keep sites on their own accounts with no exit plan.

6. Speed and security

Ask what theme or builder they use, how they handle caching, image optimisation, backups and updates. A good answer is specific.

7. Support after launch

A website needs care. Ask about maintenance options, response times and costs. See WordPress maintenance plans: what to look for.

Agency vs freelancer

  • Choose an agency when you need a large team, many services at once or strict corporate processes.
  • Choose a freelancer when you want direct communication, a focused budget and one person who knows your site inside out.

Red flags

  • A quote before any questions about your business
  • No live examples
  • Locked-in hosting or ownership
  • No plan for updates and backups
