A 5 kW system is the size most Pakistani families end up choosing, so the 5kW solar system price in Pakistan is one of the most searched questions in the market. It is big enough to run fans, lights, a fridge and one or two inverter ACs during the day, and small enough to fit on the roof of a typical 5 to 10 marla house. This page covers what it costs today, what it can run, how much it produces and how quickly it pays back.

What is inside a 5 kW system

  • Panels: about 9 panels of around 585 watts each, giving just over 5 kW. Check today's per watt rates on our solar panel price page.
  • Inverter: a 5 kW on-grid inverter, or a 6 kW hybrid inverter if you want batteries now or later.
  • Structure: usually a raised frame of galvanised steel, enough for nine panels in one or two rows.
  • Protection: DC and AC breakers, surge protection and proper earthing.
  • Roof space: roughly 25 to 30 square metres, depending on layout and shading.

How many units does a 5 kW system produce?

In most of Pakistan, a well-installed 5 kW system produces somewhere around 600 units a month on average across the year, according to the table above. Output is higher on long clear summer days and lower in foggy or cloudy winter weeks, particularly in central and upper Punjab. Dust also reduces output, so regular cleaning matters.

Expect the best months to be March to June, when days are long and the air is relatively clear, and the weakest to be December and January. For most homes, that means a 5 kW system can cover a large share of a summer bill of 500 to 700 units, provided much of the use happens during daylight.

What can a 5 kW system run?

During a sunny day, a 5 kW system can comfortably run:

  • One or two 1.5 ton inverter ACs
  • Several ceiling fans and LED lights
  • A fridge and a freezer
  • A water pump, a washing machine and an iron, if not all at once with both ACs

On an on-grid system without batteries, everything runs from the grid at night, and the system does not work during load-shedding. If you need power during outages, you will need a hybrid inverter and batteries.

On-grid, hybrid or off-grid?

On-grid is the cheapest and simplest. It reduces your bill during the day and can export spare power, but switches off during load-shedding for safety.

Hybrid adds batteries and a hybrid inverter. It keeps essentials running during outages and can store some daytime power for the evening. It costs noticeably more, mostly because of the batteries.

Off-grid has no grid connection at all and needs a much bigger battery bank. It suits remote homes without reliable supply, but is rarely the best value in cities.

Payback: a worked example

Take a family in Lahore using 600 units a month in summer and 250 in winter. Without solar, their summer bills sit in the higher slabs, so every unit solar replaces is an expensive one. With a 5 kW system running most of their daytime load, their summer bills fall sharply and their winter bills fall too, though by less.

Use the electricity bill calculator to compare the bill at 600 units with the bill at, say, 200 units, and at 250 units with the bill near zero. Add up the monthly savings over a year and divide the system cost by that figure. For a family like this, the 5kW solar system price in Pakistan is typically recovered within a few years, after which the panels keep producing for two decades or more. The exact figure depends on how much of your use happens in daylight.

Net metering, net billing and a 5 kW system

A 5 kW system often produces more than the house uses at midday. That extra can be exported to the grid if you have a bi-directional meter. How exported units are valued has changed under the move from net metering to net billing, and newer arrangements credit exports at a lower rate than before. That makes self-consumption more valuable: running the AC, pump and washing machine during sunny hours gets more from every panel. Read our net metering vs net billing guide for the details.

From order to switch-on

A straightforward 5 kW on-grid installation usually takes one to three days on the roof once materials arrive. Structure and panels go up first, then the inverter, cabling and protection. Applying for a bi-directional meter, if you want one, takes longer and depends on your distribution company's process. Ask your installer whether they handle the paperwork, what it costs, and how long it typically takes in your area.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying on panel price alone. The inverter and installation matter more for long-term performance.
  • Ignoring shade. A water tank or a neighbour's wall shading one panel in the afternoon can cut the output of a whole string.
  • Undersized cables. Thin cables waste power and can overheat.
  • No earthing. It is a safety requirement, not an extra.
  • Oversized batteries. Batteries are expensive and wear out. Size them for real outage hours.

Frequently asked questions

What is the 5kW solar system price in Pakistan today?

See the 5 kW row in the table above for the current installed range of a complete on-grid system. Hybrid systems with batteries cost more.

Can a 5 kW system run an AC?

Yes. On a sunny day it can run one or two 1.5 ton inverter ACs alongside normal household loads.

How many panels does a 5 kW system need?

About nine panels of around 585 watts each. With smaller panels you need more.

Does a 5 kW on-grid system work during load-shedding?

No. On-grid inverters shut down when the grid is off. You need a hybrid inverter with batteries for backup.
