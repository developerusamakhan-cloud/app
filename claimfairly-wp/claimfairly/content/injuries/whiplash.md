Whiplash is the most common injury in rear-end crashes. Your head snaps forward and back, straining the muscles and ligaments in your neck. It often does not hurt much at the scene, then shows up as stiffness, headaches and pain a day or two later.

Insurers see whiplash claims every day, and many treat them skeptically because the injury does not show up on an X-ray. That makes documentation especially important.

## Typical treatment and recovery

Treatment usually includes rest, over the counter pain relief, ice or heat, and physical therapy. Many people improve within a few weeks to a few months. Some have neck pain or headaches that last much longer.

| Recovery pattern | How it usually affects the claim |
|---|---|
| A few weeks, minimal treatment | Minor: lower multiplier, smaller claim |
| Two to four months of physical therapy | Moderate: multiplier around 2 to 3 |
| Pain lasting six months or more, injections, specialist care | Can move toward serious, with strong medical support |

## What raises the value of a whiplash claim

- **Seeing a doctor within a day or two** of the crash
- **Consistent physical therapy** with notes on pain levels and range of motion
- **Imaging or specialist findings**, such as an MRI showing disc involvement
- **Clear fault**, like a rear-end collision with a police report
- **Documented impact on work and daily life**

## What lowers it

- A delay of weeks before your first visit
- Gaps in treatment or stopping therapy early
- Prior neck problems in your medical history (these can still be claimed if the crash made them worse)
- Low visible damage to the cars, which adjusters sometimes use to argue the impact was minor

## Whiplash in no-fault states

In no-fault states like New York, Florida and Michigan, your own PIP pays your medical bills first. To claim pain and suffering from the other driver, the injury usually has to meet a serious injury threshold, which many whiplash cases do not. Check your state in the [state rules table](/states/).
