Our IESCO bill calculator gives households in Islamabad, Rawalpindi, Attock, Jhelum and Chakwal a quick, honest estimate of the next electricity bill. Type in the units from your meter and you get the slab-by-slab energy charge, the taxes that sit on top, and an approximate total. It takes ten seconds and you do not need your reference number.

Bills in the twin cities have a pattern. Winters are cold enough that many homes run electric heaters or heat pumps, and summers are hot enough for long AC hours. That makes IESCO bills swing more through the year than in some other regions, and it is exactly why a quick estimate in the middle of the month is useful.

Using the IESCO calculator properly

The calculator is only as good as the number you feed it, so a careful reading helps:

  1. Find the "present reading" on your last bill.
  2. Read your meter today and subtract the old reading. That is your usage so far.
  3. Divide by the number of days since the last reading and multiply by 30 for a fair full-month estimate.
  4. Enter that figure above. Tick "Protected consumer" only if you have stayed at or under 200 units in each of the last six months.

The slip shows your estimated payable amount first. Tap "Show slab-by-slab breakdown" to see which units fall into which slab.

A two-bedroom flat with fans, a fridge and lights usually sits between 150 and 250 units outside summer. Add one inverter AC used every night in June and the same flat can reach 450 to 600 units. A larger house in the E and F sectors with two or three ACs can pass 1,000 units in the hottest month, which is where solar starts to look very attractive.

A worked example for a Rawalpindi home

Take a family in Satellite Town whose last bill showed a present reading of 18,240. On the 20th day of the new cycle the meter shows 18,470, so they have used 230 units in 20 days. Spread over a full month that is about 345 units. Entering 345 in the IESCO bill calculator puts them in the 301 to 400 unit slab, with the first 300 units charged across the lower slabs and only the last 45 at the higher rate.

Now the useful part. The family can see what happens if they cut back. Dropping to around 300 units for the month, by running the AC a couple of hours less each night for the last ten days, removes the whole top slab and the tax on it. That is the kind of decision you can only make before the bill is printed, which is the point of checking mid-month.

The same family can also see what they would gain from protected status. They are well above 200 units, so it is not realistic for them in summer, but a winter of careful use followed by a summer on solar could bring them back under the line. Winter heating and IESCO bills

Islamabad is one of the few places in Pakistan where winter can be as expensive as summer. When gas pressure drops, many families switch to oil-filled radiators or fan heaters, and a single 2,000 watt heater running five hours a day adds about 300 units a month on its own. An inverter AC in heat mode does the same job for roughly a third of the electricity, so if you already own one, use it for heating before you buy a heater. Our guide on what one unit of electricity is shows how to work out what each appliance uses.

Lines on the bill the calculator does not include

  • Fuel price adjustment. Charged or refunded per unit, based on fuel costs from earlier months. See our fuel price adjustment guide.
  • Arrears. Anything unpaid from a previous bill.
  • Late payment surcharge. Added if you pay after the due date.
  • Income tax. On bigger bills for people not on the active taxpayer list.

If your printed bill and the calculator differ by more than these items, check the units first. Estimated readings happen, especially after long holidays or when a meter is hard to reach.

Time-of-use meters in the twin cities

Many houses in Islamabad have a sanctioned load of 5 kW or more, and those connections are normally billed on a time-of-use meter with separate peak and off-peak readings. If your bill has two readings, the peak units cost more. Running laundry, the water pump and the iron before evening, and pre-cooling rooms in the afternoon, can cut the peak share noticeably.

Bringing an IESCO bill down

  • Use the AC's heat mode in winter instead of resistance heaters.
  • Keep doors and windows sealed. Islamabad homes lose a lot of heat and cool air through old window frames. Weather strips are cheap.
  • Put the electric geyser on a timer. A geyser left on all day is a quiet winter drain. A simple timer switch pays for itself within a month or two.
  • Consider rooftop solar. The twin cities get good sunshine for most of the year. Check the 5kW solar system price to see what a typical family setup costs.

Frequently asked questions

Does the IESCO bill calculator work for Rawalpindi and Attock?

Yes. Every area served by IESCO uses the same tariff, so the calculator works for Islamabad, Rawalpindi, Attock, Jhelum and Chakwal alike.

Is there an IESCO bill calculator for 2026 rates?

This page is the 2026 version. We update the slab table whenever a new tariff is notified, and the month we last checked the rates is shown under the calculator. The current check is [powerbachat_checked].

Why did my IESCO bill double in one month?

The usual cause is crossing a slab, especially the 200 unit line if you were protected. A heavy fuel adjustment month or arrears from a missed payment can also push the total up. Compare your units month by month first.

Can I check my IESCO bill online?

Yes, with the 14-digit reference number on your paper bill. Once you have the online copy, put its units into this calculator to make sure the energy charge matches.
