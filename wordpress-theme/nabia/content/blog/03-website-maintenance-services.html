What website maintenance includes

A proper plan, whether you do it yourself or pay someone, covers these jobs:

  • Updates. WordPress core, your theme and plugins. Updates fix security holes, but they can also break things, so they should be tested, not just clicked.
  • Backups. Daily or weekly copies of your files and database, stored somewhere other than your hosting account.
  • Security. Malware scans, a firewall, login protection and removing old admin accounts.
  • Uptime monitoring. A tool that checks your site every few minutes and alerts someone when it goes down.
  • Speed checks. Sites slow down over time as images, plugins and data pile up.
  • Form and checkout tests. The number of sites I see with a contact form quietly sending emails nowhere is scary.
  • Small edits. New team photos, updated prices, a new service page.
  • A report. What was done, what was found and anything you should know about.

How much website maintenance costs

Across the market you will see three rough tiers:

  • $25 to $60 per month: updates, backups and monitoring, with little or no editing time
  • $60 to $150 per month: everything above plus content edits, speed and SEO checks, faster support
  • $150+ per month: online stores and busy sites that need priority support and more hours

My own care plans sit in that range and are month to month, because I do not think anyone should be locked into a contract to keep their own website safe.

A monthly website maintenance checklist

If you prefer to handle it yourself, here is the checklist I use. Put it in your calendar for the first Monday of each month:

  1. Take a full backup and confirm it downloaded correctly
  2. Update plugins, theme and WordPress, one at a time, and check the site after each
  3. Remove plugins you no longer use
  4. Submit every form on the site and confirm the email arrives
  5. Place a test order if you run a store
  6. Run your homepage through Google PageSpeed Insights and note the score
  7. Check Google Search Console for errors or security warnings
  8. Look for broken links and outdated information: prices, opening hours, team members
  9. Review user accounts and remove anyone who no longer needs access

It takes about an hour once you get used to it. The hard part is doing it every month, which is why most busy owners hand it over.

Signs your website needs maintenance right now

  • Your dashboard shows a long list of pending updates
  • The site feels slower than it used to, especially on mobile
  • You are getting fewer enquiries but traffic looks the same
  • You see spam users, strange links or redirects you did not add
  • Nobody remembers when the last backup was taken

Do you need a maintenance plan?

You probably do if your website brings in leads or sales, if you run WordPress or WooCommerce, or if you simply do not want to think about it. You might not need one if your site is a simple Wix or Squarespace page, because those platforms handle updates and security for you. You still need to check forms and content now and then, though.

What to ask a maintenance provider

  • Do you test updates before applying them to the live site?
  • Where are backups stored, and how fast can you restore one?
  • How many hours of edits are included each month?
  • What is your response time when the site is down?
  • Can I cancel any time?
