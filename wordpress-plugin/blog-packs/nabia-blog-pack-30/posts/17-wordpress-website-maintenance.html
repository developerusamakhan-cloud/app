Quick answer: a good WordPress website maintenance plan includes tested updates, off-site backups, security monitoring, uptime checks and a monthly report at the basic level, with content edits, speed and SEO checks and priority support in higher tiers. Most small business plans cost $40 to $200 per month. Choose based on how much your site matters to revenue, not on the lowest price.

Maintenance plans are hard to compare because providers describe the same work differently. Here is how to read between the lines, based on what I include in my own plans and what I see when clients switch to me from somewhere else.

What each tier usually includes

Basic

Core, theme and plugin updates, backups, uptime monitoring and security scanning. Suitable for simple sites that rarely change.

Standard

Everything in basic plus a set amount of content edits, speed optimisation, SEO health checks, database cleanup and bug fixes. Suits most small business sites.

Premium

Everything above plus faster response times, more hours, more frequent backups and extra care for stores and membership sites.

Questions to ask before you pay

  1. Are updates tested before they go live, or just clicked?
  2. Where are backups stored, how long are they kept, and are restores free?
  3. What happens if the site is hacked? Is cleanup included?
  4. How many minutes of edits are included, and do unused minutes roll over?
  5. What is the response time for urgent problems?
  6. Do I get a report every month?
  7. Is there a contract? Can I cancel any time?
  8. Do I keep full admin access to my site and hosting?

Red flags

  • Very cheap plans that only run automatic updates with no checks
  • Long contracts with cancellation fees
  • No reports, so you never know what was done
  • Providers who hold your logins or hosting hostage

Which plan does your site need?

  • Brochure site, rarely changes: basic
  • Business site with regular updates and leads: standard
  • Online store, bookings or memberships: premium
