To convert BTU per hour to watts, multiply by 0.293. A 5,000 BTU window air conditioner moves about 1,465 watts of heat, and a 10,000 BTU unit about 2,931 watts. To go from watts to BTU, multiply by 3.412: a 1,500 watt space heater gives out about 5,118 BTU per hour. The calculator above converts both ways.

The formulas

  • watts = BTU/h × 0.29307
  • BTU/h = watts × 3.41214

Heating and cooling ratings in BTU are almost always BTU per hour, a rate, which is why they convert to watts, also a rate. For larger figures, the BTU to kW calculator gives the answer in kilowatts.

Heaters versus air conditioners

For an electric space heater, oil filled radiator or kettle, the watts on the label and the heat produced are the same thing. Every watt of electricity becomes a watt of heat, so a 1,500 watt heater always gives about 5,100 BTU/h.

Air conditioners and heat pumps are different, because they move heat instead of creating it. A 5,000 BTU window unit moves about 1,465 watts of heat but usually draws only around 400 to 550 watts of electricity. To estimate the power draw, divide the BTU rating by the unit's energy efficiency ratio (EER), which is printed on the label in many countries. A 5,000 BTU unit with an EER of 11 uses about 455 watts.

Worked examples

  • 8,000 BTU window AC: 8,000 × 0.293 = 2,345 watts of cooling.
  • 1,000 watt heater: 1,000 × 3.412 = 3,412 BTU/h.
  • Gas fireplace rated 30,000 BTU: 30,000 × 0.293 = 8,792 watts of heat.

Frequently asked questions

How many watts is 5,000 BTU?

5,000 BTU per hour is about 1,465 watts of heating or cooling. A 5,000 BTU air conditioner usually draws around 400 to 550 watts of electricity to deliver it.

How many BTU is 1,500 watts?

1,500 watts is about 5,118 BTU per hour, the output of a typical plug in space heater.

How many watts does a 10,000 BTU air conditioner use?

It moves about 2,931 watts of heat. With an EER of 10 to 12, it uses roughly 830 to 1,000 watts of electricity while the compressor runs.

Is BTU the same as BTU per hour?

Strictly, no: a BTU is energy and BTU per hour is power. But appliance ratings that say BTU nearly always mean BTU per hour, so this converter treats them that way.
