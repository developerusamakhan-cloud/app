Rooftop solar subsidy by system size

The central subsidy for residential rooftop solar, under PM Surya Ghar, is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third kW, with a cap of ₹78,000. Applied to typical installed costs for DCR on-grid systems, it looks like this:

Notice how the subsidy covers a large share of a 1 or 2 kW system but a much smaller share of a 10 kW one. Per kilowatt, the support is most generous for small systems, which is deliberate: the scheme aims to bring solar to as many households as possible.

Central subsidy, state top-ups and loans

Three kinds of support can combine:

  • Central financial assistance under PM Surya Ghar, as in the table above, paid to your bank account after commissioning.
  • State top-ups in some states, which add a further amount per kilowatt or a fixed sum. These change more often than the central scheme, so check your state renewable energy agency or discom.
  • Concessional loans from banks for small residential systems, which spread your share of the cost over several years.

Our solar subsidy calculator uses the central amount only, so your net cost may be lower if your state adds support.

What qualifies for the subsidy

  • A residential electricity connection in your name, or the property owner's, at the address.
  • Solar panels with domestic content (DCR). Imported or non-DCR panels do not qualify.
  • Installation by a vendor registered on the national portal for your discom.
  • Net metering with your discom, and a successful inspection.

The DCR rule is the one that trips people up most. A vendor offering a much lower price may be quoting imported panels, which do not qualify. Ask for the panel model and its DCR certificate in writing before you pay anything. Meeting these conditions is straightforward if you start from the national portal, but buying first and applying later is a common and costly mistake.

A worked example

A family in Jaipur averages 320 units a month. Entering 320 in the solar subsidy calculator suggests a system of about 3 kW, the size that captures the full central subsidy. Housing societies and common areas

Residential welfare associations and group housing societies can also get support for rooftop systems that power common areas such as lifts, pumps and corridor lights. The terms differ from individual homes, with assistance calculated per kilowatt up to a larger limit. If you live in an apartment, a society system is often the most practical way to benefit, because individual flats rarely have their own roof.

Documents you will need

Keep these ready before you start the application, so nothing holds up the subsidy at the end:

  • A recent electricity bill with your consumer number.
  • Identity proof, and proof of ownership or the owner's consent if you rent.
  • Bank details in the applicant's name, usually with a cancelled cheque.
  • The vendor's invoice, DCR panel certificates and installation photos for the commissioning stage.

How net metering affects your saving

The subsidy lowers what you pay for the system, but the saving each month comes from net metering. During the day, your panels power the house and send surplus units to the grid. The bi-directional meter records both directions, and your bill is based on the difference, according to your state's net metering rules. Some states settle surplus credits monthly, others annually, and the rate paid for surplus units varies. If your system will export a lot, check how your state values exported units before choosing a size.

Looking beyond the first year

The subsidy is a one-time benefit, but the savings continue for decades. Quality panels typically carry performance warranties of 25 years or more. Inverters usually need replacing once in that period, and panels need regular cleaning, especially in dusty north Indian cities. When you compare quotes, compare the warranty terms and the vendor's service record, not just the price after subsidy. A cheap installation that fails in year five costs far more than the subsidy saved.

How long does the subsidy take?

Timelines depend mainly on how quickly your discom approves feasibility, installs the net meter and inspects the system. Once the commissioning report is submitted with your bank details, the subsidy is usually transferred within weeks. Keep copies of every document and the application number so you can track it on the portal.

Is a bigger system worth it without extra subsidy?

Above 3 kW, every extra kilowatt costs full price. Whether it is worth it depends on your use and your state's tariff. In states with steep upper slabs, such as Maharashtra, extra kilowatts that cut expensive units pay back well. In states with cheap tariffs or generous subsidies, such as Delhi for small users, a 3 kW system is usually enough. Frequently asked questions

What is the maximum solar subsidy for a home?

₹78,000 under the central scheme, reached at 3 kW. State top-ups, where available, are extra.

Does the solar subsidy calculator include GST?

The installed cost ranges are what households typically pay for complete systems. Ask your vendor for a quote that shows taxes clearly.

Can I get the subsidy for an off-grid system?

The residential scheme is designed for grid-connected rooftop systems with net metering. Off-grid and battery systems are generally not covered in the same way.

Do I need DCR panels?

Yes. The central subsidy requires panels with domestic content.

Will I get the subsidy if I already have solar?

The scheme is aimed at new rooftop installations. If you already have a system, check the current rules on the national portal before adding capacity.

Does the subsidy depend on my income?

The central residential subsidy is based on system size, not income. Any state top-ups may have their own conditions.
