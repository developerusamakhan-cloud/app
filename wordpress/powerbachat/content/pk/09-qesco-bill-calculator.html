This QESCO bill calculator is for households supplied by the Quetta Electric Supply Company, which serves most of Balochistan, including Quetta, Pishin, Ziarat, Loralai, Sibi, Khuzdar, Turbat, Gwadar and Nasirabad. Enter the units you have used and it shows the charge for each slab, the taxes on top and an estimated total. It works on any phone and does not need your reference number.

Balochistan is huge and its weather is anything but uniform. Quetta has a proper winter with freezing nights and occasional snow, while Sibi and Nasirabad are among the hottest places in the country in summer. On the coast, Gwadar and Turbat are humid and warm most of the year. A QESCO bill in Quetta peaks in January; one in Sibi peaks in June. The calculator handles both, because the tariff is the same everywhere.

Using the QESCO bill calculator

  1. Find the present reading on your last bill, in the meter reading box.
  2. Read your meter today and subtract. That gives your units so far.
  3. If you are in the middle of the cycle, divide by the days so far and multiply by 30.
  4. Enter that number in the calculator. Turn on "Protected consumer" only if each of your last six bills was at 200 units or less.

The slip shows the estimated amount first and the average price per unit below it. Open the breakdown to see every slab line.

Most homes in Quetta stay modest in summer, because the city's height keeps the heat bearable and fans are often enough. Winter is the expensive season. When gas pressure drops, electric heaters come out, and a home that sits at 150 units in July can pass 500 in January.

Winter heating in Quetta: the biggest lever on your bill

An ordinary electric heater uses 1,000 to 2,000 watts. Run a 2,000 watt heater for six hours a night and it uses about 360 units a month on its own, more than the rest of the house combined. A few choices make a big difference:

  • Use an inverter AC's heat mode if you have one. It produces the same warmth for roughly a third of the units, because it moves heat rather than making it.
  • Heat the room you are in, not the whole house. Close doors and keep the family in one warm room in the evening.
  • Use an electric blanket at night. It uses a small fraction of a room heater's power.
  • Seal the draughts. Felt strips on doors and thick curtains on windows keep heat in for almost nothing.
  • Put the geyser on a timer. Water heating is the second biggest winter load in many homes.

Our guide on what one unit of electricity is shows how to work out the units any appliance uses, so you can compare options yourself.

Hot districts: Sibi, Nasirabad and Jaffarabad

The plains in the east of the province face extreme summer heat, much like upper Sindh. Here the usual advice applies with extra force: cool one room well rather than many rooms badly, shade the outdoor AC unit, and use coolers during the dry weeks before humidity rises. For older relatives and small children, a small inverter AC in one room during the hottest hours is the most efficient way to keep them safe.

Load-shedding and solar in Balochistan

Long feeders, high losses and remote towns mean many QESCO areas face long daily outages. At the same time, Balochistan has some of the strongest sunshine in Pakistan. That combination makes hybrid solar, with panels and batteries, especially attractive here. It cuts the bill and keeps the house running when the grid is down. A worked example from Quetta

A family on Jinnah Town's outskirts uses about 160 units in summer. In January their meter shows 280 units used by the 15th, thanks to a heater in the lounge every evening. At that pace the month will end around 560 units. The QESCO bill calculator shows what that costs, and how much less 350 units would cost. They switch the lounge heater for the heat mode on the bedroom AC and an electric blanket for the children. They finish the month near 380 units. The saving is several thousand rupees, from changes that hardly affect comfort.

Agricultural and commercial connections

Tube wells in Balochistan's farming districts usually run on agricultural connections, and shops on commercial ones. Both use different tariffs from the household slabs here, so this calculator is not suitable for them. If you have a home and a tube well on the same meter, that is worth sorting out, because heavy pumping on a household meter will put you in the top slabs very quickly.

When a QESCO bill looks wrong

Long distances mean meter readers do not always reach every home on time, so estimated readings happen. If your bill is marked as estimated, or the units are far above what your meter shows, photograph the meter display with the date on the same day. Take the photo, the bill and your CNIC to the subdivision office and ask for a correction before the due date, and keep a copy of your complaint. A correction now is much easier than fighting a large catch-up bill a month later, which can also cost you protected status.

What the estimate leaves out

  • Fuel price adjustment, which changes every month. See the FPA guide.
  • Arrears, surcharges and income tax, which depend on your account.

Frequently asked questions

Is the QESCO bill calculator valid outside Quetta?

Yes. Every QESCO district uses the national tariff, so it works from Zhob to Gwadar. Hub and some nearby areas are supplied by K-Electric, which has its own K-Electric bill calculator.

Why is my winter QESCO bill so high?

Electric heating almost always. One heater can add more than 300 units a month. Switching to an AC's heat mode or an electric blanket cuts that sharply.

Do QESCO customers pay more per unit?

No. The base rates are identical across Pakistan. Differences come from usage and the monthly fuel adjustment.

How do I check a QESCO bill online?

Use the 14-digit reference number from your paper bill on the official online bill service, then enter its units here to check the energy charge.
