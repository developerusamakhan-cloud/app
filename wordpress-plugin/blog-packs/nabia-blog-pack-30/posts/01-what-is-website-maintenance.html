Quick answer: website maintenance is the regular care that keeps your website secure, fast, working and up to date. It covers software updates, backups, security checks, speed tuning, fixing small problems and keeping your content current. Think of it like servicing a car: skip it and things still run for a while, until they suddenly do not.

Most business owners only think about their website when something breaks. A form stops sending emails, the site goes down, or a client mentions that the page looks strange on their phone. By then the fix is usually bigger and more urgent than it needed to be. Here is what maintenance actually means, without the jargon.

What website maintenance covers

Every maintenance routine I run for clients falls into four jobs:

1. Protect

Updating WordPress, themes and plugins, scanning for malware, using a firewall and taking backups stored somewhere other than your hosting account. This is the most important part, because outdated software is the most common way small business sites get hacked.

2. Keep it fast

Sites slow down over time as images, plugins and database clutter pile up. Regular checks catch this before visitors and Google notice.

3. Fix small problems

Broken links, contact forms that stopped sending, layout issues after an update, an expired SSL certificate. Small issues are cheap to fix when caught early.

4. Improve

Updating prices, team photos, services and opening hours, adding new pages and reviewing what visitors do. This is where maintenance starts paying for itself.

How often should it happen?

  • Weekly: software updates, security scan, backup check
  • Monthly: test every form, check speed and Google Search Console, remove unused plugins
  • Every 3 to 6 months: review content, prices and photos, and look at which pages bring enquiries

What happens if you skip it

Nothing, for a while. Then one of these usually happens: a plugin vulnerability gets exploited and spam pages appear, an update breaks the layout, the host upgrades PHP and an old theme stops working, or the site quietly loses its Google rankings because it became slow. What website maintenance costs

You have three options:

  • Do it yourself: free in money, about one to two hours a month in time, plus the risk of fixing things you are not sure about.
  • Pay per job: fine for simple sites, but you only call someone after something breaks.

Do you need a maintenance plan?

You probably do if your website brings in leads or sales, runs on WordPress or WooCommerce, or if nobody on your team enjoys this kind of work. You might not need a full plan if you run a small Squarespace or Wix site, because those platforms handle updates and security for you. You still need to check forms and content now and then.
