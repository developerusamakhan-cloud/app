This LESCO bill calculator is for anyone who gets electricity from the Lahore Electric Supply Company, which covers Lahore, Kasur, Okara, Sheikhupura and Nankana Sahib. Enter the units from your meter in the tool above and it does the same slab maths that appears on your printed bill, then adds GST and electricity duty so you see a realistic total. It is free, works on any phone, and does not ask for your reference number.

We built it because the most common question we hear in Lahore is simple: "I have used this many units, how much will I pay?" The paper bill answers that question three weeks too late. With a quick meter reading and this page, you can answer it today and still have time to change something.

How to use the calculator

  1. Read the meter. Note the number on the display and subtract the "present reading" from your last bill. The difference is the units you have used so far this month.
  2. Type that number into the units box, or drag the slider. If you are mid-month, double it roughly to estimate the full month.
  3. Switch on "Protected consumer" only if your home has stayed at 200 units or less in each of the last six months. Your last bill shows this history in the box of previous months.
  4. Open the slab-by-slab breakdown to see exactly which units cost what.

The result is an estimate of the base bill. It does not include the monthly fuel price adjustment, arrears or a late payment surcharge, because those depend on the month and your own account.

The extra lines on a LESCO bill

Once you know the energy charge, the rest of the bill is easier to follow. These are the lines that most often confuse people:

  • FPA (fuel price adjustment). A per-unit charge or refund based on what fuel cost a couple of months earlier. It changes every month. Our FPA guide explains how to read it.
  • QTA (quarterly tariff adjustment). A smaller adjustment that appears for a few months at a time when the regulator revises costs.
  • GST. Sales tax at 18 percent on the energy charge and most adjustments.
  • Electricity duty. A small provincial charge. Our estimate uses 1.5 percent.
  • Income tax. Added on larger bills for people who are not on the active taxpayer list.

For a full walk through every box, read how to read your electricity bill.

Peak hours and three-phase connections

Bigger homes in areas like DHA, Gulberg, Bahria Town and Johar Town often have a sanctioned load of 5 kW or more. Those connections usually get a time-of-use meter, which records peak and off-peak units separately. Peak units cost more, and peak hours fall in the evening, shifting by season. If your bill shows two separate readings, run the AC harder in the afternoon and ease off during the peak window. The calculator above uses normal single-rate household slabs, so treat its result as a guide if you are on a time-of-use meter.

Ways Lahore households cut their LESCO bill

  • Watch the 200 unit mark. If you are protected, staying under 200 units is worth far more than any gadget. Read the meter around day 20 and plan the last week.
  • Set the AC to 26 degrees. Each degree lower adds a few percent to the cooling load. With a ceiling fan running, 26 degrees feels comfortable in most rooms.
  • Service the AC before May. Lahore dust clogs filters and coils fast. A clean unit cools quicker and switches off sooner.
  • Check the water pump. An old motor or a leaking float valve can run for hours without anyone noticing.
  • Look at solar. With summer bills where they are, a rooftop system often pays for itself in a few years. Our solar calculator sizes one from your own units.

Checking your LESCO bill online

LESCO bills are available online with the 14-digit reference number printed near the top of your paper bill. Keep a photo of that number on your phone. When the online bill appears, compare the units with your own reading and the energy charge with this calculator. If the units look wrong, for example an estimated reading when the meter reader did not visit, raise a complaint at your local subdivision office or through the official complaint line before the due date.

Frequently asked questions

Is this LESCO bill calculator accurate?

It uses the current NEPRA slab rates, 18 percent GST and electricity duty, so the base bill should come very close to your printed one. The difference is usually the fuel price adjustment, arrears or a surcharge, which the calculator does not know.

How do I know if I am a protected consumer?

Your bill shows your consumption for the previous months. If every month in the last six stayed at 200 units or less, you are protected and pay the lower rates. One month above 200 units removes that status for six months.

Why is my LESCO bill higher than the calculator?

Check the FPA line first. A positive fuel adjustment, an arrears amount from a previous month, or a late payment surcharge are the usual reasons. If the units themselves look too high, compare them with your own meter reading.

Do LESCO and other companies charge different rates?

No. All distribution companies use the same uniform residential tariff. You can confirm this with our IESCO or MEPCO calculators, which give the same base result for the same units.
