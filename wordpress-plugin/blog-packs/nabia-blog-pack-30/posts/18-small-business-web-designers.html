Quick answer: the right small business web designer shows you live sites they built, asks about your customers and goals before talking design, gives a fixed price, timeline and written scope, makes sure you own your domain, hosting and site, builds in speed and SEO basics, and trains you to update it.

There are thousands of web designers, and from the outside they all look similar: nice portfolio, friendly website, reasonable prices. These ten questions help you see the differences that matter after the contract is signed.

The 10 questions

1. Can I see three live websites you built for small businesses?

Open them on your phone. Are they fast? Is it obvious what each business does? Would you contact them?

2. What do you need to know about my business before quoting?

A good designer asks about customers, services, competitors and goals. A quote without questions is a template price.

3. What exactly is included in the price?

Pages, revisions, copywriting help, images, SEO setup, training and support after launch.

4. What is the timeline, and what do you need from me?

Content is the most common delay. A clear plan avoids months of drift.

5. Which platform will you use, and why?

Listen for a reason linked to your needs, not just "I always use X".

6. Will I own everything?

Domain, hosting, website files and logins should be in your name.

7. How do you handle SEO and speed?

Page titles, descriptions, headings, image optimisation and Google Search Console setup should be standard.

8. Can I edit the site myself?

You should be able to change text, images and blog posts without calling anyone.

9. What happens after launch?

Ask about support, updates and backups. A website needs care; see what is website maintenance.

10. How will we measure success?

Enquiries, bookings and sales, not just compliments. Read what a good website conversion rate looks like.

Freelancer or agency?

For most small businesses, a freelancer offers the best value: lower costs, direct contact and one person who knows your project inside out. Agencies make sense when you need a larger team or many services at once. For a fair comparison, read choosing a WordPress website design company.
