Of all the lines on a Pakistani electricity bill, the fuel price adjustment causes the most confusion and the most anger. It is the line that makes your bill go up even though you used fewer units, and occasionally the line that brings a pleasant surprise. This guide explains what it is, why it exists, how it is worked out, and how to read it on your own bill without needing a degree in power economics.

The short version

When the regulator sets the electricity tariff, it assumes a certain cost for the fuel that power plants will burn: gas, furnace oil, coal, imported LNG and so on. Real fuel costs rarely match that assumption. Each month, the actual cost is compared with the assumed cost. If fuel was more expensive than expected, the difference is added to bills as a per-unit charge. If it was cheaper, the difference is refunded. That per-unit charge or refund is the fuel price adjustment, shown on your bill as FPA, or FCA (fuel charges adjustment) on some bills.

Why it arrives two months late

Working out the real fuel cost takes time. Power plants report what they burned, the central power purchaser compiles the figures, the regulator holds a hearing and then issues a decision. By the time all that is done, a couple of months have passed. So the adjustment on your bill this month usually relates to fuel costs from an earlier month, not the month you are being billed for.

This delay is why the fuel price adjustment can feel disconnected from your life. Your summer bill can carry an adjustment from spring, and your winter bill one from late summer. It also means the charge is applied to the units on your current bill, even though those units were used later than the fuel was burned.

How it is calculated on your bill

The regulator announces the adjustment as a rate per unit, positive or negative. Your company multiplies that rate by the units on your bill. For example, if the adjustment is a certain number of rupees per unit and you used 300 units, the FPA line is 300 times that rate. GST is then charged on it, which makes the effective effect slightly bigger than the rate alone.

Because it is a flat per-unit amount, the fuel price adjustment hits heavy users harder in rupees but evenly per unit. A home using 600 units pays twice the FPA of a home using 300.

Some categories of consumers have at times been exempted or treated differently, such as lifeline consumers with very small use and, in some decisions, protected households. The rules are set in each decision, so check the FPA line on your own bill rather than assuming.

How to read it on your bill

Look in the charges section of your bill for a line marked FPA, FCA or fuel adjustment. Some bills show the per-unit rate and the period it relates to, and some show only the rupee amount. To work out the rate yourself, divide the FPA amount by your units. Compare it with last month's bill to see whether the adjustment went up or down.

Why the adjustment is often positive

Pakistan relies heavily on imported fuel for power generation. When the rupee weakens or global fuel prices rise, the cost of generating each unit goes up quickly. Tariffs, meanwhile, are set in advance and change only once or twice a year. That gap usually shows up as a positive adjustment. When fuel prices fall or more power comes from cheaper sources such as hydro in the summer, the adjustment can turn negative, and you see a refund.

Quarterly adjustments are different

You may also see a quarterly tariff adjustment, often marked QTA. This is separate from the monthly fuel price adjustment. It covers changes in other costs of supplying power, such as capacity payments and distribution costs, and is reviewed every quarter. It usually appears as a per-unit charge spread over several months. Like the FPA, it is applied per unit and attracts GST.

Karachi has its own adjustment

K-Electric generates much of its own power and buys the rest on its own terms, so Karachi's fuel cost adjustment is calculated separately from the other companies. In some months it is close to the national figure, in others quite different. Our K-Electric unit price page explains how this affects the real cost of a unit in Karachi.

Can you avoid the fuel price adjustment?

Not directly, because it applies to every unit you draw from the grid. But you can reduce its impact in two ways:

  • Use fewer grid units. Every unit you save saves its share of the FPA too. Small steps like a higher AC thermostat or LED lights reduce it automatically.
  • Produce your own power. Units from rooftop solar carry no fuel adjustment at all. For many families that is a meaningful part of the case for solar. Our solar calculator shows how many units a system would cover.

When the FPA looks wrong

Occasionally the FPA line is wrong, usually because it has been applied to the wrong number of units after an estimated reading. Divide the FPA amount by your units. If the rate does not match the announced rate, or if it has been applied to units you did not use, raise it with your company's local office before the due date, with a photo of your meter reading.

Frequently asked questions

What does FPA mean on an electricity bill?

FPA stands for fuel price adjustment. It is a per-unit charge or refund that reflects the difference between the fuel cost assumed in the tariff and the actual cost from an earlier month.

Is the fuel price adjustment the same every month?

No. It is decided separately each month and can be positive, negative or close to zero.

Is GST charged on the FPA?

Yes. GST is applied to the fuel adjustment as well as to the energy charge.

Why is my bill higher even though my units went down?

Usually because the fuel price adjustment or a quarterly adjustment rose that month, or because a new tariff came into effect.
