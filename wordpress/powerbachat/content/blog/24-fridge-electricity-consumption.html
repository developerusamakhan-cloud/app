The fridge is the one appliance in your home that never gets switched off. It does not draw much power at any single moment, but it runs 24 hours a day, every day of the year. That is why fridge electricity consumption quietly makes up a steady share of most household bills, and why it can be the difference between staying under a slab limit and crossing it. This guide gives realistic figures, explains what changes them, and shows how to measure your own fridge.

Why a fridge is different from other appliances

A fan or an iron uses power only while it is on. A fridge works in cycles: the compressor runs until the inside is cold enough, switches off, and starts again when the temperature rises. So the wattage printed on the label tells you only part of the story. What matters is how many hours a day the compressor actually runs, and that depends on the weather, the model and how you use it.

If units still feel abstract, our guide on what one unit of electricity is explains it simply: one unit is 1,000 watts used for one hour.

Typical units per month

These are rough ranges for Pakistani conditions. Your own figure can be higher or lower depending on the model, age and room temperature.

  • Small single-door fridge: typically around 20 to 35 units a month.
  • Standard double-door fridge, non-inverter: often around 45 to 75 units a month, at the higher end in summer.
  • Standard double-door inverter fridge: commonly around 25 to 45 units a month.
  • Chest deep freezer: roughly 50 to 90 units a month, more if it is opened often or kept in a hot room.
  • Old fridge, 12 or more years: can use well over 80 units a month if the seals and gas are tired.

Summer makes a big difference. In June, a fridge in a kitchen that reaches the high 30s will run far longer than the same fridge in January. It is common for monthly fridge electricity consumption to rise by a third or more between winter and peak summer.

Inverter vs non-inverter fridge

A normal fridge has a compressor that is either fully on or fully off. An inverter fridge uses a variable speed compressor that slows down once the inside is cold and keeps running gently instead of stopping and starting. The idea is the same as with ACs, which our article on inverter vs non-inverter ACs explains in more detail.

In practice, an inverter fridge usually uses noticeably fewer units, often something like 30 to 40 percent less than a similar older non-inverter model. It is also quieter, holds a steadier temperature and copes better with low voltage. It also starts gently, which makes it easier to run on a UPS or solar inverter during load-shedding. The trade-off is a higher purchase price. If your old fridge is still working well, the saving alone may take a few years to repay the difference. If it is old, noisy or failing, buying an inverter model makes good sense.

How to measure your own fridge

Ranges are useful, but measuring is better. There are two easy ways.

Use a plug-in energy meter

A plug-in energy meter is a small device that sits between the socket and the fridge plug and shows how many units have passed through it. They are cheap and widely available in electronics markets and online.

  1. Plug the meter into the wall and the fridge into the meter.
  2. Leave it for at least 48 hours, ideally a full week, with normal use.
  3. Read the kWh figure, divide by the number of days, then multiply by 30.

That gives your real monthly units for current weather. Repeat it in summer and winter if you want the full picture.

Use your main meter

If you do not have a plug-in meter, note your main meter reading at night, switch off everything except the fridge, and read it again in the morning. Divide the difference by the hours, then multiply by 24 and 30. This is less precise and not very practical in a busy house, but it gives a fair estimate.

What pushes the units up

  • Hot placement: next to the stove, in direct sun or in an unventilated corner.
  • No space at the back: the coils need air to release heat. Leave a gap of a few inches.
  • Worn door seal: a loose seal lets cold air leak all day. Close the door on a sheet of paper; if it slides out easily, the seal needs attention.
  • Frequent opening: every opening lets warm air in, especially on summer afternoons.
  • Hot food inside: let cooked food cool before putting it in.
  • Thermostat set too cold: a middle setting is usually enough.
  • Frost build-up: in direct cool models, thick ice acts as insulation and makes the compressor work harder.

Habits that cut the units

Most of the fixes cost nothing. Move the fridge away from the stove and the wall. Keep it reasonably full, since a stocked fridge holds cold better than an empty one, but do not block the air vents inside. Set the thermostat to the middle position, and turn it down a notch in winter. Defrost regularly if your model needs it. Clean the coils at the back twice a year. If you keep a deep freezer only for Eid meat, consider switching it off and cleaning it for the rest of the year rather than running it half empty.

A worked example with slabs

This is where fridge electricity consumption really matters in Pakistan. Domestic bills are charged in slabs, and the protected consumer rates depend on staying within a limit. Our guide on how the protected consumer rule works explains this in detail.

Take a family in Multan using about 180 units a month in summer. Their old non-inverter fridge, measured with a plug-in meter, uses about 70 units. They also run a deep freezer in the hot store room that uses another 60 or so.

Running a fridge on solar or UPS

Because it runs all day and night, a fridge is a good match for solar during daylight. During evening load-shedding, it adds to the load your battery must carry. An inverter fridge is much easier on a UPS or solar system because it starts gently, while an older fridge draws a sharp surge at every start. If you are sizing a system, include the fridge in your list on the solar calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How many units does a fridge use per day?

A typical double-door fridge uses roughly 1 to 2.5 units a day, depending on whether it is an inverter model, its age and the weather.

Is it cheaper to switch the fridge off at night?

No. It will simply work harder to cool down again, and food safety suffers. Keep it running and fix placement and seals instead.

Does a deep freezer use more than a fridge?

Usually yes, especially in a hot room. Open it less and keep it away from heat.

Is an inverter fridge worth buying?

If you need a new fridge anyway, generally yes. It uses fewer units, works better on low voltage and is kinder to a UPS or solar system.
