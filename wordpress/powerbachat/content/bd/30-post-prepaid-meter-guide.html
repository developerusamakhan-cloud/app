If you live in Dhaka or another city in Bangladesh, there is a good chance your home has a prepaid meter. Instead of a monthly bill, you recharge in advance and the meter deducts as you use electricity. It sounds simple, but many people are puzzled when the balance falls faster than expected or when a recharge seems smaller than the amount paid. This guide explains how a prepaid meter works, what is deducted and how to avoid the common surprises.

How a prepaid meter works

You buy credit through a mobile financial service, a bank, a vending point or your distributor's app. DESCO, DPDC and other distributors each run their own system, but the idea is the same everywhere. You receive a token number, or the credit is sent to the meter directly, depending on the system. Once the credit is on the meter, it is used up as you consume electricity. When the balance runs low, the meter warns you, and if it runs out, supply can be cut until you recharge.

The tariff is the same as for postpaid customers. What changes is timing: you pay before you use, and you see deductions as they happen.

What is deducted

Three things come off your balance:

  • Energy charge: your units multiplied by the slab rates. The slab is worked out on your total usage for the month, just as on a postpaid bill.
  • Demand charge: a fixed monthly amount based on your sanctioned load. On most meters it is deducted in small amounts during the month or at the start of each month.
  • VAT: 5 percent on the energy and demand charges.

That is why a recharge of a round amount does not buy a round number of units. Part of it goes to the demand charge and VAT.

Why the balance falls faster in summer

As usage rises, more units fall into the higher slabs, and units above 400 a month cost much more. So a prepaid meter's balance falls faster than usage alone suggests in the hot months. A family that spends a modest amount in January may need several times that in July. Our electricity bill calculator shows what a month's usage will deduct, so you can plan recharges.

Recharge tips

  1. Estimate your month. Use the calculator with your expected units to see roughly how much you need.
  2. Recharge before the balance is low, especially before weekends and holidays when help is harder to get.
  3. Keep the token or transaction number until the credit shows on the meter.
  4. Enter tokens carefully. A mistyped token is the most common reason a recharge "does not work".
  5. Check for any rebate. Prepaid customers have sometimes received a small rebate under the rules in force. Your deduction history will show it.

Reading the meter

Prepaid meters can show several screens: remaining balance, total units used, units used this month, last recharge, and more. Press the display button to cycle through them, or check the user manual for your meter model. Two screens are worth knowing well: the remaining balance and the cumulative kWh reading. Note the kWh reading on the first of each month, and you will always know your monthly units.

Common problems and fixes

  • Token rejected: re-enter carefully. If it still fails, contact your distributor with the transaction number.
  • Balance falling unusually fast: check whether something new is running, such as a geyser or a faulty pump. If not, ask for a meter check.
  • Supply cut with balance remaining: some meters cut supply for other reasons, such as overload above the sanctioned load. Reduce the load and reset as the manual describes.
  • Arrears deducted: if you moved into a home with outstanding dues on the meter, part of each recharge may go to them. Ask the distributor for the account history.

Moving into a home with a prepaid meter

Before you move in, ask the owner or previous tenant to show the meter's balance and any dues, and make sure the account is clear. Note the kWh reading on the day you move in. It protects you from paying for someone else's usage and from inherited arrears.

Prepaid meters and your budget

Many families find a prepaid meter easier to manage than a postpaid bill, because they see their spending every day. The trick is to plan rather than react. Read the meter weekly, estimate the month's total with the calculator, and recharge once at the start of the month with a buffer. Small top-ups throughout the month are easy to lose track of.

Prepaid versus postpaid: which is better?

Neither changes the tariff, so neither is cheaper on its own. Prepaid suits families who like to see spending day by day and avoid a large bill at the end of the month. Postpaid suits people who prefer one payment and do not want the risk of a supply cut when the balance runs out. Many households that switched to prepaid say the main benefit is awareness: when you watch the balance fall, you notice waste quickly. Whichever you have, our DESCO and DPDC calculators show what a month will cost.

Shared buildings and sub-meters

In some buildings, the owner holds one main account and tenants are billed through sub-meters. In that case, your charges depend on the owner's method, which may not match the official tariff exactly. Ask for the per-unit rate being used and the reading on your sub-meter each month, and compare it with the official tariff. Where possible, a separate prepaid meter in your own name is the clearest arrangement.

Saving units on a prepaid meter

  • Keep summer usage under 400 units where possible, to avoid the expensive slabs.
  • Use AC dry mode on humid evenings and set cooling to 25 or 26 degrees.
  • Replace old fans with efficient DC fans and use LED lights.
  • Check your IPS. An old battery wastes units every time it recharges. Our IPS calculator helps you size a replacement.

Frequently asked questions

Is the tariff different on a prepaid meter?

No. Prepaid and postpaid customers use the same residential tariff. See the electricity tariff in Bangladesh page.

Why did my recharge give fewer units than expected?

Part of each recharge covers the demand charge and VAT, and units in higher slabs cost more.

What happens if my balance runs out?

Supply can be cut until you recharge. Most meters warn you when the balance is low.

How do I find my monthly units on the meter?

Note the cumulative kWh reading on the first of each month. The difference between two readings is your monthly usage.

Can I recharge with mobile banking?

Yes. Mobile financial services and banks are widely used to recharge prepaid meters.
