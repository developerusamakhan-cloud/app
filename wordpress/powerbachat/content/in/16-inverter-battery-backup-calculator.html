How to use the calculator

  1. Set how many of each appliance you will actually run during a power cut.
  2. Add anything else in watts. The wattage is printed on the appliance label or its adapter.
  3. Choose your battery. Tubular lead-acid batteries of 150 to 200 Ah are the most common for home inverters. Lithium batteries are becoming popular, especially with solar.
  4. Set how many batteries your inverter uses. Many 1 kVA inverters use one 12 V battery; larger ones use two or more.

The maths behind it

A battery's energy in watt-hours is its voltage times its amp-hours. A 12 V 150 Ah battery holds 1,800 watt-hours on paper. You cannot use all of it: tubular batteries last much longer if you use only about half before recharging, while lithium batteries can use around 90 percent. The inverter also loses some energy, so we assume it delivers about 85 percent. Backup time is the usable energy after losses, divided by the load in watts.

So one 12 V 150 Ah tubular battery gives about 900 watt-hours usable and about 765 after inverter losses. Running two ceiling fans, four LED bulbs, a tube light, a router and two phone chargers, around 260 watts, that is close to three hours.

Choosing the right battery

Tubular lead-acid is the standard choice: affordable, widely available from brands such as Luminous, Exide and Microtek, and reliable with care. It needs distilled water topping up and lasts a few years.

Lithium (LiFePO4) costs more upfront but delivers far more usable energy per battery, charges faster, needs no maintenance and lasts many more cycles. For daily power cuts or solar use, it often costs less over its life.

Try both in the inverter battery backup calculator to see how the backup time changes.

Sizing the inverter

The inverter must handle everything you switch on at once, plus a margin for fans and motors that draw extra power when starting. The calculator suggests a minimum size with about 30 percent headroom. If you want to run a fridge or a water pump on the inverter, choose a bigger model, because they draw much more for a moment when they start. A sine wave inverter is better for fans, fridges and electronics, and quieter.

Typical home setups

  • 900 VA to 1 kVA inverter with one 150 Ah tubular battery: fans and lights in a small home for two to three hours.
  • 1.5 kVA inverter with two 150 Ah batteries: a medium home's fans, lights and TV for longer cuts.
  • Solar hybrid inverter with a lithium battery: long backup and daytime charging from solar.

Your inverter adds to your bill

The inverter does not make electricity. Every time power returns, it recharges the battery from the grid, and some energy is lost along the way. An old battery and an inefficient inverter waste more. Matching backup to your area's power cuts

Size the battery for the cuts you actually get, not the worst case you can imagine. Look back over a few weeks: how long is a typical cut, and do they come back to back? If cuts are usually under two hours, a single battery that covers two hours of fans and lights is enough. If you often face several cuts in an evening, a battery that recharges quickly matters as much as its size, which favours lithium. Oversizing a tubular bank costs money upfront and wastes energy, because large banks take longer to charge and lose more along the way.

Making batteries last longer

  • Keep the battery in a cool, ventilated place, never in direct sun.
  • Top up tubular batteries with distilled water only, and check monthly.
  • Avoid draining the battery completely on a regular basis.
  • Do not mix old and new batteries in one bank.
  • Keep heavy loads such as irons, heaters and mixers off the inverter.

Switching to BLDC fans doubles your backup

Ceiling fans are usually the biggest load on an inverter. A BLDC fan uses roughly half the power of an ordinary fan. Swapping the fans on your inverter circuit can nearly double the backup time from the same battery, and it lowers your monthly bill too. Try it in the calculator: change the fan wattage by setting fewer fans and adding the BLDC wattage in "Anything else".

Frequently asked questions

How long will a 150 Ah inverter battery last?

One 12 V 150 Ah tubular battery runs about 260 watts, roughly two fans and some lights, for close to three hours. Use the inverter battery backup calculator with your own appliances.

Which is better for an inverter: tubular or lithium?

Tubular is cheaper upfront. Lithium gives more usable backup, lasts longer and needs no maintenance, so it often costs less over time.

Can an inverter run an AC?

Only a large inverter with a big battery bank. For most homes, it is better to keep the inverter for fans and lights.

Why does my battery not give the backup it used to?

Batteries lose capacity with age, heat and deep discharge. A tubular battery more than a few years old may need replacing.

How many batteries does a 1.5 kVA inverter need?

Many 1.5 kVA home inverters run on two 12 V batteries in series, but check your inverter's manual for its rated battery voltage before buying.

How often should I top up a tubular battery?

Check the water level every month, more often in summer, and top up with distilled water only, up to the marked level.

Can I connect a lithium battery to my old inverter?

Only if the inverter supports lithium charging settings. Many older home inverters do not, so check the manual or ask the brand.
