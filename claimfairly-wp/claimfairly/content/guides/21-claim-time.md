Two weeks after the crash, the car is still at the shop, the rental is running out and the adjuster is not returning calls. How long should a car insurance claim take, and is this normal? Sometimes it is. Sometimes it means the claim is stuck and needs a push.

This guide gives realistic timelines for each kind of claim, explains what slows them down, and shows how to move a claim that has stalled. If your question is about an injury settlement specifically, our guide on [how long a car accident settlement takes](/how-long-does-a-car-accident-settlement-take/) goes deeper on that part.

## Typical timelines at a glance

These are common ranges, not promises. State rules, the insurer, the season and the details of your claim all affect them.

| Type of claim | Common timeline |
|---|---|
| Claim acknowledged, adjuster assigned | A few days to about two weeks |
| Simple repair claim, clear fault | About one to four weeks to approve the estimate |
| Repair with hidden damage (supplements) | Add one to three weeks |
| Total loss, from declaration to payment | Often two to six weeks |
| Fault disputed between insurers | Weeks to a few months |
| Injury claim, minor injuries | Often a few months after treatment ends |
| Injury claim, serious injuries or a lawsuit | Many months to a few years |

## Rules on how fast insurers must act

Most states have rules, many based on NAIC model laws, that require insurers to handle claims within reasonable time frames. In general terms, these rules commonly require insurers to:

- Acknowledge a claim and respond to your communications promptly
- Start an investigation and finish it within a reasonable time
- Accept or deny the claim within a set period after receiving proof of loss
- Pay promptly once the claim is accepted and the amount is agreed
- Tell you in writing if they need more time, and why

The exact deadlines are different in each state, and they often allow extensions when the insurer explains the delay. Your state insurance department's website usually lists the rules that apply to you.

## What slows a claim down

### Fault is not settled

If the other driver's insurer has not accepted liability, it may not pay anything until its investigation ends. Police reports can take days or weeks to become available. Witness statements and recorded statements add more time.

### Missing documents

A claim waits on the slowest document: the police report, photos, repair estimates, medical records, a lender's payoff letter or title paperwork. Each request that bounces back and forth adds a week.

### Hidden damage

When the shop takes the car apart and finds more damage, it sends a supplement. The insurer may want to reinspect before approving it.

### Parts and shop backlogs

Repair time depends on parts availability and how busy the shop is. That part is outside the insurer's control, but it affects how long you need a rental.

### Total loss paperwork

If there is a loan, the insurer needs a payoff letter from the lender, and the lender needs to release the title. If you own the car outright, you need the signed title. Missing paperwork is a common cause of delay.

### Injury treatment

Injury claims are usually not settled until treatment ends, because the full cost is not known before then. This is the main reason injury claims take months.

### Adjuster workload

After storms and during busy seasons, adjusters handle large caseloads. Responses slow down across the board.

## How to speed up a stalled claim

1. **Send everything at once.** Put your documents in one email with the claim number in the subject line, and keep copies.
2. **Ask what is missing.** Ask the adjuster in writing: "What do you still need from me to make a decision?"
3. **Set a date.** Ask for a decision or a status update by a specific date, about one to two weeks away.
4. **Keep a log.** Note every call, who you spoke with and what they said.
5. **Escalate politely.** If the adjuster stops responding, ask for their supervisor.
6. **Use your own coverage.** If the other driver's insurer is slow, your collision coverage can pay for repairs, and your insurer can recover the money later.
7. **Contact your state insurance department** if the insurer misses deadlines or will not explain the delay.

A short, firm follow-up works well:

[cf_note type="info"]Claim number 7741029. I sent the repair estimate and photos on March 3 and have not received a decision. Please tell me what else you need, or confirm the decision by March 20. Thank you.[/cf_note]

## Rental car timing

Rental coverage usually has limits: a daily amount, a maximum number of days, or both. If the claim drags on, ask in writing whether the rental will be extended and who is paying. With a claim against the at-fault driver's insurer, delays caused by the insurer are a fair reason to ask it to cover a longer rental.

## Don't let speed cost you money

A fast claim is not always a good one. For injury claims especially, settling before you finish treatment can leave you paying future bills yourself, because a signed release closes the claim.
