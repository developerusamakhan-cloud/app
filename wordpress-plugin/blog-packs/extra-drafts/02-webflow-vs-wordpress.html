Quick answer: pick Webflow for a design-led marketing website with custom animation, fast hosting included and a small team editing content. Pick WordPress when you need plugins, ecommerce, memberships, a large blog or full ownership at the lowest long-term cost.

Clients often come to me after reading two passionate opinions: "Webflow is the future" and "WordPress runs the internet". Both are partly true. What matters is which one fits how your business works.

Design freedom

Webflow is a visual builder that writes clean code. Designers can create almost any layout, interaction or animation without a theme getting in the way. WordPress can match that with a custom theme, but it needs a developer for anything beyond what the theme or page builder offers. If your site is a brand showcase where motion and detail matter, Webflow is a joy to build in.

Editing content

Both are friendly for day-to-day edits once set up. Webflow's Editor lets your team update text, images and CMS collections without risk of breaking the design. WordPress uses the block editor or a builder like Elementor, which is flexible but makes it easier for someone to accidentally change styling. Good setup and a short training video solve most of that.

Plugins and features

This is where WordPress pulls ahead. Bookings, memberships, learning platforms, multilingual sites, directories, advanced forms and deep integrations are usually one well-chosen plugin away. Webflow has integrations and an app marketplace, but complex features often need third-party tools or custom code.

Cost

Webflow bundles hosting, SSL and security into site plans, and costs rise as you add CMS items, traffic or team seats. WordPress hosting can be cheaper, but you or a maintenance plan must handle updates and backups. For small marketing sites the yearly totals are often close. For big content sites WordPress is usually cheaper to run.

SEO

Both can rank very well. Webflow gives clean markup, fast hosting and easy control of titles, descriptions, redirects and Open Graph images. WordPress gives the same through plugins, plus more control for large sites with hundreds of posts. Speed matters on both, and I explain what to measure in Core Web Vitals explained.

Ecommerce

Webflow Ecommerce works for small, design-focused shops. For larger catalogues, subscriptions or complex shipping, WooCommerce or Shopify are stronger choices. Read ecommerce website design for what makes any store sell.

Ownership and lock-in

WordPress is open source: you own the files and can move hosts or developers whenever you want. Webflow lets you export code for static pages, but CMS content and interactions work best inside Webflow. That is fine for many businesses, just worth knowing before you commit.

My rule of thumb

  • Startup, agency or product launch site with a strong visual identity: Webflow.
  • Service business that wants to blog and grow through Google: WordPress.
  • Online store or members area: WordPress with WooCommerce, or Shopify.
  • Owner who wants zero maintenance and a simple site: Webflow or Squarespace.
