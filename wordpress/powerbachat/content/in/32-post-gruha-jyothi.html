Gruha Jyothi is the Karnataka government's scheme for free household electricity. Since it began in 2023, it has brought the electricity bill of millions of homes down to zero, or close to it. But "200 units free" is not quite how it works for most families, and many people are surprised when a bill arrives anyway. This guide explains Gruha Jyothi clearly: who qualifies, how your free limit is set, and what happens when you go over it.

The basic idea

Under Gruha Jyothi, eligible domestic households do not pay the energy charge for electricity used up to a monthly limit. The limit is personal to each household. It is based on the home's average monthly use over a reference period, plus a margin, and it can never go above 200 units a month.

So a family that used 90 units a month on average has a limit a little above 90, not 200. A family that used 150 a month has a limit a little above 150. The scheme is designed to cover a household's normal use, not to encourage more.

Who can benefit

  • Domestic electricity connections in Karnataka, supplied by BESCOM and the other state distribution companies.
  • Households that have registered for the scheme.
  • Typically one connection per household, in the name of the consumer.
  • Homes whose usage stays within the scheme's limits.

Registration was done through the state's online service and local centres. If you moved house or the connection changed hands, check that your registration is linked to your current connection.

How your entitlement is worked out

Your free limit, or entitlement, was set from your average monthly consumption over a reference year, with a margin added on top. When the scheme launched, the margin was 10 percent. Later adjustments have aimed to be fair to very low users. Your entitlement is usually printed on your bill, so that is the number to watch.

What you pay above the limit

If you use more than your entitlement in a month, you pay for the extra units at the regular tariff, along with charges such as the fixed charge where applicable. Going a little over usually means a small bill. Going far over means a bigger one. And if a household's use goes above 200 units in a month, the scheme's limits mean that month is treated differently, so check your bill carefully.

A worked example

A family in Mysuru has an entitlement of 118 units, shown on their bill. In February they use 105 units, so the energy charge is waived. In April, with a new cooler and summer heat, they use 150 units. They pay for the 32 units above their entitlement at the regular rate. In May they plan ahead: the cooler runs only in the afternoon, and they switch the geyser off completely. They finish at 122 units and pay for just four.

Tips to stay within your entitlement

  1. Know your number. Find your entitlement on your bill and write it near the meter.
  2. Read the meter weekly. Divide your entitlement by four for a weekly budget.
  3. Watch the geyser. In Bengaluru's cool mornings, a geyser left on is the most common way to exceed the limit.
  4. Use a solar water heater if you have a terrace. It removes the biggest winter load.
  5. Switch to BLDC fans and LED lights. They cut the steady daily load.
  6. Check the water pump. A faulty float switch can waste units every day.

When your family's needs grow

A new baby, an elderly relative moving in, a work-from-home job or a new AC can all push usage above an entitlement set from past years. In those cases, the scheme still covers your entitlement, and you pay only for the extra. If your use rises a lot and stays high, rooftop solar becomes worth considering, because it reduces the units you draw above your limit. Tenants and Gruha Jyothi

The scheme is tied to the electricity connection and the household using it. Tenants with their own meter have generally been able to benefit, but the process for linking a tenant to the connection depends on the scheme's rules, so check with your distribution company. If you move, update your details so the benefit follows you.

Checking your bill each month

Your bill shows the units used, your entitlement and the amount waived. Compare the units with your own meter reading, and check that the waiver appears when you are within your limit. If the reading is wrong, contact BESCOM or your distribution company through the helpline or app before the due date. Common misunderstandings

  • "Everyone gets 200 units free." No. Your free limit is based on your past average plus a margin, capped at 200.
  • "Going over by one unit cancels everything." Generally you pay for the units above your entitlement. The 200 unit cap is the line to treat with care.
  • "The bill will always be zero." Fixed charges and other lines can still appear, depending on your connection and current rules.

Gruha Jyothi and other states

Several states have schemes that reduce household bills, but they work differently. Delhi fully subsidises bills up to 200 units and partly up to 400, as explained on our Delhi electricity bill calculator page. Tamil Nadu gives the first 100 units free in every two-month cycle. Karnataka's approach is unusual because each household's free limit is personal. Comparing them helps explain why neighbours in different states can pay very different amounts for the same units.

Frequently asked questions

Is Gruha Jyothi 200 units free for everyone?

No. Each household's free limit is based on its own past average plus a margin, and the maximum is 200 units a month.

Where do I find my Gruha Jyothi entitlement?

It is usually printed on your monthly electricity bill.

What happens if I use more than my entitlement?

You pay for the units above your entitlement at the regular tariff, plus other applicable charges.

Does solar affect my Gruha Jyothi benefit?

Solar reduces the units you draw from the grid. Check with your distribution company how net metering and the scheme interact for your connection before you install. See the solar subsidy calculator for costs.

Does Gruha Jyothi cover commercial connections?

No. It is for domestic household connections.
