The 3kW solar system price in India is the number most families look for first, and for good reason. Three kilowatts is the size that captures the full central subsidy under PM Surya Ghar, it fits on most independent house roofs, and it produces enough to cover the bill of a typical home using around 300 units a month. This page sets out the price before and after subsidy, what the system includes, what it produces and how quickly it pays back.

When people compare the 3kW solar system price in India across vendors, the subsidy is the same for all of them, so differences come down to equipment and service. The subsidy for a 3 kW system is ₹78,000, the maximum the central scheme offers. That is why 3 kW gives the best value per rupee of subsidy for most homes: a 2 kW system gets less support, and anything above 3 kW gets no more.

What a 3 kW system includes

  • Panels: usually five or six DCR panels of 540 to 600 watts, adding up to about 3 kW.
  • Inverter: a 3 kW on-grid inverter, or a hybrid inverter if you want batteries.
  • Mounting structure: galvanised steel, often raised so the terrace stays usable.
  • Cabling and protection: DC and AC cables, a combiner box, surge protection and earthing.
  • Net meter: a bi-directional meter installed by your discom.
  • Roof space: roughly 15 to 20 square metres of shade-free roof.

How much does 3 kW produce?

In most of India, a well-installed 3 kW system produces about 350 units a month on average across the year:

Output is higher in the dry, sunny months and in states such as Rajasthan, Gujarat and Tamil Nadu, and lower during the monsoon and in hazy north Indian winters. Over a year, the swings even out, and net metering lets surplus summer units offset winter use in states that settle annually.

What can a 3 kW system run?

On a sunny day, 3 kW comfortably covers fans, lights, a fridge, a TV, a washing machine and one 1.5 ton inverter AC running in the afternoon. It does not keep the house running during a power cut unless you choose a hybrid inverter with batteries, because on-grid inverters switch off when the grid goes down.

Payback: a worked example

A family in Ahmedabad uses about 330 units a month. Their 3 kW system produces roughly that amount on average. With net metering, many of their monthly bills fall to the fixed charge and a small balance. Their share of the cost after the ₹78,000 subsidy is the figure in the 3 kW row above. Divide that by their yearly bill saving and the payback lands at a few years, after which the system keeps saving for two decades or more.

Your own payback depends on your state's tariff. In states with steep slabs, every unit solar replaces is expensive, so payback is faster. Use the PM Surya Ghar calculator to run it for your units.

DCR panels and the price difference

To qualify for the subsidy, panels must have domestic content (DCR). DCR panels cost more per watt than imported or non-DCR panels, which is why subsidised systems are priced higher per kW than unsubsidised ones in the open market. The subsidy more than covers the difference at 3 kW. Our solar panel price in India page shows today's DCR and non-DCR rates.

Hybrid 3 kW systems with batteries

If you face regular power cuts, a hybrid 3 kW system with a battery keeps essentials running. It adds a hybrid inverter and a battery bank, which raise the price considerably. Size the battery for your real outage hours rather than your whole night-time use. Our inverter battery backup calculator helps you size it.

Net metering and your monthly bill

With a 3 kW system and a net meter, your bill each month is based on the difference between units imported from the grid and units exported by your panels, under your state's rules. In sunny months you may export more than you import, building a credit. In monsoon or winter months you may import more. Most families still pay a fixed charge every month. The exact treatment of surplus units varies by state, so ask your discom how credits are settled before you decide how large to go. For most homes, 3 kW is a size that uses most of its own output, which makes the saving predictable.

Running costs over the years

  • Cleaning: rinse the panels every week or two in dusty months, early in the morning, with plain water.
  • Inverter: typically lasts around ten years. Budget for one replacement over the panels' life.
  • Annual check: tighten connections, check the earthing and look for damaged cables.

These costs are small compared with the savings. Across 25 years, the 3kW solar system price in India after subsidy is usually repaid many times over.

Getting a fair quote

  1. Ask for an itemised quote listing panel model and DCR certificate, inverter brand and model, structure, cables and protection.
  2. Confirm the vendor is registered on the national portal for your discom.
  3. Ask what is included in the price: net meter fees, transport, civil work, and after-sales service.
  4. Compare warranties: panel performance, inverter and workmanship.
  5. Get at least three quotes for the same specification.

Common mistakes with 3 kW systems

  • Shade: a water tank or parapet shading one panel can pull down the whole string.
  • Wrong tilt: panels laid flat collect dust and produce less than tilted ones.
  • No earthing: a safety requirement, not an optional extra.
  • Skipping cleaning: in dusty cities, uncleaned panels can lose a noticeable share of output.
