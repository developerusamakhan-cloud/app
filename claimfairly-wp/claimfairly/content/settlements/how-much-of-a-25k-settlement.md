A $25,000 settlement is common for soft tissue injuries like whiplash with a few months of treatment. It is also exactly the minimum bodily injury limit in many states, so it is often the most the at-fault driver's policy will pay.

The quick answer: with a one third attorney fee and nothing else taken out, you would keep about **$16,668**. Real settlements usually have case costs and medical liens too. The calculator below is already set to $25,000. Change the fee, costs and liens to match your case.

## A realistic $25,000 example

Here is a typical breakdown with a one third (33.33%) fee taken from the full settlement, $500 in case costs, and a $4,000 medical lien that the lawyer negotiated down by 30%.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Settlement | $25,000 |
| Attorney fee (one third (33.33%)) | minus $8,333 |
| Case costs | minus $500 |
| Medical lien after reduction | minus $2,800 |
| **You keep** | **$13,368** |

If the fee were calculated **after** costs instead, you would keep about **$13,534**, a difference of $167.

## When $25,000 is the policy limit

If the other driver carries only $25,000 per person, a $25,000 offer may be a "policy limits" offer, meaning the insurer is offering everything the policy allows. If your damages are higher, ask about **underinsured motorist (UIM)** coverage on your own policy, which can pay the difference. Many insurers require your consent before you accept a limits offer from the other driver, so check with your own insurer first.

## How to keep more of your $25,000 settlement

- Ask whether your fee is calculated before or after costs.
- Ask your lawyer to negotiate every medical lien.
- Request a written settlement statement before you sign the release.
- Check whether your own MedPay coverage already paid some bills.
