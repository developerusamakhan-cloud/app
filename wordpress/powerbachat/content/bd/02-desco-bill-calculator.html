This DESCO bill calculator is for households supplied by the Dhaka Electric Supply Company, which covers the northern part of Dhaka, including Mirpur, Pallabi, Uttara, Gulshan, Banani, Baridhara, Badda, Khilkhet and nearby areas, along with parts of Tongi. Enter your monthly units above and you will see each slab, the demand charge, VAT and an estimated total. If you have a prepaid meter, the total tells you roughly how much a month's use will take from your balance.

North Dhaka is a mix of dense apartment blocks, older houses and large homes in the diplomatic zone, and usage ranges widely. What every DESCO household shares is the BERC residential tariff, which makes a reliable estimate possible for anyone who knows their units.

How to use the DESCO bill calculator

  1. Find your units for the month. On a prepaid meter, use the meter's monthly usage screen or your recharge and usage history. On a postpaid bill, subtract the previous reading from the present reading.
  2. If you are mid-month, divide by the days so far and multiply by 30.
  3. Enter the units above. The slip shows each slab, the demand charge and VAT.
  4. Open the breakdown to see which units fall into the expensive slabs.

A two-bedroom flat in Mirpur with fans, lights, a fridge and a TV often uses 150 to 250 units. Add an AC on summer nights and 350 to 450 is common. Larger flats in Gulshan or Uttara with several ACs and a geyser can pass 800 units in the hottest months, with a large share of the bill coming from the top slabs.

DESCO prepaid meters

Most DESCO households now use prepaid meters. You recharge through mobile financial services, banks or vending points, and the meter deducts for energy, the demand charge and VAT as you go. The tariff is the same as postpaid. A few points help:

  • Check the deduction history on your meter or account. It shows exactly what was charged and when.
  • Recharge before the balance runs low. Most meters warn you, but supply can be cut when the balance runs out.
  • Keep your token or transaction number until the recharge appears on the meter.

A worked example from Uttara

A family in Uttara Sector 7 recharged ৳3,000 at the start of June, as they do every month. By the 18th, the balance is nearly gone. Their meter shows 340 units used so far, so the month is heading for about 560. The DESCO bill calculator shows what 560 units cost compared with 400. The gap is large, mostly from the units above 400. They switch the lounge AC off in the afternoons, use the bedroom AC only at night and finish the month at 430 units. Their next month's recharge plan is now based on a number, not a guess.

Apartment buildings and common loads

In apartment blocks, lifts, water pumps, stair lights and security lighting are usually on a separate common meter paid through the building's service charge. Your flat's meter covers only your own use. If your building's service charge has risen, the common meter's usage may have crossed into higher slabs too, especially in summer when pumps run more. Building committees can use the same calculator to plan the common meter.

The seasonal pattern in north Dhaka

DESCO households typically see their lowest usage from December to February and their highest from May to September. The monsoon does not bring much relief, because humidity keeps fans and ACs running. If you are on a prepaid meter, plan for larger recharges from April and keep a small buffer, so a heavy week does not cut your supply. If you are postpaid, use the twelve-month history on your bill to see exactly when your usage crosses 400 units, and focus your effort on those months.

Water heaters and winter bills

Although winters are short, electric geysers in north Dhaka flats can add a surprising number of units in December and January. A 2,000 watt geyser running two hours a day uses around 120 units a month. A timer switch, or simply switching it off after morning use, keeps winter bills low.

Lowering a DESCO bill

  • Stay under 400 units in summer if possible. It is the biggest single saving.
  • Use AC dry mode on humid evenings. Dhaka's humidity is often the real discomfort, and dry mode handles it with less power.
  • Set the AC to 25 or 26 degrees with a fan on.
  • Replace old fans with efficient DC fans.
  • Look at your IPS. An old battery wastes units every time it recharges. Our IPS calculator helps you size a replacement.

When a deduction or bill looks wrong

If your balance falls much faster than usual without a change in use, check the deduction history first. A meter fault, a wiring problem or a neighbour drawing from your line are possible causes. Photograph the meter display, note the dates and contact DESCO's office or helpline with your account or meter number. For postpaid bills, compare the reading with your meter before the due date.

If you are moving into a new flat, ask the owner or previous tenant for a few months of meter history before you set your first recharge. It gives you a realistic starting figure and helps you notice if the meter was left with a large outstanding demand charge or arrears.

Frequently asked questions

Does the DESCO bill calculator work for prepaid meters?

Yes. The tariff is the same. The total shows roughly how much a month's use deducts from your balance.

Are DESCO rates different from DPDC?

No. Both use the same BERC residential tariff. Our DPDC bill calculator gives the same result for the same units.

What is the demand charge on my DESCO meter?

A fixed monthly amount based on your sanctioned load. Our estimate assumes 2 kW.
