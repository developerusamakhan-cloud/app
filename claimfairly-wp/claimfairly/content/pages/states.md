The same crash can be worth very different amounts depending on where it happened. A driver who is 20% at fault gets 80% of their damages in California, but can get nothing at all in Virginia. That difference comes from state law, not from the insurer.

This page puts every state's basic rules side by side. Click a state name to open its full page, where the claim calculator is already set to that state's rules.

## The three rules that matter most

- **Fault system.** In at-fault states the other driver's insurer pays for your injuries. In no-fault states your own PIP coverage pays first, and suing for pain and suffering has extra hurdles.
- **Negligence rule.** This decides what happens when both drivers share blame. It ranges from pure comparative (you can always recover something) to contributory (any fault can bar you).
- **Filing deadline.** The statute of limitations for an injury lawsuit runs from 1 to 6 years depending on the state. Miss it and the claim is usually over.

## What the negligence rules mean in dollars

Say your claim is worth $50,000 and you are found 30% at fault:

| Rule | What you could recover |
|---|---|
| Pure comparative | $35,000 |
| Modified, 50% bar | $35,000 (but $0 at 50% fault or more) |
| Modified, 51% bar | $35,000 (but $0 above 50% fault) |
| Contributory | $0 |
