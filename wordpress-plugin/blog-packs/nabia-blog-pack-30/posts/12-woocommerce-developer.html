Quick answer: hire a WooCommerce developer who can show live stores they built, explains how they handle speed, security and updates, asks about your products, shipping and payments before quoting, and gives you a fixed scope in writing. Expect roughly $30 to $100 per hour, or $1,000 to $5,000 for a small store build.

A store is not a brochure site. When the checkout breaks, you lose money immediately. That is why choosing the right WooCommerce developer matters more than choosing a designer for a five page site.

What a WooCommerce developer does

Sets up products, variations and stock; configures payments, shipping zones and taxes; customises product pages and checkout; builds custom features with WooCommerce hooks instead of stacking plugins; connects the store to other systems; and keeps it fast and secure. For a full overview see WooCommerce development explained.

Eight questions to ask

  1. Can I see three live stores you built? Add something to the cart and go to checkout on your phone.
  2. What hosting do you recommend for this store, and why?
  3. How will you handle my product options? Listen for a plan, not "we will find a plugin".
  4. How do you keep cart and checkout fast?
  5. How are updates tested? A staging site is the right answer for stores.
  6. Who owns the code and accounts? You should.
  7. What is included, and what costs extra?
  8. What support do I get after launch?

Red flags

  • No live stores to show, only mockups
  • A quote without asking about products, shipping or payments
  • Many premium plugins for simple needs
  • No mention of backups or testing updates
  • Pressure to host the store on their account

Write a brief that gets accurate quotes

  • What you sell, how many products and how many variations
  • Where you ship and how you charge for shipping
  • Payment methods you want
  • Systems to connect: accounting, inventory, email marketing
  • Three stores you like and why
  • Budget range and launch date

Freelancer or agency?

For small and medium stores, an experienced freelancer is usually the best value: direct communication, lower overheads and the same person who builds the store supports it.
