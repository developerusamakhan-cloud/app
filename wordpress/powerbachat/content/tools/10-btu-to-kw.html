To convert BTU per hour to kilowatts, multiply by 0.000293, or divide by 3,412. A 12,000 BTU air conditioner has a cooling capacity of about 3.52 kW, and a 60,000 BTU furnace an output of about 17.6 kW. Type any figure into the calculator above, in either box.

BTU, BTU per hour and kW

A British thermal unit is the heat needed to warm one pound of water by one degree Fahrenheit. Air conditioner and heater ratings are almost always BTU per hour, a rate of heating or cooling, even when the label simply says BTU. That rate converts directly to kilowatts:

  • kW = BTU/h × 0.00029307
  • BTU/h = kW × 3,412.14

If you are converting an amount of energy instead, such as gas use on a bill, the same factor applies: 1 BTU is 0.000293 kWh, and one therm of 100,000 BTU is about 29.3 kWh.

Cooling kW is not electricity kW

The kW from this calculator is the heat an air conditioner moves, not the electricity it uses. Because air conditioners and heat pumps move heat rather than make it, they use far less electricity than their capacity. The energy efficiency ratio (EER) tells you how much: electricity in watts = BTU/h ÷ EER. A 12,000 BTU unit with an EER of 10 draws about 1,200 watts, while an efficient inverter model with an EER of 13 draws about 920 watts at full output.

Electric resistance heaters are different: they turn every watt into heat, so a 1.5 kW heater gives 1.5 kW, or about 5,100 BTU/h, of heat. See BTU to watts for small heaters and BTU to tons for air conditioner sizes.

Worked examples

  • 9,000 BTU split AC: 9,000 × 0.000293 = 2.64 kW of cooling.
  • 18,000 BTU (1.5 ton) AC: 18,000 × 0.000293 = 5.28 kW of cooling.
  • 24 kW combi boiler: 24 × 3,412 = about 81,900 BTU/h.

Frequently asked questions

How many kW is 12,000 BTU?

12,000 BTU per hour is 3.52 kW, which is also one ton of cooling.

How many BTU is 1 kW?

1 kW is about 3,412 BTU per hour.

How do I convert BTU to kWh?

Multiply the BTU by 0.000293. For example, 100,000 BTU of gas is about 29.3 kWh of energy.

Does a 12,000 BTU AC use 3.5 kW of electricity?

No. It moves 3.5 kW of heat but typically uses around 0.9 to 1.3 kW of electricity at full output, depending on its efficiency.
