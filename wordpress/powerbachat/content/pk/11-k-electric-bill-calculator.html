This K-Electric bill calculator is for Karachi households. K-Electric, usually called KE, is the only privately owned power company in Pakistan that both generates and distributes electricity, and it supplies Karachi along with a few nearby towns in Sindh and Balochistan such as Dhabeji, Gharo, Hub and Uthal. Enter the units from your meter above and you will see the slab-by-slab charge, the taxes and an estimated total for the month.

Karachi's climate shapes its bills. The city is rarely as hot as Jacobabad, but it is humid for much of the year, so fans run almost constantly and ACs are needed on many nights from April to October. The sea breeze helps in the evening, until it stops for a few days and the whole city reaches for the remote. A quick estimate mid-month helps you decide whether those extra AC hours are worth it.

Using the K-Electric bill calculator

  1. Find the previous and present readings on your last KE bill.
  2. Read your meter today and subtract the present reading. That is what you have used since.
  3. Scale up to a full month: divide by days passed and multiply by 30.
  4. Enter the result above. Tick "Protected consumer" only if each of your last six bills was at or below 200 units.

Many Karachi homes now have smart or AMR meters that send readings automatically. The display still shows a cumulative kWh reading you can note down, usually by pressing a button to cycle through screens. Look for the one labelled kWh or total import.

Slab rates in the calculator

A one-bedroom flat in Gulshan or Nazimabad with fans, lights, a fridge and a washing machine often sits around 150 to 250 units. Add an inverter AC every night in summer and 400 to 500 units is normal. Bungalows in DHA and Clifton with several ACs, a pump and a geyser can pass 1,500 units in the peak months, at which point most of the bill comes from the top slab.

Flats, shared meters and sub-meters

Karachi has more apartment buildings than any other city in Pakistan, and electricity arrangements in them vary. Some flats have their own KE meter. Others share a bulk meter with the building and pay the management through a sub-meter. If you are on a sub-meter, your charges depend on how the building divides the KE bill, which may not follow the household slabs at all. The calculator still helps you understand how much your own units should roughly cost, but your building's method is what decides your share. Common area lighting, lifts and the water pump are also usually split between flats.

Humidity, ACs and Karachi bills

In humid weather an AC does two jobs: it cools the air and removes moisture. That second job is why a room feels comfortable at 26 or 27 degrees once the humidity drops. Many Karachi families run their ACs at 18 to 20 degrees out of habit, which wastes a lot of units without much extra comfort. Try "dry" mode on humid evenings when the temperature is not extreme, and set cooling to 26 with a fan on. The difference over a summer is large.

A worked example from Gulshan-e-Iqbal

A family in Gulshan reads 290 units used by the 18th of the cycle. At that pace they will end near 480 units. The K-Electric bill calculator shows the estimate and, in the breakdown, that around 80 units fall in the 401 to 500 slab. Raising the AC from 20 to 26 degrees and switching it off an hour before waking cuts their daily use enough to finish at about 400 units. Their bill drops by the whole top slab and the tax on it. Nothing else changes in the house.

Geysers, pumps and the hidden Karachi loads

Two loads surprise Karachi families more than any other. The first is the water pump. Many buildings and houses pump water from an underground tank to a roof tank every day, and a pump with a faulty float switch can run for hours. The second is the electric geyser in homes where gas supply is weak. A geyser left on all day can add well over 100 units a month. A timer switch on the geyser and a working float valve on the tank are two of the cheapest fixes on any bill.

Load-shedding in Karachi

K-Electric runs load-shedding by feeder, based mainly on line losses and recovery in each area. Neighbourhoods with high losses can face several hours a day, while others have little or none. If your area is affected, your UPS recharges from the grid after each cut and adds to your bill. An efficient inverter and healthy batteries waste less. Charges the calculator does not include

  • Fuel cost adjustment for Karachi, which changes monthly. See our fuel price adjustment guide for how these work.
  • Arrears and late payment surcharge.
  • Income tax on larger bills for non-filers.
  • Time-of-use pricing on connections with higher sanctioned loads, which have separate peak and off-peak readings.

Checking your KE bill online

K-Electric bills can be viewed online and through the company's app using the account number printed on your bill. Once you have it, compare the units with your own meter and the energy charge with this calculator. If the units are wrong, raise a complaint through KE's official channels before the due date and keep the complaint number.

Frequently asked questions

Is the K-Electric bill calculator accurate for Karachi?

Yes for the base bill: the slab rates, GST and electricity duty. The monthly fuel cost adjustment, arrears or surcharges explain most differences from the printed bill.

Does K-Electric charge more per unit than WAPDA companies?

Base household rates follow the same uniform tariff. The monthly fuel adjustment for Karachi is set separately and can differ from the rest of the country.

Can I use this calculator for a shop or office?

No. Commercial connections use a different tariff. The K-Electric bill calculator is for household connections only.

Is solar worth it in Karachi?

For most homes using over 300 units a month, yes. Karachi gets good sunshine, and daytime AC use matches solar output well. Start with our solar calculator.
