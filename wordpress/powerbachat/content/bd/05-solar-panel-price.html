Small panels: 50 W to 200 W

Small panels are still widely used in Bangladesh, especially in rural areas and for charging batteries in small home systems. A 100 watt panel can keep a battery topped up for lights, a fan and phone charging. People often search for the price of 100 watt, 150 watt and 200 watt panels; multiply the per watt rate in the table by the wattage for a quick estimate. Expect small panels to cost noticeably more per watt than the large rooftop panels.

Large rooftop panels: 540 W to 580 W

For city rooftop systems, large panels of 540 to 580 watts are now the norm. Fewer panels mean less wiring and a smaller structure for the same system size. A 5 kW system needs about nine of them. If you are building a grid-connected or hybrid system, these are the panels to compare.

What makes prices differ

  • Brand and grade. A-grade panels from established manufacturers cost more than lower grades or unbranded panels, and come with real warranties.
  • Technology. Newer N-type panels lose less output in heat than older types, which matters in Bangladesh's hot, humid climate.
  • Size. Per watt, large panels are cheaper than small ones.
  • Warranty support. A panel with a warranty honoured by a local distributor is worth more than one without.
  • Exchange rate and import costs. Most panels are imported, so the taka's value and duties move prices.

How many panels do you need?

As a rough guide for Bangladesh, each kilowatt of panels produces around 100 to 110 units a month on average, after normal losses. A home using 300 units a month needs about 3 kW to cover most of its use, as long as much of it happens during the day. Charge controllers for small systems

A small panel charging a battery needs a charge controller between them. It stops the battery from overcharging and protects it from deep discharge. There are two common types. PWM controllers are cheap and fine for small panels matched to a 12 V battery. MPPT controllers cost more but get noticeably more energy from the panel, especially with larger panels or in changing light. If you are spending on a good panel and battery, an MPPT controller is usually worth it.

Charging an IPS with solar

Many families want to charge their existing IPS battery with solar so it recharges during the day without using grid units. This needs the right equipment: a solar charge controller or a solar-ready IPS, panels sized to the battery, and correct wiring. A single 12 V tubular battery is usually well served by one or two panels of 150 to 200 watts with a suitable controller. Ask the seller to check compatibility with your IPS model.

Checking a quote

  1. Ask for the exact panel brand, model and wattage in writing.
  2. Compare the per watt price with the table above.
  3. Ask about the product and performance warranty, and who honours it locally.
  4. For small systems, check that the charge controller matches the panel and battery.
  5. For rooftop systems, ask for an itemised quote for the full system, not panels alone.

Panels and batteries go together

Most Bangladeshi solar systems include batteries, because load-shedding is the main reason families install solar. Panels charge the battery during the day, and the battery runs the house during outages and in the evening. The battery is usually the part that needs replacing first, so choose it carefully. Installation and care

Mount panels facing south with a tilt of around 20 to 25 degrees for the best year-round output in Bangladesh. Keep them clear of shade from water tanks and neighbouring buildings. Clean them regularly, especially in the dry season when dust settles quickly, and after long periods without rain. Use proper cable sizes and fuses, and make sure the system is earthed. A well-installed panel keeps producing for twenty years or more.

Watch out for mislabelled panels

Occasionally, panels are sold with a wattage higher than they really produce, or with a brand label on a lower-grade product. A simple check helps: the specification sticker on the back should match the model on the brand's official datasheet, and the panel's size and weight should be consistent with its wattage. Buy from established dealers who give a proper invoice and warranty card.

When is the best time to buy?

Demand for solar and IPS equipment rises before summer and during periods of heavy load-shedding, and prices often firm up with it. If you can plan ahead, buying in the cooler months can mean better prices and more attention from installers. A small change in the solar panel price in Bangladesh makes only a modest difference to a full system's cost, though, so a well-planned installation matters more than waiting for the lowest price.

Frequently asked questions

What is the solar panel price in Bangladesh per watt?

See the price table at the top of this page. It lists current per watt and per panel prices with the date of the last update.

What is the price of a 100 watt solar panel?

Multiply the per watt price of a small panel in the table by 100. Small panels cost more per watt than large ones.

Which brand is best in Bangladesh?

Established brands with local warranty support are the safest choice. Compare warranties as well as price.

How long do solar panels last?

Good panels typically last twenty to twenty-five years or more with basic cleaning.

Do I need a charge controller?

Yes, for any panel charging a battery directly. It protects the battery and, in the MPPT type, increases the energy you collect.
