Quick answer: an AI website builder is fine if you need a simple site online today and have almost no budget. If your website needs to bring in customers, rank on Google and represent a real brand, a web designer who also uses AI tools will get you a much better result for a modest cost.

I use AI every day in my work, so I am not writing this to scare anyone away from it. I am writing it because I see a lot of AI generated sites come through my free audit, and they tend to fail in the same predictable ways.

What AI website builders do well

  • Speed. You describe your business and get a full site in minutes.
  • Starting points. Layout ideas, colour palettes and draft text to react to.
  • Low cost. Most are a monthly subscription similar to other website builders.
  • No technical setup. Hosting, SSL and basic mobile layouts are included.

For a pop-up event, a side project or a "coming soon" page, that is often all you need.

Where they fall short

Generic copy

AI writes text that sounds right but says little. "We are passionate about delivering exceptional results" could be on any website in the world. Visitors skim past it, and search engines increasingly favour content that shows real experience.

No strategy

A builder does not ask who your best customers are, what they worry about or why they choose you over a competitor. Those answers decide which pages you need and what goes on them.

Weak SEO structure

Most AI generated sites put everything on one or two pages, with vague headings and no plan for which searches each page should win. See my SEO guide for small businesses for what good structure looks like.

Everyone looks the same

When thousands of businesses generate sites from the same models and templates, they start to look alike. Standing out is part of the point of a website.

Lock-in

Many AI builders are closed platforms. Moving away later usually means starting again.

How I use AI as a designer

AI is my assistant, not my replacement. I use it to research industries and competitors, brainstorm layout options, draft first versions of text that I then rewrite, and check code faster. That saves hours of repetitive work, which lets me spend more time on the parts that make a difference: understanding your customers, design details, conversion and testing.

The result is a site built faster than a few years ago, but still planned and written by a human who knows your business.

A sensible middle path

  1. Use an AI builder or a simple template to get online quickly if you truly need to.
  2. Collect real questions from customers and notice what they ask before buying.
  3. When the site starts to matter, invest in a proper design with pages built around those questions.
  4. Add AI features where they help your customers, like an AI chatbot that answers common questions at night.

Questions to ask before using an AI builder

  • Can I export my content and move the site later?
  • Can I edit page titles, descriptions and headings for SEO?
  • Can I connect my own domain and Google tools?
  • What does it cost after the introductory price?

The bottom line

AI makes building websites faster for everyone, including me. It does not make the thinking optional. If you want a website that feels like your business and works as hard as you do, pair the speed of AI with human judgement.
