A $50,000 settlement usually reflects a moderate injury: several months of treatment, some missed work and real disruption to daily life. At this size, fees, costs and liens can take close to half of the headline number.

The quick answer: with a one third attorney fee and nothing else taken out, you would keep about **$33,335**. Real settlements usually have case costs and medical liens too, so most people keep less. ## A realistic $50,000 example

Here is a typical breakdown with a one third (33.33%) fee taken from the full settlement, $2,000 in case costs, and a $6,000 medical lien that the lawyer negotiated down by 25%.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Settlement | $50,000 |
| Attorney fee (one third (33.33%)) | minus $16,665 |
| Case costs | minus $2,000 |
| Medical lien after reduction | minus $4,500 |
| **You keep** | **$26,835** |

If the fee were calculated **after** costs instead, you would keep about **$27,502**, a difference of $667. That is why it is worth reading your fee agreement closely. Our guide on [attorney fees and medical liens](/attorney-fees-and-medical-liens/) explains each deduction in detail.

## How the fee percentage changes a $50,000 settlement

Before costs and liens:

| Fee | Attorney fee | Left for you |
|---|---|---|
| 30% | $15,000 | $35,000 |
| 33.33% | $16,665 | $33,335 |
| 35% | $17,500 | $32,500 |
| 40% | $20,000 | $30,000 |

## What kind of case settles for about $50,000?

Claims that settle near $50,000 often have medical bills in the $10,000 to $20,000 range, several months of therapy, some imaging such as an MRI, and a few weeks of missed work. Injuries like a disc bulge, a significant sprain or a simple fracture that healed without surgery often fall here, depending on the state and the evidence. To see where your own claim may land, enter your bills, lost wages and injury severity in the [car accident settlement calculator](/car-accident-settlement-calculator/), which also applies your state's fault rule.

## Where the money goes on a $50,000 settlement

On most $50,000 settlements the attorney fee is the biggest deduction, followed by medical liens. Liens are also the easiest to shrink: hospitals and health plans often accept a reduced amount if your lawyer asks, especially when the bills are large compared with the settlement. A 25% reduction on a $6,000 lien puts $1,500 back in your pocket.

## How long a $50,000 settlement usually takes

Claims of this size often take six to twelve months from the crash, mostly because treatment has to finish before the demand goes out. After you sign the release, payment usually arrives within two to six weeks. Liens can add time. Read [how long a car accident settlement takes](/how-long-does-a-car-accident-settlement-take/) for the full timeline.

## Is $50,000 a fair number for your claim?

Run through these before you accept:

- Does it cover all medical bills, including future care your doctor recommended?
- Does it cover lost wages and out-of-pocket costs?
- Is there a reasonable amount left for pain and suffering? Check with the [pain and suffering calculator](/pain-and-suffering-calculator/).
- Did the insurer assign you any fault, and do you agree with it?
- Is it close to the other driver's policy limit?
- After fees and liens, is the amount you keep enough to cover what the crash cost you?

If several answers are "no," read [should you accept the first settlement offer](/should-you-accept-the-first-settlement-offer/) before you respond.

## How to keep more of your $50,000 settlement

- Ask whether your fee is calculated before or after costs.
- Ask your lawyer to negotiate every medical lien.
- Ask for an itemized list of costs with receipts.
- Request a written settlement statement before you sign the release.
- Check whether your own MedPay coverage already paid some bills without a lien.

## Three versions of the same $50,000 settlement

| Scenario | Fee | Costs | Lien paid | You keep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No lawyer, lien paid in full | $0 | $0 | $6,000 | $44,000 |
| Lawyer, settled before a lawsuit (33.33%) | $16,665 | $2,000 | $4,500 | $26,835 |
| Lawyer, settled after a lawsuit (40%) | $20,000 | $4,000 | $4,500 | $21,500 |

This comparison assumes the same $50,000 gross settlement in all three cases, which is not always realistic. Represented claimants often settle for more, and lawyers usually negotiate liens down. The point is to see how each factor moves your share, not to suggest which path is right for you.

## When the money arrives

A $50,000 settlement often arrives after months of reduced income. Many people use part of it to catch up on bills first, then set aside a cushion before making bigger decisions.

Before you sign a $50,000 release, four checks matter most:

1. **Ask your own insurer for a PIP or MedPay payment ledger.** If it paid bills, find out whether it expects to be repaid from the settlement. The rules differ by state.
2. **Ask each health plan for its lien in writing**, based on what it actually paid, not the hospital's full billed amount.
3. **Compare the fee in your agreement with the market.** On a pre-suit case, one point of percentage is worth $500 here.
4. **Read who the release covers.** It should release the other driver and their insurer, not your own underinsured motorist claim.

## No-fault states and a $50,000 settlement

Where you live changes how a $50,000 claim works. In a **no-fault** state, your own personal injury protection (PIP) pays your medical bills and some lost wages first, whoever caused the crash. You can usually claim pain and suffering from the other driver only if your injury passes a threshold set by state law, such as a serious injury definition or a minimum amount of medical bills.

According to our state rules data, the no-fault states are **Florida, Hawaii, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, New York, North Dakota and Utah**, and **Kentucky, New Jersey and Pennsylvania** let drivers choose. See [at-fault vs no-fault states](/at-fault-vs-no-fault-states/) for how each system works.

**Why it matters at $50,000:** a claim this size usually means the injury met the threshold, so part of the money is for pain and suffering. But PIP may already have paid part of your bills, and in some states the PIP insurer can be repaid from the settlement. Ask before you sign.

### What a 30% fee instead of one third changes

Using the same example as above:

| Fee | Attorney fee | Costs and lien | You keep |
|---|---|---|---|
| 33.33% | $16,665 | $6,500 | $26,835 |
| 30% | $15,000 | $6,500 | $28,500 |

That is **$1,665 more** in your pocket, which is why it is worth asking about the percentage before you sign a fee agreement.
