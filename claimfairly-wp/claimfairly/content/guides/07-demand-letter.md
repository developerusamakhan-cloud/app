A demand letter is your claim in writing. It tells the insurance company what happened, what it cost you and what you want to settle. A good one is clear, organized and hard to argue with. It does not need legal jargon.

Here is how to write one, section by section. If you would rather skip the formatting, our free [demand letter generator](/demand-letter-generator/) builds it from a short form and gives you a PDF.

## When to send it

- **Injury claims:** after you finish treatment, or once your doctor can estimate future care. Send it too early and you may leave out costs you cannot add later.
- **Property damage:** once you have the repair invoice or the total loss valuation.
- **Diminished value:** after the repair is complete.

Keep an eye on your state's filing deadline. A demand letter does not stop the clock.

## The 7 parts of a strong demand letter

### 1. Header and claim details

Your name and address, the date, the insurer's name and address, the adjuster's name, the claim number, their insured's name and the date of loss. Adjusters juggle many files, so make yours easy to match.

### 2. A short, factual account of the crash

Two to five sentences. Where you were, what their driver did, and what the police report says. For example: "On March 3, I was stopped at a red light on Main Street when your insured struck the rear of my car. The police report lists your insured as at fault for following too closely."

### 3. Your injuries and treatment

In order: the ER or urgent care visit, the diagnosis, follow ups, therapy, any procedures. Use the words your doctors used.

### 4. How the injury affected your life

This supports pain and suffering. Be specific: time off work, help you needed at home, activities you had to stop, trouble sleeping.

### 5. An itemized list of damages

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| City Hospital emergency room | $3,200 |
| Imaging | $1,150 |
| Physical therapy (24 visits) | $2,880 |
| Lost wages (3 weeks) | $2,700 |

Include small items: prescriptions, parking at appointments, mileage.

### 6. Your demand

A clear number. Most people open at the high end of a realistic range, leaving room to negotiate. Our [settlement calculator](/car-accident-settlement-calculator/) can help you find that range.

### 7. A response deadline

"Please respond in writing within 30 days" is standard and reasonable.

## What to attach

Copies, never originals, of:

- Medical records and bills
- Proof of lost wages
- Photos of the vehicles, the scene and your injuries
- The police report
- Repair estimates or invoices

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Admitting fault** or apologizing.
- **Exaggerating.** One inflated claim can make the adjuster doubt everything else.
- **Leaving out future treatment** your doctor has recommended.
- **Accepting a phone offer** before you have seen it in writing.
- **Asking for more than the policy limit** without a plan for the difference.

## After you send it

Send by certified mail or through the insurer's claim portal, and keep a copy. Expect a counteroffer. Respond in writing, point to your evidence and adjust your number only when you have a reason to.

For serious injuries or disputed fault, having an attorney review the letter before you send it is worth considering. Many offer free consultations.
