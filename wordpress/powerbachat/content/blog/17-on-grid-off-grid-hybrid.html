When you start looking at rooftop solar in India, installers quickly ask whether you want on-grid, off-grid or hybrid solar. The answer changes the price, whether you get the subsidy, whether the lights stay on during a power cut, and how much you save. This guide compares the three in plain terms so you can choose with confidence.

On-grid solar

An on-grid system connects your panels to the grid through an on-grid inverter. During the day, the panels power your home and any surplus flows to the grid through a net meter. At night, you draw from the grid as usual.

  • Cost: the lowest of the three, because there are no batteries.
  • Subsidy: the PM Surya Ghar residential subsidy is designed for grid-connected systems with net metering.
  • Power cuts: the inverter shuts down when the grid fails, for the safety of line workers. No backup.
  • Savings: the highest per rupee spent, through net metering.

Off-grid solar

An off-grid system has no connection to the grid. Panels charge a battery bank, and the house runs from the batteries.

  • Cost: high, because it needs a large battery bank to last through nights and cloudy days.
  • Subsidy: generally not covered by the residential grid-connected scheme.
  • Power cuts: irrelevant, because you are not on the grid.
  • Best for: farmhouses, remote homes and places without a reliable grid connection.

Hybrid solar

A hybrid solar system combines both. It is connected to the grid and has batteries. During the day, panels power the home and charge the batteries. During a power cut, the hybrid inverter switches to battery power for selected loads. Surplus power can still go to the grid where net metering allows.

  • Cost: in between, depending on battery size.
  • Power cuts: backup for essential loads, without a separate home inverter.
  • Savings: similar to on-grid during normal days, plus avoided generator or inverter costs.
  • Best for: homes with frequent outages that also want lower bills.

Which one should you choose?

  • Reliable grid, few power cuts: on-grid. Lowest cost, best savings, full subsidy.
  • Frequent cuts, want backup: hybrid solar with a modest battery for essentials.
  • No grid or very unreliable grid: off-grid, sized carefully.
  • Already own a home inverter and battery: on-grid solar plus your existing inverter can be a cheaper middle path, as long as they are wired correctly.

A worked example

A family in Patna faces two or three hours of cuts on many summer evenings. A 3 kW on-grid system would cut their bill but leave them in the dark during cuts. They choose hybrid solar with a 3 kW hybrid inverter and a modest lithium battery covering fans, lights and the router for three hours. They pay more than for on-grid, but their old inverter and battery are retired, and they no longer waste units charging them from the grid. Their neighbour in a better-supplied area of the city chooses on-grid, takes the full subsidy and pays less upfront. Both made sensible choices for their situation.

Subsidy and hybrid systems

The central subsidy is linked to grid-connected rooftop systems registered on the national portal, with DCR panels and net metering. Some hybrid systems are grid-connected and may fit within the rules, while batteries themselves are not what the subsidy pays for. Before you buy a hybrid system expecting the subsidy, confirm with your vendor and discom how your exact setup will be treated. Our PM Surya Ghar calculator covers the subsidy side.

Net metering with each type

Net metering lets you export surplus solar units to the grid and offset them against your imports. On-grid systems are built around it. Hybrid solar systems can also export where the discom allows it, while charging batteries first. Off-grid systems cannot export at all. If your state values exported units well, on-grid and hybrid systems recover their cost faster. Your discom's net metering rules, and how often credits are settled, are worth checking before you decide. The solar power price calculator estimates the saving side.

Inverter choice matters most

The inverter decides what kind of system you have. An on-grid inverter cannot use batteries. A hybrid inverter can work with or without them, which makes it a flexible choice if you are unsure: start without batteries and add them later. An off-grid inverter needs batteries to work. Ask your installer which type they are quoting, its warranty and whether it supports lithium batteries, because that affects the cost of future upgrades.

Batteries: the biggest decision in hybrid and off-grid

Batteries are the most expensive part to replace. Lithium batteries cost more upfront but last much longer and deliver more usable energy than tubular lead-acid. For daily cycling in a solar system, lithium usually costs less over its life. Size the battery for real needs, such as fans, lights and the fridge for the length of a typical cut, rather than for running the whole house all night.

Questions to ask before you buy

  • How many hours a week does the power go off in my area, and at what times?
  • Which loads must run during a cut, and what do they add up to in watts?
  • Is the quoted inverter on-grid, hybrid or off-grid, and can it take batteries later?
  • How will the system be treated for the subsidy and net metering?

Answering these four questions honestly usually makes the choice obvious.

Frequently asked questions

Does on-grid solar work during a power cut?

No. On-grid inverters shut down when the grid fails, for safety.

Is hybrid solar worth the extra cost?

If you face frequent power cuts, often yes. It replaces a separate inverter and keeps essentials running.

Can I get the subsidy for off-grid solar?

The residential subsidy is designed for grid-connected systems. Off-grid systems are generally not covered.

How big should the battery be in hybrid solar?

Size it for essential loads during a typical cut. Use the inverter battery backup calculator with your fans, lights and fridge.

Which type gives the fastest payback?

On-grid, because it has no batteries and qualifies for the subsidy. See the 3kW solar system price page for an example.

Can I convert on-grid to hybrid later?

Often yes, by changing the inverter or adding a battery-compatible one. Plan for it when choosing your first inverter.
