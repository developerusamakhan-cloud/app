Quick answer: a WordPress designer focuses on how the site looks, a site builder assembles sites with themes and page builders, a WordPress developer writes custom code, and a WordPress expert covers all of this plus speed, security, hosting and strategy. Simple brochure sites rarely need an expert; stores, slow sites, broken sites and custom features usually do.

Search for help with WordPress and you will see every title imaginable. The labels are not regulated, so the same person might call themselves a designer on one platform and an expert on another. What matters is what they can actually do for your project.

The four types, explained

WordPress designer

Strong on layout, typography, colour and brand. Usually designs in Figma and may or may not build the site.

Site builder

Uses ready-made themes and page builders like Elementor to assemble sites quickly. Great value for straightforward projects, less suited to custom features or performance problems.

WordPress developer

Writes PHP, JavaScript and CSS: custom themes, custom plugins, integrations with other systems, WooCommerce customisation. I explain the role in more detail in what does a WordPress developer do.

WordPress expert

Combines design and development with the less visible skills: hosting, caching, security hardening, database performance, SEO structure and knowing which plugins to trust. An expert can tell you what not to build.

When you need an expert

  • Your site is slow and nobody can say why
  • It was hacked, or keeps breaking after updates
  • You run a store or membership site where downtime costs money
  • You need custom features or integrations
  • You are moving hosts or platforms and cannot afford to lose rankings

When a site builder is enough

A simple, five page site with a contact form and a gallery does not need deep expertise. Choose someone with good taste, a clear process and fair prices.

How to spot real WordPress expertise

  • Live sites, not just screenshots. Open them on your phone and check how fast they load.
  • Clear answers about speed and security. Ask what they do for caching, backups and updates.
  • Fewer plugins, not more. Experts solve problems with the minimum needed.
  • They ask about your business. Good advice depends on goals, not just features.
  • They explain trade-offs. "It depends, and here is why" is a good sign.

What it costs

Experienced WordPress freelancers often charge between $25 and $100 per hour, depending on experience and location, and many prefer fixed prices for defined projects.
