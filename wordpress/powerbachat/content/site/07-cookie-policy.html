This cookie policy explains the cookies and similar browser storage that PowerBachat uses, and how you choose which ones are on. We keep the list short on purpose. Essential cookies keep the site working; anything else stays off until you say yes in the cookie popup.

Last updated: [powerbachat_updated]

What cookies are

A cookie is a small text file that a website stores in your browser. It lets the site remember something between pages or visits, such as a setting you chose. Browsers also offer similar storage, called local storage, which works in much the same way.

Your choices

On your first visit, a small popup asks which cookies you are happy with. There are three groups:

  • Essential: always on, because the site needs them to work.
  • Analytics: off unless you turn them on. They would let us count visits anonymously and see which pages are useful.
  • Marketing: off unless you turn them on. They would let us measure adverts and show more relevant offers.

You can change your mind at any time with the "Cookie settings" link at the bottom of every page. If we use Google services such as Analytics, they receive your choice through Google's consent mode and do not set analytics or advertising cookies unless you allowed them.

Essential cookies we use

  • pb_consent remembers your cookie choices so we do not ask on every page. It lasts 6 months.
  • pb_cc stores a two-letter country code (pk, in or bd) so the site shows the right country without looking it up again. It lasts 30 days. It does not contain your IP address or any other information about you.
  • pb_alert is set for a short time after you sign up for tariff alerts, so the form can show a thank you message instead of asking again. It lasts 30 days and contains no personal information.
  • pb_lead makes sure the "website like this" message from the studio that built PowerBachat appears at most once a day. It lasts 24 hours. The same limit and the time spent on the site during your visit are also kept in your browser's local storage (pb_lead_last and pb_lead_time).

If you log in to the site as an administrator or editor, WordPress sets its own login cookies. Ordinary visitors never receive these.

Outside services

The site loads fonts from Google Fonts. This does not set cookies, but your browser does send your IP address to Google when it downloads the fonts. Our privacy policy explains this and our country lookup in more detail.

When we add an analytics or advertising service, we will list its cookies here. They are only set if you turn on Analytics or Marketing in the cookie popup.

Managing cookies

You can delete or block cookies in your browser's cookie settings. Every major browser lets you clear cookies for a single site or for all sites, and to block them in future. If you block the pb_cc cookie, the site still works; it simply needs to work out your country again on each visit, which can make pages slightly slower to settle.

Changes

If we change the cookies we use, we will update this page and the date at the top. For anything else about how we handle information, see our privacy policy and terms of use.
